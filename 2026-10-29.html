A clinical multimodal vision language foundation model with fine-grain explainability

Medical imaging MLscore 10 Nature Medicine · IF 50.0journal list2026-10-29Bai, Yang, Cheng, Haoran, Zhou, Yang et al.

Current medical foundation models are often modality-specific and lack transparent reasoning, which limits clinical adoption. EVLF-FM was developed and tested on over 1.3 million samples from 18 global datasets spanning 10 imaging modalities and six specialties (dermatology, hepatology, ophthalmology, pathology, pulmonology, radiology), with external validation on 13,443 independent samples from 17 additional datasets across 10 modalities. The model is a multimodal vision language foundation model supporting disease diagnosis, visual question answering, and pixel-level visual grounding with reasoning, trained via a hybrid strategy combining supervised and visual reinforcement fine-tuning. In internal validation it reached the highest average diagnostic accuracy of 0.895, and in medical visual grounding it achieved an average Acc@0.5 of 0.842 across nine modalities, with external testing confirming consistent zero-shot and few-shot performance and competitive F1 scores despite a smaller model size; human evaluation of reasoning outputs using the S.C.O.R.E. framework gave a mean of 4.25/5. The work is notable for combining broad multi-modality diagnostic capability with fine-grained, visually grounded explanations, though the authors note the need for validation across diverse healthcare settings and prospective clinical studies before routine deployment.

DOISummary
Deep dive (from full text via springer-oa-api)

EVLF-FM addresses the gap between generalist medical vision-language models (broad but weak at pixel-level tasks and opaque) and specialist models (precise but narrow), aiming for a single model that performs diagnosis, medical VQA and visual grounding while producing auditable reasoning. The authors assemble ~1.3 million curated images across 10 modalities and six specialties, and train an 8B-parameter VLM in two stages: supervised fine-tuning with LoRA using dual encoders (a disease-level encoder supplying a global pathology embedding and a pixel-level encoder preserving dense spatial features, each projected into the LLM space), followed by visual reinforcement fine-tuning with GRPO using verifiable task rewards (exact-match diagnosis, IoU-based localization, token-F1 for open VQA) plus a sentence-level clinical reasoning alignment reward balancing completeness and faithfulness. Chain-of-thought rationales were generated with MedGemma/GPT-5-mini and filtered via vision-amplified semantic entropy and GPT scoring (60,455 retained from 110,290). Headline results: mean internal diagnostic accuracy 0.895, best on MedMNIST (0.851/0.808 F1), closed VQA ~90% and open recall >80% on VQA-RAD/SLAKE, grounding Acc@0.5 0.842 versus 0.755 for InternVL 8B, zero-shot superiority on 8/12 external datasets, and clinician S.C.O.R.E. mean 4.25±0.94. Genuinely new elements are the dual-encoder exemplar-prompting design, the reasoning-alignment reward, and independent clinician grading of rationale faithfulness; the core claim of generalist-plus-pixel-precision is credible but incremental over concurrent medical VLMs.

  • Only 2D static images; no volumetric or time-resolved processing (CT/OCT handled as slices)

Development and validation of a model using thoracic vertebrae data from low-dose CT scans for detecting AI-QCT-defined lumbar osteoporosis.

Bone and skeletalscore 9 Bone · IF 3.5journal list2026-10-29Y Zhang, C Rong, Y Zhang et al.

Opportunistic lumbar osteoporosis screening on low-dose CT is supported by evidence but lacks a clinically deployable strategy, so the authors built and validated a model using thoracic bone mineral density (BMD) from LDCT. The multi-centre cross-sectional study used 18,298 adults who underwent LDCT covering T1 to L2 at 134 Chinese health check-up centres, with the outcome defined as AI-QCT lumbar osteoporosis (mean L1-L2 BMD < 80 mg/cm3 by a phantom-less AI-QCT system), present in 1730 (9.5%) participants. Five machine learning models were trained on T1-T12 BMD (n = 11,065) and evaluated internally (n = 4743) and externally (n = 2490), with logistic regression selected as the final model; T12 BMD showed the strongest association (OR 0.781 per 1 mg/cm3, 95% CI 0.769-0.793). In external validation the model reached an AUC of 0.993 (95% CI 0.991-0.996), AUPRC of 0.922 (95% CI 0.887-0.951), F1 of 0.813, sensitivity of 0.975 and specificity of 0.963. The work indicates that AI-QCT-derived thoracic BMD could support large-scale opportunistic screening if integrated into lung cancer screening, though prospective validation against independent standards is still required.

Interpretable deep learning maps spatial morphological phenotypes to HCC and LUAD survival.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Cell Reports Medicine · IF 11.7journal list2026-10-29Z Liu, Q Wu, H Lin et al.

Deep learning models for pathology-based prognosis are limited by their black-box nature, which obscures the biological basis of their predictions. The study uses whole-slide images from a multi-center cohort of 1,067 patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD). The proposed MorphX framework fuses macro-scale tissue texture with micro-scale nuclear metrics to attribute survival risk to interpretable morphological phenotypes rather than latent features. MorphX achieves concordance indices of 0.775-0.799 in discovery cohorts, and eight morphology-based biomarkers driven by texture heterogeneity in necrotic regions, lymphocyte density, and nuclear shape show reproducible survival associations across independent cohorts. The work matters because it converts opaque computational predictions into validated biological insights that offer finer resolution of tumor aggressiveness than traditional histological grading.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via elsevier)

MorphX addresses a familiar clinical question in computational pathology: can H&E whole-slide images (WSIs) predict postoperative overall survival (OS) in a way that pathologists can actually inspect and trust? The authors' premise is that latent deep features from foundation models are opaque, whereas hand-crafted morphological descriptors are interpretable but weak. MorphX therefore builds spatially aligned WSI-level feature maps (168 channels for HCC, 165 for LUAD) combining published tissue-type segmentation (PaSegNet for HCC; a multi-layer pseudo-supervision model for LUAD), 40 GLCM/GLRLM texture descriptors, and 120 HoVer-Net-derived nuclear/cell descriptors, then trains a residual CNN (MFM) with Cox partial-likelihood loss. Integrated gradients on these maps yields tile- and feature-level attributions; the top 16 high-attribution tiles are fused with global features in a multi-scale model (MSFM). Across 1,067 patients (TCGA-LIHC 330, KMMUFH 151, TCGA-LUAD 285, GDPH 301), MSFM reached external C-indices of 0.676 (HCC) and 0.612 (LUAD), competitive with GigaPath (0.602/0.600) and BEPH (0.589/0.637), with final-model external C-indices of 0.683 and 0.670 and adjusted HRs of 2.35 and 2.46. The genuinely new element is not predictive accuracy but the construction of an intrinsically interpretable input space in which attributions map directly onto named histological descriptors, enabling model-guided discovery of tissue-specific texture biomarkers (e.g., tumor/necrotic GLCM features, lymphocyte density).

Datasets

  • TCGA-LIHC: H&E WSI, 330 patients (112 OS events), public (GDC), discovery/training with 10-fold CV
  • KMMUFH: H&E WSI, 151 patients (56 events), private (controlled access), independent external validation
  • TCGA-LUAD: H&E WSI, 285 patients (98 events), public (GDC), discovery/training with 10-fold CV
  • GDPH: H&E WSI, 301 patients (62 events), private (controlled access), independent external validation
  • TCGA bulk RNA-seq (STAR counts) for matched molecular context analyses, public

Method

  • MorphX = MFM (4-residual-block CNN on BN-normalized multi-channel morphological feature maps) + MSFM (fuses global embedding with local features from top-16 integrated-gradient-selected tiles)
  • Input maps: 40 GLCM/GLRLM texture + 120 HoVer-Net nuclear/cell descriptors + 7 HCC / 4 LUAD tissue classes, at 10x, 75-px tiles with 37-px step
  • Cox proportional hazards partial-likelihood loss; sigmoid-bounded risk score (unbounded variant in ablation)
  • 10-fold CV on discovery cohorts with fixed seed and shared fold assignments across models; checkpoint selected by internal-test C-index
  • Baselines: GigaPath, BEPH, MIL models, prognosis-specific WSI model (PaSegNet-based)

Validation

  • Internal 10-fold CV on TCGA discovery cohorts; single independent external cohort per cancer (KMMUFH, GDPH) with no external tuning
  • Harrell C-index (mean +/- SD across folds; 95% CI for final model), Kaplan-Meier log-rank, univariable/multivariable Cox with MICE imputation
  • Retrospective only; no prospective or blinded reader study

Results

  • External C-index: MSFM 0.676 (HCC) vs GigaPath 0.602 and BEPH 0.589; MSFM 0.612 (LUAD) vs GigaPath 0.600 and BEPH 0.637
  • Final MorphX risk score external C-index 0.683 (95% CI 0.610-0.753, KMMUFH) and 0.670 (0.600-0.732, GDPH); KM HRs 2.70 and 3.49, log-rank p<0.0005
  • Clinical adjustment: HR 2.35 (KMMUFH) and 2.46 (GDPH); clinical-only vs combined C-index 0.520/0.691 and 0.771/0.791
  • Ablation: full morphological maps raise external C-index 0.4955->0.6724 (HCC) and 0.5439->0.5949 (LUAD); adding top-16 tiles 0.7592->0.7988 (TCGA-LIHC) and 0.7586->0.7749 (TCGA-LUAD)
  • Candidate biomarkers: TUM MCC HR 2.42/2.15 (TCGA-LIHC/KMMUFH); NEC DA HR 2.45/2.27 (TCGA-LUAD/GDPH); LYM DEN HR 0.15/0.27

Limitations

  • Retrospective, one external cohort per cancer; no prospective clinical utility demonstrated; treatment regimens not consistently available for adjustment
  • Tissue-specific candidates and two-feature scores are exploratory because external outcomes influenced candidate retention; HCC pair not uniquely determined (near-identical AIC alternative)
  • Bulk RNA-seq associations are cohort-level, not spatial or causal; organ-specific tissue taxonomies and tile-level nuclear aggregation limit cross-cancer interpretation
  • Omitted: no comparison against end-to-end foundation-model fine-tuning on identical folds beyond reported baselines; no pathologist reader study quantifying interpretability benefit; modest LUAD external performance (~0.61-0.67) and wide CIs

Three-dimensional DXA for compartment-specific assessment of proximal femoral bone in type 2 diabetes and diabetic kidney disease.

Bone and skeletalscore 9 Osteoporosis International · IF≈3.92026-10-29CJ Choi, YS Chung, YJ Choi

Areal bone mineral density (aBMD) is a poor surrogate for fracture risk in diabetic kidney disease (DKD), so the authors asked whether 3D-DXA reconstructed from a routine hip DXA can provide compartment-specific bone phenotyping in type 2 diabetes (T2DM) and DKD. They analysed 3D-SHAPER reconstructions from 1520 adults (549 with T2DM, 971 non-diabetic), excluding bone-affecting comorbidities and antiresorptive or glucocorticoid use, using sequential multivariable models and propensity-score matching with per-SD standardization and Benjamini-Hochberg control; the abstract does not state whether the cohort was single- or multi-centre. T2DM showed higher integral, trabecular and cortical volumetric BMD than non-diabetic controls (β = +9.91, +6.33 and +17.64 mg/cm³; all FDR q < 0.02), but in adjusted analyses DKD with reduced eGFR had lower integral, trabecular and cortical vBMD than T2DM without DKD (all P < 0.03). The work indicates that 3D-DXA captures compartment-specific differences across diabetes-kidney status groups that aBMD does not, supporting its further evaluation as an imaging adjunct in DKD, with prospective fracture-outcome studies needed for clinical translation.
