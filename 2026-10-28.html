4DLoG: Generative modeling of neurodegenerative brain anatomy with 4D longitudinal diffusion model.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Medical Image Analysis · IF 11.8journal list2026-10-24N Jayakumar, S Deb, B Jafrasteh et al.

The paper addresses the difficulty of modeling and predicting neurodegenerative disease progression from medical images when longitudinal neuroimaging datasets are temporally sparse, with gaps and missing follow-up scans making it hard to capture continuous subject-level anatomical change. Validation uses two large-scale longitudinal neuroimage datasets (modalities, cohort sizes, sources, and whether single- or multi-centre are not specified in the abstract). The proposed 4DLoG is a full 4D (3D×T) longitudinal generative diffusion framework that jointly models spatial and temporal dependencies via dedicated spatiotemporal attention with spatial patch extraction and temporal alignment, and explicitly learns the distribution of topology-preserving spatiotemporal deformations, conditioned on clinical and demographic variables. The model is evaluated through synthetic sequence generation, downstream longitudinal disease classification, and brain segmentation, and is reported to outperform state-of-the-art baselines in generating anatomically accurate, temporally consistent, and clinically meaningful brain trajectories, though the abstract gives no numerical results. What is new is the combination of a full 4D diffusion framework with explicit modeling of topology-preserving deformation distributions, enabling generation of plausible future brain states from a scan at any time point.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via unpaywall-pdf)

4DLoG addresses the clinically motivated problem of predicting an individual's future brain anatomy from a single baseline T1-weighted MRI, given that real longitudinal neuroimaging cohorts are sparse, irregularly sampled, and plagued by dropout. The authors propose a two-stage framework: (1) a diffeomorphic registration network (SVF-based, scaling-and-squaring) that maps the baseline scan to each available follow-up, yielding a sequence of stationary velocity fields; and (2) a transformer-based denoising diffusion model that jointly generates the entire 4D (3D×T) sequence of velocity fields, conditioned on baseline age, disease class, and the spatial gradient of the baseline image as an anatomical prior. Key architectural choices are frame-wise 3D volumetric patch embedding (LVPE), which avoids tubelet assumptions of uniform temporal spacing, age-aligned patchwise positional encoding (APPE), factorized spatial/temporal attention, and adaLN conditioning. Generated velocity fields are integrated and applied to the baseline to synthesize follow-up volumes. On ADNI (1021 subjects, 4 time points, 128³), the deformation variant achieves FID 0.241, PSNR 25.90, SSIM 0.93, KID 0.003, outperforming Latent-SADM, BrLP, and CounterSynth with paired t-test significance; only 3.5×10⁻⁴% negative Jacobian determinants indicate strong topology preservation. Augmentation with synthetic scans improved VGG3D AD/CN/MCI classification and hippocampal U-Net Dice by up to ~3% on OASIS. The genuinely new element is full 4D diffusion in deformation (velocity) space rather than intensity space, with irregular-time-aware encoding; the downstream gains are modest and largely augmentation-based.

Datasets

  • ADNI: T1-weighted MRI, 1021 subjects with ≥4 visits, 128×128×128×4, public, 70/15/15 subject-level train/val/test; 512-subject subset (181 AD/201 MCI/130 CN) for downstream classification
  • OASIS-3: T1-weighted MRI, 53 subjects × 4 scans = 212 volumes, 128³, public, 42 train / 11 test subject-level; used only for out-of-distribution downstream hippocampus segmentation (SynthSeg labels)

Method

  • Two-stage: SVF diffeomorphic registration network (CNN) producing velocity fields baseline→each follow-up; then transformer diffusion model (DiT-style LDT) over the 4D velocity-field sequence
  • Frame-wise 3D patch embedding (patch 4, stride 4) + age-aligned sinusoidal positional encoding (APPE) + fixed 3D spatial encoding; factorized spatial then temporal attention blocks
  • Conditioning via adaLN on diffusion timestep, disease class, and spatial gradient of baseline image; L1 noise-prediction loss; cosine schedule, 1000 diffusion steps, predictor-corrector sampling (M=2)
  • Velocity fields synthesized at 32³ and trilinearly upsampled to 128³; registration trained 1500 epochs, diffusion ~200K steps, Adam, lr 1e-4, batch 48, A100 GPUs
  • Baselines: Latent-SADM (VAE-GAN latent), BrLP (no AUX), CounterSynth; ablations LDT-S/L/XL and intensity-space variant 4DLoG-Int (VAE-GAN latent)

Validation

  • Internal ADNI test split (15%, subject-level) for generation metrics; paired t-tests vs each baseline
  • OASIS used as independent out-of-distribution cohort for downstream segmentation only; no external test for generation quality
  • Metrics: PSNR, SSIM, FID, KID, negative Jacobian determinant percentage; downstream VGG3D accuracy and U-Net Dice

Results

  • 4DLoG-Def: FID 0.241±0.2, PSNR 25.90±1.30, SSIM 0.93±0.01, KID 0.003±0.005 vs CounterSynth 0.78/25.39/0.92/0.008, BrLP 2.095/22.38/0.87/0.053, Latent-SADM 9.255/19.16/0.83/1.119
  • Paired t-tests vs CounterSynth: PSNR p=1.18e-17, SSIM p=2.56e-9, FID p=1.13e-3, KID p=1.34e-3 (all significant)
  • Topology: only 3.5×10⁻⁴% negative DetJac across all samples; per-frame negative DetJac ≤1.9e-6 to 7.5e-4 across LDT variants
  • Scaling: LDT-XL FID 0.160 vs LDT-L 0.241 vs LDT-S 0.499; PSNR roughly flat (25.4–25.9)
  • Downstream: hippocampal Dice improved up to ~3% with 1–3 synthetic frames; classification accuracy improved across CN/MCI/AD and all training-set sizes (figures only, no tabulated values)

Limitations

  • Two-stage design: registration errors propagate; underestimated velocity fields leak geometric change into appearance, overestimated ones inject artifacts, especially for disease-transition subjects (authors' stated)
  • No explicit future disease-status inference; assumes constant diagnosis across the sequence, which is unrealistic for MCI converters (authors' stated)
  • External validation limited to OASIS for segmentation only; generation metrics are internal to ADNI (authors' stated)
  • No tabulated downstream classification numbers or confidence intervals; intensity vs deformation comparison is largely qualitative; only 4 fixed time points modeled

Availability

Code available on GitHub (URL not given in text); ADNI and OASIS are public repositories.

Anatomy-informed CT Spatial Phenotype Associated with Extrapancreatic Perineural Invasion in Resected Pancreatic Ductal Adenocarcinoma.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Radiology: Artificial Intelligence · IF 20.1journal list2026-10-28C Chen, J Yu, Y Shen et al.

The problem is identifying extrapancreatic perineural invasion (EPNI) in resected pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma from preoperative imaging, where the finding is otherwise not directly visible. The data are contrast-enhanced CT from a multicenter cohort of 1854 patients (median age 63 years, 1131 male), spanning training, internal, neoadjuvant, retrospective external, prospective external, and TCIA test sets. The method is the PROPHET model, an interpretable CT tumor-vessel spatial phenotype with a locked threshold, evaluated by AUC and threshold metrics, compared against six radiologists reading with and without assistance, and linked to survival via a stratified Cox model. AUCs were 0.92 in prospective external testing, 0.85 in retrospective external testing, 0.90 after neoadjuvant therapy, 0.99 internally, and 0.80 in TCIA, where sensitivity was 80% (24/30) but specificity only 11% (2/19); radiologist assistance raised mean sensitivity from 82% to 93% and cut reading time from 3.64 to 3.02 minutes per patient, and higher PROPHET risk was associated with shorter survival (adjusted HR 1.95, 95% CI 1.53-2.47). What is new is a CT-visible spatial phenotype tied to pathologically confirmed EPNI that also carries prognostic information and improves radiologist sensitivity when used as decision support.

Deep dive (from abstract only)

The authors developed the PROPHET (Preoperative Risk of Perineural High-risk Extension in Tumors) model, an interpretable CT spatial phenotype capturing tumor-vessel relationships, and locked both model and threshold before any testing. A Multicenter Ultrasound Habitat Radiomics Framework For Diagnosing Tuberculous Lymphadenitis And Stratifying Rifampicin Resistance Risk.

Radiomicsscore 9late · published 2026-10-27 Academic Radiology · IF≈3.32026-10-27T Qian, P Chen, Y Zhang et al.

The study addresses the need to noninvasively distinguish tuberculous lymphadenitis (TBLN) from non-tuberculous lymphadenopathy and to predict rifampicin resistance risk in confirmed TBLN patients. It used a multicenter retrospective cohort of 618 patients with superficial lymphadenopathy who underwent B-mode ultrasound at four tertiary hospitals between January 2022 and December 2025, with 530 definitively diagnosed patients for diagnostic differentiation (training and external validation cohorts) and 236 confirmed TBLN patients for resistance risk assessment. The method was a two-stage ultrasound habitat radiomics framework, with rifampicin resistance risk assessed via nested cross-validated out-of-fold predictions and performance evaluated by ROC, calibration, decision curve analysis, and SHAP-based interpretability. The habitat-based model achieved an AUC of 0.83 in the training cohort for differentiating tuberculous from non-tuberculous nodes with consistent external test performance, and an AUC of 0.83 for predicting rifampicin resistance, outperforming conventional radiomics with superior clinical net benefit. Spatial visualization localized high-risk habitats to specific intralesional regions, indicating that spatial heterogeneity contributes to both diagnosis and drug resistance assessment.

Development and Multicenter Validation of a Hybrid Radiomics-Deep Learning Model for Risk Stratification of O-RADS 4 Adnexal Masses.

Medical imaging MLscore 9late · published 2026-10-27 Academic Radiology · IF≈3.32026-10-27Y Sun, H Shen, Y Gu et al.

O-RADS 4 adnexal masses are indeterminate on ultrasound, and the study addresses the need to distinguish benign from borderline/malignant lesions in this category. The data comprise ultrasound images from 653 retrospective patients across four centres (521 from Center A for training and internal validation, 132 from Centers B-D for external validation) plus 126 prospectively enrolled patients from Center A, with pathology as the gold standard. The method combines a machine-learning radiomics Rad-score, a transfer-learning CNN DL-score, and clinical predictors into a hybrid model, evaluated by AUC and, in the prospective set, by six radiologists reading with and without model assistance. The hybrid model achieved AUCs of 0.88 (95% CI 0.82-0.93) in prospective validation and 0.86 (95% CI 0.78-0.92) in external validation, model assistance significantly improved accuracy and specificity for all readers (p < 0.05), inter-reader agreement rose from kappa 0.49 to 0.73, and the model separated 4a from 4b lesions with malignancy rates of 4.5%-16.1% versus 75.4%-97.1% across datasets. The work is notable for multicentre external and prospective validation and for demonstrating reader-level benefit as a decision-support tool rather than model performance alone.

UNICAL: Uncertainty-Aware Multimodal Learning with MRI-Derived Synthetic Amyloid PET for Alzheimer's Disease Classification.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Journal of Imaging Informatics in Medicine · IF≈3.92026-10-27SSA Challapalli, KM Bhattarai, FA Kamangar et al.

The limited availability of amyloid PET motivates deriving molecular-imaging representations from routinely acquired MRI for Alzheimer's disease classification. Synthetic 18F-florbetapir PET was validated in 228 paired OASIS-3 MRI-AV45 examinations, and the framework was developed on 813 OASIS-3 sessions from 811 participants and externally tested on a matched ADNI3 cohort of 261 participants (87 AD, 174 CN), with the development and external endpoints differing (AD-spectrum versus AD-versus-CN labels). UNICAL combines a self-supervised 3D MRI encoder, Contourlet Wavelet Stationary Radiomics features, and MRI-derived synthetic PET, where four stochastic PET realizations per MRI (CoCoLIT) provide mean and voxelwise variance for uncertainty gating before probability-anchored transformer fusion. Synthetic PET reached a mean per-examination voxelwise Pearson correlation of r = 0.864 and cortical-composite SUVR agreement of r = 0.954 with acquired PET; participant-grouped five-fold cross-validation over 20 seeds gave AUROC 0.868 and ECE 0.061, and the frozen model achieved AUROC 0.836 (95% CI 0.791-0.876) on ADNI3 with ECE rising to 0.089. The work shows that uncertainty-aware fusion of MRI-derived synthetic amyloid PET retains cross-dataset discrimination under domain shift, with calibration more sensitive to shift than rank discrimination, while remaining distinct from acquired molecular imaging and not a replacement for amyloid PET as a clinical biomarker.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via springer-oa-api)

UNICAL addresses a practical bottleneck in Alzheimer's disease (AD) biomarker work: amyloid PET (18F-florbetapir/AV45) is informative but scarce, whereas structural T1 MRI is ubiquitous. The authors ask whether MRI-derived synthetic amyloid PET, combined with learned and handcrafted MRI representations, can improve AD-spectrum versus cognitively normal (CN) classification, and whether such a pipeline survives cross-dataset transfer. The approach has three branches: (1) a 3D CNN-transformer encoder pretrained by masked autoencoding (60% mask, 300 epochs, 512-d embedding); (2) CoWS-Rad engineered descriptors (wavelet, contourlet/directional, GLCM, gradient, asymmetry, regional features, with ANOVA-selected top 50 of 2288); and (3) synthetic AV45 PET from a frozen CoCoLIT latent-diffusion model, run stochastically K=4 times. Voxelwise variance across realizations forms an uncertainty map that exponentially gates the mean synthetic PET (alpha=0.8) before PET encoding, and an 18-d PET/uncertainty summary feeds subject-level reliability weighting. Modality-specific probabilities form a weighted anchor, and a one-layer transformer adds a tanh-bounded residual (max 0.50 logit). In OASIS-3 (813 sessions, 5-fold participant-grouped CV x 20 seeds), UNICAL reached AUROC 0.868 versus 0.826 for probability averaging and 0.791 for synthetic PET alone. Frozen transfer to a matched ADNI3 cohort (n=261) gave AUROC 0.836 (95% CI 0.791-0.876), with calibration degrading more (ECE 0.061 to 0.089). Genuinely new elements are the stochastic-variance gating of synthetic PET and the bounded-residual probability-anchor fusion, plus paired synthetic-versus-acquired PET validation (cortical SUVR r=0.954; 89.9% amyloid-status concordance). The synthetic PET branch is, however, a learned transform of MRI, not independent molecular evidence.

  • External: frozen model, preprocessing, and threshold applied to matched ADNI3 cohort with no retraining, refitting, recalibration, or threshold optimization; 2000 subject-level bootstrap CIs
  • Metrics: AUROC (primary), AUPRC, balanced accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, ECE, Brier score; K in {2,4,8,16} sensitivity analysis over 5 seeds; exploratory sex/scanner/race subgroups
