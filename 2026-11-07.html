TotalSpineSeg: automated whole-spine vertebral segmentation with high robustness to labeling errors

Bone and skeletalscore 9 European Radiology · IF 4.7journal list2026-11-07Warszawer, Yehuda, Molinier, Nathan, Valošek, Jan et al.

Automated whole-spine vertebral and intervertebral disc instance segmentation is hampered by fusion and fragmentation errors and by heterogeneous contrast, resolution, and field of view, including truncated localizer scans. The model was trained on 1404 MRIs and evaluated across diverse contrasts and pathologies, with generalization tested on CT. TotalSpineSeg is a two-stage cascaded deep learning model that uses an odd/even representation, conditioning the second stage on an odd-intervertebral disc mask from the first stage to assign alternating semantic classes and enforce separation between neighboring vertebrae, combined with landmark-based logic anchored at C2-C3, C7-T1, T12-L1, and L5-S and a localizer-based strategy for short field-of-view scans. It achieved a 96.22% vertebral identification rate on localizer MRI and 92.87% on CT, and 99% intervertebral disc labeling accuracy with a mean positional L2 error of 1.55 mm. The approach matters because the topological constraints remove fusion and fragmentation errors and enable fully automated biomarker extraction from routine clinical scans for assessing stenosis, lesions, and atrophy against normative ranges.

Deep dive (from abstract only)

This paper addresses automated whole-spine vertebral and intervertebral disc instance segmentation, plus spinal cord and spinal canal segmentation, in arbitrary 3D medical images regardless of contrast, resolution, or field of view. The clinical motivation is fully automated biomarker extraction (stenosis, lesions, atrophy) against normative ranges. The approach is a two-stage cascaded deep learning model trained on 1404 MRIs. Its central novelty is an 'odd/even' representation: stage one predicts an odd-intervertebral disc mask, and stage two is conditioned on it to assign alternating semantic classes to adjacent vertebrae, enforcing separation and preventing fusion (adjacent vertebrae merging) and fragmentation (one vertebra splitting) errors. A landmark-based logic anchors sequential labeling at C2-C3, C7-T1, T12-L1, and L5-S, while a localizer-based strategy handles truncated, short field-of-view scans. Headline results: 96.22% vertebral identification rate on localizer MRI, 92.87% on CT, and 99% intervertebral disc labeling accuracy with mean positional L2 error of 1.55 mm, reported as surpassing state-of-the-art. Genuinely new is the topology-constrained odd/even conditioning that converts instance separation into a semantic labeling problem, plus explicit handling of localizers and truncated fields of view. Caveats: only the abstract was available, so architectural details, baselines, and statistical testing cannot be verified.

  • Localizer MRI subset, private, evaluation of identification rate
  • CT scans, source not stated, external/generalization test

  • Two-stage cascaded deep learning segmentation model
  • Odd/even representation: stage two conditioned on odd-intervertebral disc mask from stage one to assign alternating vertebral classes
  • Landmark-based logic anchoring sequential labels at C2-C3, C7-T1, T12-L1, L5-S
  • Localizer-based strategy for truncated/short field-of-view scans
  • Baselines: existing state-of-the-art methods (unspecified in abstract)

  • Statistical testing not stated in abstract

  • Vertebral identification rate 92.87% on CT

Virtual liver biopsy for detecting steatohepatitis in chemotherapy-treated patients: comparison with whole-liver radiomics and external validation

Radiomicsscore 9 European Radiology · IF 4.7journal list2026-11-07Ammirabile, Angela, Mastroleo, Federico, De Martino, Renato et al.

Non-invasive preoperative diagnosis of chemotherapy-associated steatohepatitis (CASH) remains an unmet need in patients undergoing liver surgery for colorectal liver metastases. The study used two retrospective cohorts totaling 180 patients: a public Colorectal-Liver-Metastases dataset (105 patients, 38% CASH+) for training and internal validation, and a monocenter external series from Humanitas Research Hospital (75 patients, 19% CASH+) for validation, based on post-chemotherapy CT with slice thickness ≤5 mm. IBSI-compliant radiomic features were extracted from whole-liver parenchyma and from a manually segmented 2-mL cylindrical virtual biopsy of non-tumoral liver, with estimated liver volume from three orthogonal diameters, and four XGBoost models (clinical, clinical+virtual-biopsy radiomics, clinical+virtual-biopsy radiomics+estimated volume, clinical+whole-liver radiomics) were trained with a 70/30 split and 5-fold cross-validation. In internal validation, whole-liver (AUC 0.857, accuracy 0.774) and virtual-biopsy+volume (AUC 0.831, accuracy 0.839) models performed comparably (p = 0.814) and outperformed the virtual-biopsy-only model (AUC 0.724), with SHAP identifying liver volume metrics as key predictors; external validation of the virtual-biopsy+volume model yielded AUC 0.745 and accuracy 0.680. The work indicates that a standardized virtual biopsy combined with volumetry can match whole-liver radiomics for CASH prediction while offering a reproducible, localized sampling strategy.

Deep dive (from full text via springer-oa-api)

This retrospective study addresses the non-invasive diagnosis of chemotherapy-associated steatohepatitis (CASH), a histologic entity (Kleiner score ≥4) linked to postoperative liver failure and 90-day mortality after resection of colorectal liver metastases, which conventional imaging cannot detect. The authors propose a 'virtual liver biopsy' (VB): a manually placed cylindrical ROI (10 mm diameter, 25 mm height, ~2 cm³) in non-tumoral right-lobe parenchyma on portal-phase CT, from which 107 IBSI-compliant PyRadiomics features are extracted. XGBoost models combine clinical data (age, sex, BMI), VB radiomics, and an estimated liver volume (eLV) from three orthogonal diameters, and are compared against a whole-liver radiomic model (tumors plus 5-mm peritumoral rings excluded). Training/internal testing used the public TCIA Colorectal-Liver-Metastases cohort (n=105; CASH 38%); external validation used a Humanitas cohort (n=75; CASH 19%). Internal test AUCs were 0.713 (clinical), 0.724 (VB), 0.831 (VB+eLV) and 0.857 (whole liver), with no significant difference between the latter two (DeLong p=0.814). External AUC for VB+eLV was 0.745 (accuracy 0.680, sensitivity 0.714, specificity 0.672). The genuinely new element is the demonstration that a small, rapidly delineable standardized ROI plus a diameter-based volume surrogate matches whole-liver radiomics, with SHAP showing liver volume as the dominant predictor (threshold effect ~1700 cm³). The absolute performance is moderate, and the incremental value over volume/BMI benchmarks is modest.

Datasets

  • TCIA 'Colorectal-Liver-Metastases' (MSKCC, 2003-2007): contrast-enhanced abdominal CT, 197 patients originally, 105 after inclusion; public; train (70%)/internal test (30%)
  • Humanitas Research Hospital cohort (Costa et al., Milan, 2018-2020): contrast-enhanced abdominal CT, 78 patients originally, 75 after inclusion; private (available from corresponding author); external validation only

Method

  • XGBoost gradient boosting binary classifier; four models: clinical, clinical+VB radiomics (ViB), clinical+VB radiomics+eLV (ViBE+), clinical+whole-liver radiomics (WholeLiver)
  • 107 IBSI-compliant PyRadiomics v3.0.1 features (first-order, shape, texture) from unfiltered portal-phase CT in native 3D; shape features only for Liver-ROI; 5-mm peritumoral ring excluded from Liver-ROI
  • Preprocessing: near-zero variance filter, pairwise correlation pruning at r>0.85, z-score scaling fitted on training only; grid search with 5-fold CV maximizing AUC; Youden-J threshold
  • eLV = (AP × CC × Trans)/3.6 from three orthogonal diameters; benchmark models: VB mean HU, Liver-ROI mean HU, eLV alone, BMI+eLV
  • SHAP summary and dependence plots; logistic recalibration with Brier score; decision curve analysis

Validation

  • Internal 70/30 stratified hold-out split of the public cohort; external validation of ViBE+ only in the independent Humanitas cohort (WholeLiver not externally tested)
  • AUC with 95% bootstrap CIs (5000 resamples, BCa), DeLong test for pairwise AUC comparisons; accuracy, sensitivity, specificity; Brier score and calibration curves; DCA net benefit
  • Inter-rater reproducibility in 50 internal patients: ICC 0.921 for eLV, median ICC 0.896 for radiomic features

Results

  • Internal test AUC: ViBE+ 0.831 (95% CI 0.549-0.956) vs WholeLiver 0.857 (0.676-0.954), DeLong p=0.814; ViB 0.724; clinical 0.713
  • External validation ViBE+: AUC 0.745 (0.604-0.886), accuracy 0.680, sensitivity 0.714, specificity 0.672
  • Internal ViBE+ operating point: sensitivity 0.583, specificity 1.000; WholeLiver sensitivity 1.000, specificity 0.632
  • Benchmark models lower: AUC 0.624-0.701 internally and 0.597-0.656 externally vs 0.831 and 0.745 for ViBE+
  • Calibration Brier score 0.162 (internal) and 0.128 (external); eLV strongly correlated with segmented mesh volume (r=0.873); SHAP threshold effect at ~1700 cm³

Limitations

  • Retrospective design, small samples (internal test set ~32 patients, 14 external events) yielding wide confidence intervals and unstable thresholds
  • External net benefit on DCA modest and near zero at most thresholds; calibration showed mild overprediction at high risk
  • Only limited clinical variables (no chemotherapy regimen/duration) in the public dataset; no central pathology review across cohorts; single-reader segmentation with only 50-patient reproducibility subset
  • Whole-liver model not externally validated, so the claimed equivalence rests on internal comparison only; single contrast phase and native-space extraction may retain acquisition variability; CASH-specific proof of concept

Availability

Public TCIA dataset for training; external cohort available from the corresponding author; code availability not stated.

Accuracy and reliability of privacy-compliant large language models for MRI protocol selection in radiological workflows

EHR and clinical NLPscore 8 European Radiology · IF 4.7journal list2026-11-07Bendella, Zeynep, Clauberg, Ralf, Kindler, Christine et al.

Manual MRI protocol selection from clinical referrals is time-consuming and variable, motivating an evaluation of whether open-weight large language models can automate it accurately. The study used 1,201 consecutive real-world referral forms (mean age 58 ± 14 years; 646 men) from a single institution, given as unaltered text, with a consensus of two board-certified radiologists as reference. Each of four open-weight LLMs selected an institutional protocol and optional sequences from predefined lists, scored by protocol accuracy and mean Jaccard index. Larger general-purpose models performed best (GPT-OSS-120B: Jaccard 0.96, 95% CI 0.96–0.97, accuracy 0.94, 0.93–0.95; Qwen-3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507: Jaccard 0.96, 0.95–0.97, accuracy 0.94, 0.93–0.96), exceeding MedGemma-27B-Text-IT (Jaccard 0.85, accuracy 0.79) and Gemma-3-27B-IT (Jaccard 0.61, accuracy 0.58), with unacceptable selections ranging from 5.8% (GPT-OSS) and 5.9% (Qwen3) to 24.7% (MedGemma) and 68.3% (Gemma3). The work shows that privacy-compliant open-weight LLMs can approach expert protocolling concordance, but model-dependent clinically relevant errors, most often anatomically correct yet clinically suboptimal protocols, indicate that radiologist oversight remains necessary.

Deep dive (from full text via springer-oa-api)

This retrospective single-center study asks whether locally deployable, open-weight LLMs can select appropriate MRI protocols and optional sequences from unstructured clinical referral text, a task normally requiring radiologist expertise and a source of inter-institutional variability. The authors extracted 1201 consecutive referrals (Aug 2023–Jul 2024; 77.5% brain, 15.5% spine) after excluding 199 incomplete cases, and prompted four open-weight models (GPT-OSS-120b, Qwen3-235B-A22B, Gemma-3-27B-IT, MedGemma-27B-IT) with age, sex, symptoms, indication, and an institution-specific protocol/sequence library, constraining output via JSON schema and XGrammar-guided decoding. Reference standards were consensus of two board-certified radiologists; concordance was rated identical/acceptable/unacceptable, with Jaccard index, protocol accuracy, and sequence-level F1 as metrics. GPT-OSS and Qwen3 reached Jaccard 0.96, protocol accuracy 0.94, and F1 0.96–0.97, with unacceptable rates of 5.8–5.9%; MedGemma (Jaccard 0.85, accuracy 0.79) and Gemma3 (0.61, 0.58; 68.3% unacceptable) lagged. The genuinely new contribution is a head-to-head comparison of four contemporary open-weight models at both protocol and sequence level with scan-time analysis and structured error classification, showing that scale and instruction tuning, not narrow medical fine-tuning, drove performance. The clinical framing (privacy-preserving local deployment) is relevant, but the task is largely standardized and the evidence remains retrospective and single-institution.

Datasets

  • Single-center retrospective cohort, University of Bonn radiology information system; clinical referral text (age, sex, symptoms, indication); 1400 retrieved, 1201 analyzed after excluding 199 incomplete; private; single split used for evaluation against radiologist consensus (no train/val/external test)

Method

  • Four open-weight LLMs without fine-tuning: GPT-OSS-120b (MoE reasoning), Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct, Gemma-3-27B-IT (dense), MedGemma-27B-Text-IT (medical fine-tune of Gemma3)
  • Two-step output: one primary protocol from institutional library plus optional supplementary sequences; identical prompt instructing expert radiological planning with clinical correctness and time efficiency criteria
  • Structured JSON output via Pydantic schema with XGrammar-guided decoding in SGLang v0.5.3; provider-recommended sampling parameters, single inference pass per referral; 8×A100 80GB
  • Baselines: pairwise model comparisons and comparison to two-radiologist consensus reference; no classical NLP/ML baselines reimplemented

Validation

  • Internal only: retrospective single-center evaluation against consensus of two board-certified radiologists (blinded to model outputs); no external or prospective test set
  • Metrics: mean Jaccard index, protocol accuracy, sequence precision/recall/F1, identical/acceptable/unacceptable ratings, estimated scan duration; 1000-sample bootstrap 95% CIs
  • Statistics: Wilcoxon signed-rank with Pratt correction for pairwise model comparisons, Friedman with Dunn post hoc for scan times, Cohen's kappa (quadratic weighted) for inter-rater agreement; LLM-based error classifier checked against blinded radiologist reassessment in 100 cases

Results

  • GPT-OSS: Jaccard 0.96 (0.96–0.97), protocol accuracy 0.94 (0.93–0.95), F1 0.97 (0.96–0.98); Qwen3 statistically indistinguishable (p=0.20)
  • Qwen3: Jaccard 0.96 (0.95–0.97), protocol accuracy 0.94 (0.93–0.96), F1 0.96 (0.95–0.97)
  • MedGemma: Jaccard 0.85 (0.84–0.87), accuracy 0.79 (0.76–0.81), F1 0.86; Gemma3: Jaccard 0.61 (0.59–0.63), accuracy 0.58 (0.55–0.60), F1 0.68
  • Unacceptable ratings: GPT-OSS 70/1201 (5.8%), Qwen3 71/1201 (5.9%), MedGemma 297/1201 (24.7%), Gemma3 820/1201 (68.3%); dominant error was anatomically correct but clinically suboptimal protocol (57.7–70.7%)
  • Scan time: reference median 21:05; GPT-OSS comparable (p=0.18), Qwen3 faster (mean 19:15 vs 19:58 min, p=0.002), Gemma3 much longer (median 31:09), MedGemma 22:08 (p<0.001)

Limitations

  • Single institution with institution-specific protocol library and consensus ground truth reflecting local practice; no external or multi-center validation
  • Retrospective design; 14.2% of referrals excluded for incomplete/ambiguous information, likely biasing toward clearer cases and overestimating performance
  • Partial reader overlap (one radiologist in both reference standard and concordance rating); error-classifier check is internal consistency, not independent validation
  • Single inference pass with non-zero, model-specific sampling temperatures; run-to-run variability and prompt sensitivity not quantified; no closed-source API models compared, so no performance ceiling established

Systolic pressure gradient across myocardial bridge mediates the effect of bridge depth on clinical symptoms

Medical imaging MLscore 8 European Radiology · IF 4.7journal list2026-11-07Sun, Weihang, Song, Meiyi, Li, Jiayun et al.

The study addresses the unclear relationship between myocardial bridge morphology and function and the pathways leading to angina-equivalent symptoms in patients with left anterior descending MB without atherosclerosis. It retrospectively analyzed 180 patients with deep MB (depth > 2 mm) and no plaques, all imaged with photon-counting detector CT angiography at a single centre (source not specified beyond the cohort). CT-FFR was computed by machine learning to derive systolic and diastolic trans-bridge pressure gradients, and MB depth and length were measured, with associations assessed by logistic regression and mediation analysis. Symptomatic patients (n = 81) had greater depth (3.30 vs 2.45 mm), longer length (36.06 vs 26.10 mm), higher systolic gradient (3.81% vs 1.62%) and diastolic gradient (3.45% vs 2.15%) than asymptomatic patients (n = 99, all p < 0.001); depth (OR 1.77) and systolic gradient (OR 1.16) were independent predictors, and the systolic gradient significantly mediated the effect of depth on symptoms (indirect effect 0.1881, 95% CI 0.1023–0.2897). The work indicates that the systolic trans-bridge pressure gradient may serve as a non-invasive imaging biomarker linking MB morphology to clinical presentation.

Deep dive (from full text via springer-oa-api)

This single-center retrospective study asks why some patients with a deep left anterior descending (LAD) myocardial bridge (MB) and no coronary atherosclerosis have angina-equivalent symptoms while others do not, and whether a functional hemodynamic measure explains the structure-symptom link better than anatomy alone. The authors used photon-counting detector CCTA (NAEOTOM Alpha, 400 µm, 140 kV, 67 keV reconstructions) in 180 patients with deep MB (depth >2 mm) and no plaque, and computed on-site ML-based CT-FFR (ShuKun v8.7, reduced-order CFD with a neural network) in both systole and diastole. They defined delta-CT-FFR (ΔCT-FFR) as the relative pressure drop between points immediately proximal and distal to the bridge, separately for systole and diastole, and related it to MB depth/length and to symptoms (81 symptomatic, 99 asymptomatic). Symptomatic patients had deeper (3.30 vs 2.45 mm) and longer (36.1 vs 26.1 mm) bridges and larger ΔCT-FFRsys (3.81% vs 1.62%) and ΔCT-FFRdia (3.45% vs 2.15%), while absolute proximal and distal CT-FFR values did not differ. In multivariable analysis, depth (OR 1.77/mm) and ΔCT-FFRsys (OR 1.16/%) were independent predictors; length was not. Bootstrap mediation showed ΔCT-FFRsys carried 37.6% of the depth-symptom effect (37.4% after age/sex adjustment), whereas the diastolic mediation disappeared after adjustment. The genuinely new element is the demonstration, with a non-invasive within-patient systolic pressure gradient, that the morphological effect of bridge depth on symptoms is partly mediated by systolic hemodynamics; the sample is small, cross-sectional, and single-center, so this is mechanistic hypothesis-generating evidence rather than validated prediction.

Datasets

  • Single-center retrospective CCTA database (Nov 2025–May 2026), 4549 patients screened, 180 included LAD MB patients without atherosclerosis; PCD-CT (NAEOTOM Alpha); private; no train/val/external split (single cohort, symptom vs no-symptom groups)

Method

  • On-site ML-based CT-FFR (ShuKun v8.7): automated coronary segmentation plus reduced-order CFD with a deep neural network for pressure drops
  • ΔCT-FFR defined as relative pressure gradient (proximal minus distal)/proximal ×100%, computed separately in systole and diastole
  • MB depth and length measured automatically by workstation software, verified by two radiologists with senior adjudication; maximum systolic/diastolic values used
  • Statistics: RCS for nonlinearity, Spearman correlation and collinearity checks (VIF<5, r<0.6, κ<10), univariable/multivariable logistic regression, bootstrap mediation (5000 resamples), ROC, post-hoc power and Monte Carlo power for indirect effects, jackknife sensitivity

Validation

  • Internal only: single-center retrospective cohort, no external or prospective test set
  • Metrics: odds ratios with 95% CI, AUC for the multivariable model (reported in supplement), bootstrap bias-corrected CIs for indirect effects
  • Robustness: jackknife leave-one-out resampling of mediation effect; post-hoc power from observed ORs and Monte Carlo simulation (10,000 replications)

Results

  • ΔCT-FFRsys 3.81% [2.33, 6.05] vs 1.62% [1.05, 3.09], p<0.001; ΔCT-FFRdia 3.45% vs 2.15%, p<0.001 (symptomatic vs asymptomatic)
  • Absolute CT-FFR proximal/distal did not differ between groups in either phase (all p≥0.110)
  • Multivariable: depth OR 1.77 per mm (95% CI 1.27–2.46, p=0.001); ΔCT-FFRsys OR 1.16 per % (1.05–1.27, p=0.002); length OR 1.04 (0.99–1.07, p=0.060)
  • Mediation: ΔCT-FFRsys indirect effect 0.1881 (95% CI 0.1023–0.2897), 37.58% of depth effect; after age/sex adjustment 0.1761 (0.0939–0.2726), 37.42%; ΔCT-FFRdia mediation non-significant after adjustment (−0.0031 to 0.1278)
  • Within symptomatic patients, ΔCT-FFRsys exceeded ΔCT-FFRdia (3.81% vs 3.45%, p=0.016); no such difference in asymptomatic patients (p=0.695)

Limitations

  • Single-center, cross-sectional, retrospective design: association not causality; no outcome (MACE) follow-up
  • Symptoms defined by patient report/clinical assessment, subjective; no objective ischemia reference (stress perfusion, CFR) or invasive FFR
  • No assessment of microvascular function, which commonly coexists with MB; LAD-only focus limits generalizability
  • Proprietary single-vendor CT-FFR software and PCD-CT platform; UHR mode used in only a minority; no external multi-center validation

MambaSleepCVD for prediction of long term cardiovascular outcomes from polysomnography

Medical imaging MLscore 7 npj Digital Medicine · IF 15.1journal list2026-11-07Calzoni, Alessia, Rossi, Alvise, Monachino, Giuliana et al.

Long-term cardiovascular disease risk prediction is a clinical priority, and this study asks how much prognostic information full-night polysomnography (PSG) carries once demographic confounding is explicitly controlled. The data are full-night PSG from the Sleep Heart Health Study (SHHS), a single reference cohort, evaluated for survival-based CVD risk ranking. The method is a modular framework built on Coupled Mamba for simultaneous cross-modal and temporal fusion, combining task-specific supervised unimodal encoders, and it is compared against task-agnostic multimodal self-supervised pre-training. Performance is comparable to or better than current architectures in the fully supervised, data-abundant regime, but the cardiac channel alone accounts for most of the discriminative signal, and chronological age strongly drives survival ranking in SHHS, acting as a shortcut when supplied as an explicit input that obscures PSG-derived physiology. The work indicates that supervised unimodal experts integrated through a fusion engine offer a flexible route to prognostic risk ranking from sleep recordings, while reliable CVD screening will require independent external cohorts and explicit confounder control.

SurgPace: Complexity-aware progressive distillation for surgical multimodal large language models

Medical imaging MLscore 7 Medical Image Analysis · IF 11.8journal list2026-11-07Zhicheng He, Xiaohan Xing, Hongqiu Wang et al.

Compact multimodal large language models for robotic surgery are needed because full-size models are too costly to deploy, but direct knowledge distillation is unstable on surgical data, which varies widely in difficulty, visual ambiguity, and required clinical context. The method is evaluated on Surg-396K, a benchmark assembled from four public surgical datasets covering five dialogue paradigms; the abstract does not state cohort size or whether the source datasets are single- or multi-centre. SurgPace is a complexity-aware continuous curriculum for progressive distillation that scores sample difficulty from predictive confidence and Lorentz geometric consistency, uses the fused score to order training from easy to hard, and adds hierarchical representation alignment at the feature, logit, and probability levels while shifting emphasis from teacher imitation to task supervision. The abstract reports that SurgPace consistently outperforms strong MLLM distillation baselines and yields compact, memory-efficient models with faster decoding, but gives no quantitative numbers. Its contribution is separating sample-level curriculum from objective-level scheduling to stabilize distillation of surgical vision-language models.

Virtual liver biopsy for detecting steatohepatitis in chemotherapy-treated patients: comparison with whole-liver radiomics and external validation

Radiomicsscore 9 European Radiology · IF 4.7journal list2026-11-07Ammirabile, Angela, Mastroleo, Federico, De Martino, Renato et al.

Non-invasive preoperative diagnosis of chemotherapy-associated steatohepatitis (CASH) remains an unmet need in patients undergoing liver surgery for colorectal liver metastases. The study used two retrospective cohorts totaling 180 patients: a public Colorectal-Liver-Metastases dataset (105 patients, 38% CASH+) for training and internal validation, and a monocenter external series from Humanitas Research Hospital (75 patients, 19% CASH+) for validation, based on post-chemotherapy CT with slice thickness ≤5 mm. IBSI-compliant radiomic features were extracted from whole-liver parenchyma and from a manually segmented 2-mL cylindrical virtual biopsy of non-tumoral liver, with estimated liver volume from three orthogonal diameters, and four XGBoost models (clinical, clinical+virtual-biopsy radiomics, clinical+virtual-biopsy radiomics+estimated volume, clinical+whole-liver radiomics) were trained with a 70/30 split and 5-fold cross-validation. In internal validation, whole-liver (AUC 0.857, accuracy 0.774) and virtual-biopsy+volume (AUC 0.831, accuracy 0.839) models performed comparably (p = 0.814) and outperformed the virtual-biopsy-only model (AUC 0.724), with SHAP identifying liver volume metrics as key predictors; external validation of the virtual-biopsy+volume model yielded AUC 0.745 and accuracy 0.680. The work indicates that a standardized virtual biopsy combined with volumetry can match whole-liver radiomics for CASH prediction while offering a reproducible, localized sampling strategy.

TotalSpineSeg: automated whole-spine vertebral segmentation with high robustness to labeling errors

Bone and skeletalscore 9 European Radiology · IF 4.7journal list2026-11-07Warszawer, Yehuda, Molinier, Nathan, Valošek, Jan et al.

Automated whole-spine vertebral and intervertebral disc instance segmentation is hampered by fusion and fragmentation errors and by heterogeneous contrast, resolution, and field of view, including truncated localizer scans. The model was trained on 1404 MRIs and evaluated across diverse contrasts and pathologies, with generalization tested on CT. TotalSpineSeg is a two-stage cascaded deep learning model that uses an odd/even representation, conditioning the second stage on an odd-intervertebral disc mask from the first stage to assign alternating semantic classes and enforce separation between neighboring vertebrae, combined with landmark-based logic anchored at C2-C3, C7-T1, T12-L1, and L5-S and a localizer-based strategy for short field-of-view scans. It achieved a 96.22% vertebral identification rate on localizer MRI and 92.87% on CT, and 99% intervertebral disc labeling accuracy with a mean positional L2 error of 1.55 mm. The approach matters because the topological constraints remove fusion and fragmentation errors and enable fully automated biomarker extraction from routine clinical scans for assessing stenosis, lesions, and atrophy against normative ranges.

Accuracy and reliability of privacy-compliant large language models for MRI protocol selection in radiological workflows

EHR and clinical NLPscore 8 European Radiology · IF 4.7journal list2026-11-07Bendella, Zeynep, Clauberg, Ralf, Kindler, Christine et al.

Manual MRI protocol selection from clinical referrals is time-consuming and variable, motivating an evaluation of whether open-weight large language models can automate it accurately. The study used 1,201 consecutive real-world referral forms (mean age 58 ± 14 years; 646 men) from a single institution, given as unaltered text, with a consensus of two board-certified radiologists as reference. Each of four open-weight LLMs selected an institutional protocol and optional sequences from predefined lists, scored by protocol accuracy and mean Jaccard index. Larger general-purpose models performed best (GPT-OSS-120B: Jaccard 0.96, 95% CI 0.96–0.97, accuracy 0.94, 0.93–0.95; Qwen-3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507: Jaccard 0.96, 0.95–0.97, accuracy 0.94, 0.93–0.96), exceeding MedGemma-27B-Text-IT (Jaccard 0.85, accuracy 0.79) and Gemma-3-27B-IT (Jaccard 0.61, accuracy 0.58), with unacceptable selections ranging from 5.8% (GPT-OSS) and 5.9% (Qwen3) to 24.7% (MedGemma) and 68.3% (Gemma3). The work shows that privacy-compliant open-weight LLMs can approach expert protocolling concordance, but model-dependent clinically relevant errors, most often anatomically correct yet clinically suboptimal protocols, indicate that radiologist oversight remains necessary.
