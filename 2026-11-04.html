CT-derived fractional flow reserve for anatomically long-segment coronary disease: a multicentre diagnostic accuracy study

Medical imaging MLscore 9 European Radiology · IF 4.7journal list2026-11-04An, Ziyu, Ma, Zhao, Zhang, Min et al.

The study addresses whether machine-learning-based CT-derived fractional flow reserve (CT-FFR) retains diagnostic accuracy for haemodynamically significant coronary disease in anatomically long-segment lesions (length ≥20 mm), where such assessment may be more challenging. It is a retrospective multicentre study of 329 patients and 377 vessels who underwent coronary CT angiography and invasive pressure-wire FFR within 30 days, with 137 patients and 144 vessels having long-segment disease; invasive FFR ≤0.80 served as the reference standard. CT-FFR was evaluated at vessel and patient levels using diagnostic discrimination, threshold-based classification, and Bland–Altman agreement. At the vessel level, CT-FFR achieved an AUC of 0.941 (95% CI 0.917–0.966), sensitivity 94.3%, specificity 83.7%, and accuracy 88.6%; in long-segment vessels the AUC was 0.961 (95% CI 0.922–1.000), with sensitivity 95.7%, specificity 92.2%, and accuracy 94.4%, and patient-level AUCs were 0.941 overall and 0.971 for long-segment disease. Mean bias was small but limits of agreement were clinically relevant, so the authors position CT-FFR as a non-invasive physiological adjunct rather than a substitute for invasive pressure-wire assessment, noting that values near the 0.80 threshold require cautious interpretation and that subgroup comparisons remained exploratory.

A multi-guideline clinical agent for vascular surgery: architecture, implementation, and benchmark evaluation

EHR and clinical NLPscore 8 JAMIA · IF 7.1journal list2026-11-03Vangelis G Alexiou, Areti Vassiliou, Bauer E Sumpio et al.

Clinicians cannot practically cross-reference the roughly 1100 pages of European Society for Vascular Surgery guidelines at the point of care, and unguarded large language models risk hallucination, motivating a guideline-locked design. The work covers the full ESVS corpus of 14 clinical practice guidelines and evaluates the system on 55 vignettes scored by 2 blinded LLM judges over 3 runs (330 judgments), with clinician adjudication of every flagged claim; the abstract does not state the vignette source or whether data are single- or multi-centre. The system extends a validated single-guideline GraphRAG prototype with an LLM-driven Guideline Router, a Context Gate that withholds retrieval when decision-critical parameters are absent, dual-channel retrieval, and GPT-5 synthesis under a mandatory citation template. It stayed within its locked corpus, declined all out-of-scope queries, achieved 98.4% guideline-routing recall, showed 91.9% specificity and 78.6% sensitivity for underspecified queries at the Context Gate, and all 259 cited recommendations were verifiable within the corpus; judges flagged unsupported claims in 18.2% of items under a sensitive screening definition, and adjudication by 2 vascular surgeons found clinically consequential errors in 7.3% of items, none graded capable of causing harm. Clinical acceptability of complete answers was not scored and is reserved for prospective expert evaluation, so the contribution is the feasibility of a multi-guideline context-aware agent with citation traceability and locked-corpus provenance supporting future auditability.

This paper addresses a practical clinical question in vascular surgery: how to make the roughly 1100 pages of 14 European Society for Vascular Surgery (ESVS) clinical practice guidelines usable at the point of care without exposing clinicians to LLM hallucination. The authors extend a previously validated single-guideline GraphRAG prototype into a multi-guideline agent by adding an LLM-driven Guideline Router, an embedded Context Gate that suppresses retrieval when decision-critical parameters are missing, dual-channel retrieval, and GPT-5 synthesis constrained by a mandatory citation template. Evaluation used an automated benchmark of 55 vignettes scored by two blinded LLM judges over three runs (330 judgments), with clinician adjudication of every flagged claim. Headline results: 98.4% guideline-routing recall, Context Gate specificity 91.9% and sensitivity 78.6% for underspecified queries, and all 259 cited recommendations verifiable within the locked corpus; the system declined all out-of-scope queries. Under a sensitive screening definition, judges flagged unsupported claims in 18.2% of items, but after adjudication by two vascular surgeons only 7.3% of items contained a clinically consequential error, none graded capable of causing harm. The genuinely new elements are the multi-guideline routing layer, the explicit abstention gate for underspecified queries, and corpus-locked citation traceability; the evidence remains a small, LLM-judged benchmark rather than prospective clinical validation, and clinical acceptability of complete answers was not scored.

Cross-paradigm audit of large language model judges for clinical text evaluation

EHR and clinical NLPscore 8 npj Digital Medicine · IF 15.1journal list2026-11-04Pakull, Tabea M. G., Warmer, Sina, Arzideh, Kamyar et al.

The paper addresses the use of large language models as judges (LLM-as-a-judge) for evaluating clinical texts, noting that existing evidence is mostly English and relies on pooled agreement metrics that hide clinically relevant failure modes. It audits six open-weight models against expert judges on four German clinical datasets covering report and nursing documentation summarization, discharge summary generation, and patient letter generation, evaluated across three paradigms and four prompt configurations; the abstract does not state cohort size or whether the data are single- or multi-centre. Deep dive (from full text via springer-oa-api)

This audit asks whether open-weight LLM judges can substitute for expert clinician panels when evaluating German clinical text, a question with direct implications for benchmark design and hospital governance in the EU, where GDPR constrains use of closed commercial APIs. The authors evaluate six open-weight judges (24B–235B parameters, including a medical-domain model and reasoning variants) across three judgment paradigms—pointwise Likert scoring (adapted PDSQI-9), pairwise preference, and pass-or-fail error detection—on four expert-annotated German datasets (radiology/pathology/surgery report summaries, nursing summaries, discharge summaries, and molecular tumor board patient letters), each under four prompt configurations (baseline, rule-augmented, few-shot demonstration, multiagent ensemble). Headline findings: pointwise reliability often approached human–human agreement, but macro MAE was significantly worse than the human threshold on reports; pairwise judging was the weakest paradigm (macro Gwet AC1 0.20–0.50, no judge reaching substantial agreement); pass-or-fail recall ranged from 0.90 down to 0.13. Judges diverged most on items where experts were unanimous, showed 17–34% vote flips under order reversal, and systematically preferred LLM-written over human-written text (odds ratios 2.6–13.8). A decision-impact case study found 57.2% of pointwise ratings changed category, 41.7% of pairwise winners flipped, and 10% of major/critical errors were missed. Genuinely new: a cross-paradigm, consensus-stratified, bias-and-decision-impact audit on non-English clinical text with expert (not lay) references, showing that pooled agreement statistics mask clinically consequential failure modes and that within-paradigm validation does not transfer.

  • Clinical Report Summaries (radiology, pathology, surgery; Qwen3-235B-generated; 2695 ratings; 7 adapted PDSQI-9 criteria; 3–4 clinicians; private, single German university hospital; pointwise)
  • Nursing Documentation Summaries (47 full-documentation + 50 endpoint summaries; Qwen3-235B-generated; 3990 ratings; 10 adapted PDSQI-9 criteria incl. 2 binary stigma criteria; ≥4 raters; private; pointwise)
  • Discharge Summaries (35 human-vs-LLM pairs; 700 ratings; 5 comparative criteria; 4 clinicians; private; pairwise)
  • Molecular Tumor Board Patient Letters (710 segments; Llama-3.3-70B-generated; 2 oncologists; union of major/critical error flags; private; pass-or-fail)
  • All datasets are fixed expert-annotated evaluation sets; no train/val split—judges are used zero/few-shot, so all data serve as test items

Method

  • Six open-weight judges: Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507-FP8, Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking-2507-FP8, gpt-oss-120b (MXFP4), Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Instruct, medgemma-27b-it, Magistral-Small-2509
  • Four prompt configurations: baseline rubric, rule-augmented (codebook operationalizations), demonstration-based few-shot, and multiagent majority-vote ensemble (gpt-oss-120b, Magistral-Small-2509, Qwen3-235B-Instruct)
  • Constrained decoding via llguidance; model-card-recommended inference settings; three random seeds per judgment; vLLM on A100/H100; 61.1 GPU-hours total
  • Reference labels: human median (ordinal), human majority (binary/pairwise), union of clinician error flags (pass-or-fail); no LLM-judge baselines beyond the six models and four prompt variants

Validation

  • Internal only: single-institution expert panels; no external or prospective validation; bootstrap 95% CIs (1000 item-level resamples)
  • Metrics: MAE, accuracy, recall, Gwet AC2 (quadratic weights) and AC1 vs human–human reference; Binned Jensen–Shannon divergence stratified by consensus (≤60%, 60–100%, 100%)
  • Bias tests: intra-pair instability under order swap, McNemar tests with Holm correction for identity bias, per-item seed disagreement across three seeds; FDR-corrected comparisons to human thresholds

Results

  • Pointwise reports: macro MAE significantly worse than human threshold 0.53 for all judges; succinct criterion MAE 1.05–1.40
  • Reliability: macro AC2 0.69–0.86 (nursing) and 0.62–0.87 (reports); pass-or-fail AC1 0.52–0.88; pairwise only 0.20–0.50, no judge reaching substantial agreement
  • Pass-or-fail recall 0.90 (Qwen3-235B-Instruct/rule) to 0.13 (medgemma-27b-it/rule); highest-recall config had lowest macro AC1 (0.517)
  • Pairwise: intra-pair instability 17–34%; accuracy dropped when human summary was second (e.g., medgemma 54%→30%); flip-toward-model odds ratios 2.6–13.8, all McNemar p≤0.003
  • Decision impact: 57.2% pointwise category flips (95% CI 48.5–64.8), 41.7% pairwise winner flips (26.9–58.4), 10% missed major/critical errors (5–18%)

Limitations

  • Only open-weight models, some quantized (FP8/MXFP4), potentially affecting calibration; closed frontier models untested
  • Single-institution clinician panels and small samples constrained by annotation cost; no multi-center validation
  • No dataset evaluated under more than one paradigm, so paradigm and dataset effects are confounded; only one ensemble strategy tested
  • Prompt space not exhausted (no weighted voting, personas, debate, or fine-tuning); authors note pooled statistics can hide criterion-level failures, but the audit itself remains retrospective with no deployment trial

Code and inference configurations deposited in a Zenodo repository; datasets are private single-institution clinical data from prior studies (not publicly released).

External Validation of a Machine Learning Model for Delirium Risk

EHR and clinical NLPscore 8 JAMA Network Open · IF 11.7journal list2026-11-04Joseph I. Friedman, Prathamesh Parchure, Weijia Fu et al.

Published external validation of machine learning delirium risk models is scarce, and such models often degrade when transported to new hospitals. The study evaluated live deployment of a fusion multimodal model incorporating natural language processing among patients aged 60 years or older admitted to non-intensive care units at two Mount Sinai hospitals (MSH development site and MSM external site), covering 33,095 admissions in total, with 9,020 MSM admissions scored during live operations and compared with 21,186 MSH admissions. Performance was assessed via AUROC and monthly delirium detection rates using a 2x2 confusion matrix comparing pre- and post-deployment periods. The model achieved an AUROC of 0.91 (95% CI, 0.90-0.92) at MSM and 0.94 (95% CI, 0.93-0.94) at MSH without retraining, with positive predictive values of 25.9% and 19.4%, respectively, and the mean monthly delirium detection rate at MSM rose from 9.23% to 19.59% after deployment (P < .001). The findings indicate that discriminative performance was maintained across hospitals without retraining and that deployment increased diagnostic yield, though at the cost of a high false positive rate.

The study evaluated live deployment of a fusion multimodal model incorporating natural language processing among patients aged 60 years or older admitted to non-intensive care units at two Mount Sinai hospitals (MSH development site and MSM external site), covering 33,095 admissions in total, with 9,020 MSM admissions scored during live operations and compared with 21,186 MSH admissions. Performance was assessed via AUROC and monthly delirium detection rates using a 2x2 confusion matrix comparing pre- and post-deployment periods. The model achieved an AUROC of 0.91 (95% CI, 0.90-0.92) at MSM and 0.94 (95% CI, 0.93-0.94) at MSH without retraining, with positive predictive values of 25.9% and 19.4%, respectively, and the mean monthly delirium detection rate at MSM rose from 9.23% to 19.59% after deployment (P < .001). The findings indicate that discriminative performance was maintained across hospitals without retraining and that deployment increased diagnostic yield, though at the cost of a high false positive rate.
