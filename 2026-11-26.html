Dual-domain regularized deep unrolled MR fingerprinting reconstruction using structural and spatial constraints.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Medical Image Analysis · IF 11.8journal list2026-11-24P Li, Y Ji, Y Hu

Existing MRF reconstruction methods mainly exploit matricized or tensorized data characteristics, leaving nonlinear structural information in the fingerprint domain and tissue-parameter-domain priors underused, which limits imaging accuracy. The study evaluates the proposed framework on both simulated and in vivo datasets, though the abstract does not specify modality details beyond MRF, cohort sizes, data source, or whether the data are single- or multi-centre. The method is a dual-domain regularized deep unrolled framework that jointly enforces geometric structure priors of fingerprints, generalized to a wider range of Lp norms, and spatial sparsity constraints on parameter maps, implemented as a learnable p-norm enhanced manifold structure regularization module plus an attention-based soft-thresholding sparsity module. The abstract reports that the method outperforms several state-of-the-art methods on simulated and in vivo data but gives no quantitative numbers. Its contribution is the joint use of fingerprint-domain structural priors and parameter-map spatial constraints within a learned unrolled reconstruction, extending manifold priors beyond standard formulations.

Deep dive (from abstract only)

This paper addresses quantitative MRI reconstruction in magnetic resonance fingerprinting (MRF), where the goal is to jointly recover high-quality fingerprints and accurate tissue parameter maps (e.g., T1/T2) from highly undersampled, temporally varying acquisitions. The clinical motivation is reliable multi-parametric tissue characterization, relevant to musculoskeletal and bone-marrow imaging where quantitative relaxometry supports tissue composition assessment. The authors propose a dual-domain regularized deep unrolled framework that enforces two priors simultaneously: a manifold/geometric structure prior on fingerprints (generalized from existing formulations to a family of Lp norms) and a spatial sparsity prior on the parameter maps. The iterative reconstruction is unrolled into a network with two learned modules: a learnable p-norm enhanced manifold structure regularization module providing data-driven structural priors, and a learned sparsity module using an attention-based soft-thresholding operator. Experiments on simulated and in vivo datasets are reported to outperform several state-of-the-art methods. Because only the abstract is available, specific quantitative gains, datasets, and baselines cannot be verified. The genuinely new elements are the Lp-generalized manifold prior with learnable p and the attention-driven soft-thresholding for parameter-map sparsity within an unrolled MRF solver; the underlying unrolling paradigm itself is established.

The authors argue existing CT-radiomics or clinical-only models suffer from labor-intensive ROI annotation, unimodal fragility, and single-endpoint scope. Machine Learning and Delta Habitat Radiomics Predict Neoadjuvant Chemoradiotherapy Response in Locally Advanced Rectal Cancer: A Multicenter Study.

Radiomicsscore 10 Biomedicines · IF≈6.12026-11-13Y Zhang, L Wan, H Lin et al.

Predicting response to neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy and long-term outcomes in locally advanced rectal cancer non-invasively remains difficult. The study used 1228 LARC patients who completed NCRT across five tertiary centres, retrospectively split into four datasets, with serial T2-weighted and diffusion-weighted MRI acquired before and after treatment and co-registered for delta habitat radiomic feature extraction. Fourteen machine-learning algorithms were trained and externally validated across three signature sets (T2 delta habitat, DWI delta habitat, combined), with performance assessed by ROC, Kaplan-Meier survival, and decision-curve analysis. The combined T2WI+DWI delta habitat multilayer perceptron achieved AUCs of 0.932, 0.903, 0.925, and 0.922 in training, validation, Test1, and Test2 cohorts, versus 0.801-0.867 for T2 alone and 0.769-0.895 for DWI alone. Deep dive (from full text via europepmc)

This retrospective multicenter study addresses the clinically unresolved problem of predicting pathological response to neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (NCRT) in locally advanced rectal cancer (LARC), where accurate identification of responders could enable watch-and-wait or local excision strategies. The authors introduce a delta habitat radiomics framework: pre- and post-NCRT T2WI and DWI are voxel-wise clustered (Gaussian mixture model on five GLCM features within 5x5x5 sliding windows, k selected by silhouette, fitted only on training data) into intratumoral subregions ('habitats'); 4683 Pyradiomics features per habitat are extracted, averaged across habitats, and delta features (relative pre-to-post change) are selected via t-test, correlation pruning, and LASSO. Fourteen classifiers were benchmarked across a training cohort (n=809) and three external cohorts (n=203, 113, 103). A multilayer perceptron (MLP) on combined T2+DWI delta habitat features achieved AUCs of 0.932, 0.903, 0.925, and 0.922 across the four cohorts, outperforming single-modality models (T2: 0.788-0.867; DWI: 0.769-0.895). Exploratory analyses examined ypT1N0M0/ypCR discrimination (AUC 0.717-0.784) and survival stratification. The genuinely new element is the combination of longitudinal delta radiomics with spatially resolved habitat clustering, validated across four cohorts with leakage-controlled pipeline fitting; however, the methodological novelty is incremental, and the survival and organ-preservation claims are explicitly exploratory and inconsistently significant across cohorts.

Clinical and AI-derived digital pathology features of invasive lobular and ductal carcinoma for predicting 5-year recurrence or metastasis.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Breast · IF≈2.12026-11-19SW Cao, XY Wang, HY Zhang et al.

Invasive lobular carcinoma (ILC) is known to differ from invasive ductal carcinoma (IDC) in morphology and metastatic behaviour, but its outcomes and quantitative tissue features are incompletely characterised, motivating a comparison of clinical and digital pathology features for predicting 5-year recurrence or metastasis. The retrospective study included 9476 patients (831 ILC, 8645 IDC) from two centres for clinical and survival analyses, with digital pathology analysis at one centre covering 743 patients (115 ILC, 628 IDC) and 758 whole-slide images, including 15 matched ILC primary-metastatic pairs. The authors compared clinicopathological characteristics, survival and metastatic sites with propensity-score matching, and developed exploratory clinical, digital pathology and combined elastic-net Cox models for recurrence or metastasis in 81 ILC patients, tested internally in 34. ILC was associated with poorer disease-free survival (adjusted HR 1.39, 95% CI 1.13-1.71) and overall survival (HR 1.56, 95% CI 1.27-1.92), with more frequent bone metastasis in ILC and lung metastasis in IDC; digital pathology captured differences in nuclear morphology and cellular spatial organisation between subtypes and between paired primary and metastatic lesions. In the internal test set, 60-month time-dependent AUCs were 0.710 for the clinical model, 0.746 for the digital pathology model and 0.822 for the combined model (95% CI 0.612-0.974), with a combined-model Uno C-index of 0.799, suggesting that adding quantitative tissue features to clinical variables may improve recurrence prediction, though the authors note the need for evaluation in larger independent cohorts.

fastrad: Complete, IBSI-Validated GPU Acceleration of the Full PyRadiomics Feature Set.

Radiomicsscore 9 Computer Methods and Programs in Biomedicine · IF≈7.42026-11-20E Sánchez-Femat, JM Celaya-Padilla, CE Galván-Tejada

PyRadiomics, the reference radiomics implementation, runs only on CPU, and existing GPU alternatives cover at most two of the eight standardised feature classes without reported numerical validation against PyRadiomics. Correctness was tested against the IBSI Phase 1 digital phantom (107 features, seven classes) and against PyRadiomics on six clinical CT cases from the TCIA NSCLC-Radiomics collection, with timing on an NVIDIA RTX 4070 and Intel Core i9-14900KF and scan-rescan reproducibility on 10 RIDER Lung CT pairs. The fastrad library implements all eight PyRadiomics-compatible feature classes in PyTorch on CPU or CUDA GPU, cropping images and masks to the ROI before computation. It met the IBSI criterion for all 107 features (maximum relative deviation 1.46×10-13%) and matched PyRadiomics on all 107 features across all six cases (largest absolute difference 3.2×10-9); on the primary case (56,637 voxels) extraction took 29.2 ms on GPU versus 1.83 s for 32-thread PyRadiomics, a 62-fold speedup, and cohort throughput reached 2020 scans/min on one GPU versus 468 scans/min for 32 CPU processes, with identical ICC values to seven decimal places. Exact shape agreement required computing the marching-cubes mesh in PyRadiomics' coordinate frame because the mesh is not always closed and open-mesh volume depends on the origin.

Deep dive (from abstract only)

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in radiomics: PyRadiomics, the de facto reference implementation, is CPU-only, and prior GPU alternatives cover at most two of the eight standardized feature classes and lack numerical validation against PyRadiomics. fastrad is a GPU-native Python library reimplementing all eight PyRadiomics-compatible feature classes in PyTorch, with ROI cropping before computation and execution on CPU or CUDA tensors.
