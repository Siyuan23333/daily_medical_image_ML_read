Deep Auxiliary Learning via Bilevel Feature Optimization for Label-Efficient Medical Image Segmentation.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging · IF 10.0journal list2026-11-24F Wu, X Jia, L Zhang et al.

The paper addresses the cost of expert annotation in medical image segmentation, specifically whether auxiliary learning can help when auxiliary task labels must be derived automatically rather than manually annotated. Evaluation uses three medical image segmentation benchmarks under limited-label settings; the abstract does not specify modalities, cohort sizes, sources, or whether the data are single- or multi-centre. The proposed framework formulates auxiliary learning as a bilevel optimization problem, with a feature communication module enabling knowledge transfer between auxiliary tasks and the primary segmentation task. Results show consistent improvements over state-of-the-art methods and better label efficiency, though the abstract gives no quantitative numbers. The contribution is the removal of additional manual labeling for auxiliary tasks by deriving their labels from existing segmentation annotations, which matters for label-scarce medical imaging settings.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via arxiv)

The paper addresses label-efficient medical image segmentation: can auxiliary tasks whose labels are derived for free from existing segmentation masks (boundary, signed distance, saliency, Canny edges) improve a primary segmentation task without extra annotation, extra modalities, or unlabeled data? The authors propose ALBO, a U-Net-shaped encoder built from bidirectional communication modules (BCM) in which main and auxiliary branches learn task-specific features and then exchange them, trained under a bilevel formulation that places segmentation at the upper level and the auxiliary task at the lower level. The lower-level update is a one-step look-ahead on the auxiliary loss; the resulting Hessian-vector product is approximated by central finite differences, adding two forward-backward passes per iteration. Evaluated on REFUGE (2D fundus, optic cup/disc), ACDC (2D cardiac MRI) and Hippocampus (3D MSD) with 10-20 labeled cases, ALBO outperforms Independent, Joint, GCS, AuxiLearn, AuxiNash, CAGrad, Nash, RLW and AMTL, e.g. REFUGE OC Dice 73.58% vs 67.54% (Independent) at 10 labels, and ACDC average Dice 79.48% vs 75.37%. Genuinely new: annotation-free auxiliary labels combined with an asymmetric bilevel objective and a bidirectional feature-exchange encoder, with demonstrated transfer to ViT/DINOv3-LoRA and Mamba backbones and complementarity with pseudo-labeling. The gains are real but concentrated in the very-low-label regime; the bilevel training costs ~5.6x per iteration.

Datasets

  • REFUGE 2018: 2D color fundus photographs, 400 train/400 val/400 test, public; 10/20/40/80/160/400 labeled subsets for training, fixed test set, 3 random seeds
  • ACDC: 2D short-axis cardiac MR cine, 200 cases, public; 5-fold CV, 160 training cases per fold with 10/20/40/80/160 labeled subsets, 40 validation cases
  • Hippocampus (Medical Segmentation Decathlon Task 04): 3D MRI, 260 scans, public; 5-fold CV, 208 training / 52 validation per fold, with 20-label subset
  • Comparison-only: BRBS, DSPNet, UPRENet few-shot/SSL baselines on REFUGE with 1/4/10 labels (plus 390-399 unlabeled images in SSL mode)
  • Comparison-only: PL, AdvSemiSeg, CPS, CrossTeaching, URPC, MERU SSL baselines on ACDC with 10 labeled + 150 unlabeled scans per fold

Method

  • Two-branch U-Net encoder of bidirectional communication modules (BCM): per-branch Conv Blocks, then cross-branch Conv Blocks fused by addition; separate main and auxiliary decoders
  • Bilevel optimization: upper level minimizes main segmentation loss over {theta_m, theta_a, psi_m}; lower level minimizes auxiliary loss over {theta_a, psi_a}; one look-ahead inner step on theta_a
  • Finite-difference approximation of the Hessian-vector product (two extra forward-backward passes, O(P) memory) instead of explicit second-order differentiation
  • Auxiliary labels auto-derived from ground-truth masks: boundary, signed distance map, Gaussian-smoothed saliency, Canny edges; boundary selected as default
  • SGD, lr alpha=beta=0.1 with 0.9 decay every 200 iterations, batch size 20, max 15,000 iterations, single A100; U-Net-S (128 bottom channels, ~0.5M params) for REFUGE, U-Net-L (1024 channels) for ACDC/Hippocampus
  • Baselines: Independent, Joint, GCS, AuxiLearn, AuxiNash (AL); CAGrad, Nash-MTL, RLW, AMTL (MTL); plus DINOv3 ViT-B/16 with LoRA and Vision-Mamba (VSS) backbones

Validation

  • Internal held-out test only: REFUGE fixed 400-image test set with 3 seeds; ACDC and Hippocampus 5-fold cross-validation; no external multi-site or prospective validation
  • Metrics: Dice, ASSD, HD95; paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test on per-image scores (p<0.05, p<0.01) and paired t-test in ablations
  • Ablation of BCM vs CCM and BLO vs Independent/Joint; auxiliary-task selection study (1-4 tasks); comparison with few-shot and SSL methods; computational cost analysis

Results

  • REFUGE, 10 labels: OC Dice 73.58% vs 67.54% (Independent+BCM) and 59.74-67.48% for other AL/MTL methods; OD Dice 82.42% vs 80.91%; all differences p<0.01
  • REFUGE, 400 labels: OD Dice 94.32% (best) and OD ASSD 6.38 px / HD95 15.44 px vs 7.07-7.86 px ASSD for competitors; OC Dice 82.30% second to GCS 83.39%
  • ACDC, 10 labels: RV/Myo/LV Dice 70.74/80.21/87.48% vs 67.92/74.97/83.22% (Independent); average Dice 79.48% vs 75.37%, all metrics p<0.01
  • ACDC, 160 labels: average Dice 91.47% vs 90.88% (Independent), comparable to CAGrad 91.58%; ASSD average 0.60 mm (best)
  • Hippocampus, 20 labels: HH/HB Dice 86.25/84.51% vs 85.02/82.81% (Independent); at 208 labels near-saturated, ALBO best HD95 on HH (1.38 mm) but several differences non-significant
  • Ablation (REFUGE, full data): removing BLO drops OC Dice 83.99->81.41% (distance task) and OD Dice 94.32->93.09% (boundary); BCM alone without BLO underperforms ALBO
  • SSL combination (ACDC, 10 labels + 150 unlabeled): PL+ALBO average Dice 81.69% (distance) and 80.23% (boundary) vs PL 76.59% and PL+Joint 77.98%/75.12%
  • Cost: 5.6x training time per iteration (3.81 s vs 0.68 s), 1.46x peak memory (9.9 vs 6.8 GB), 3.2x wall-clock to best model; identical inference latency (~14 ms) and 0.50M parameters

Limitations

  • Stated: bilevel training is ~5.6x slower per iteration and uses 46% more memory than single-level training (training-only overhead)
  • Stated: gains shrink with abundant labels; on near-saturated benchmarks (Hippocampus, full-data REFUGE/ACDC) ALBO is competitive but not uniformly best and many differences are non-significant
  • Stated: failures on small, low-contrast or atypical structures (optic cup Dice 73.58%, 10.4% of test images with OC Dice<0.5; abnormal RV; posterior hippocampus down to Dice 0.09); no cross-domain/site-shift evaluation
  • Omitted: no external or prospective validation; BCM benefit is dataset- and scale-dependent (hurts ACDC with small U-Net, overfits REFUGE with U-Net-L); only one auxiliary task used in main comparisons; 2D slice-wise cardiac model ignores through-plane context; finite-difference epsilon and look-ahead step size not systematically tuned

Availability

Code publicly available at https://github.com/FupingWu90/ALBO; all datasets are public (REFUGE, ACDC, MSD Hippocampus).

Generative fundus angiography from misaligned handheld images for non-invasive screening of infantile retinal diseases

Medical imaging MLscore 9 npj Digital Medicine · IF 15.1journal list2026-11-24Xie, Yingpeng, Zhao, Xinyu, Wu, Zhenquan et al.

Infantile retinal diseases cause childhood blindness, but the vascular findings needed for screening are poorly visible in color fundus photography (CFP), while invasive fluorescein angiography (FFA) is hard to use routinely in vulnerable infants. The work uses 83,950 registration-derived CFP-FFA image-pair records from 259 infants, acquired with handheld contact fundus cameras whose probe angle, contact pressure, and field of view vary between acquisitions, producing weak spatial correspondence; the abstract does not state the number of centres. The proposed InfantAngio is a diffusion-based framework that reformulates CFP-to-FFA synthesis as a deterministic latent transport trajectory. It performed favorably on quantitative image-quality benchmarks, and ophthalmologists distinguished real from synthesized FFA images at near-chance accuracy of 49.8% in blinded assessment, while adding synthesized FFA to CFP improved downstream classifier performance and diagnostic accuracy in a retrospective reader study. The contribution is a non-invasive angiographic approximation that may serve as an adjunct for screening high-risk infants and reduce reliance on dye-based procedures.

Deformable image registration methods based on neural networks struggle with highly deformed structures and complex motions such as sliding or respiratory dynamics, because isotropic diffusive pairwise regularization causes inefficient message passing and oversmoothed boundaries. The evaluation uses four realistic datasets and two synthetic datasets spanning different modalities and input constraints, though the abstract does not specify cohort sizes, sources, or whether the data are single- or multi-centre. The proposed VoxelSlicer is a neural network module inspired by the traditional bilateral grid, augmented with learnable adaptive filtering to perform voxel-wise adaptive filtering and more efficient message passing for adaptive displacement-cost propagation. This paper addresses deformable image registration (DIR), the task of estimating dense displacement fields that align anatomical structures across images, with emphasis on clinically hard cases such as inter-subject abdominal scans and intra-subject cardiac sequences where large deformations, sliding interfaces, and respiratory motion violate smoothness assumptions. The authors argue that prevailing learning-based DIR networks rely on isotropic, diffusive pairwise regularization, which propagates information inefficiently and oversmooths displacement boundaries at sliding or discontinuous tissue interfaces. Their proposed remedy, VoxelSlicer, is a neural module inspired by the classical bilateral grid but augmented with learnable adaptive filtering, enabling voxel-wise adaptive filtering and more efficient message passing so that displacement-cost information propagates selectively rather than diffusively. The abstract reports experiments on four realistic datasets and two synthetic datasets spanning different modalities and input constraints, with competitive registration performance and favorable accuracy-complexity and accuracy-smoothness trade-offs, plus a compact parameter footprint. Whether this materially improves sliding-interface and respiratory-motion registration over strong baselines remains to be confirmed from the full text.

CTLink: A Text-Prompted Segmentation Model for Linking Radiology Report Findings to 3D Chest CT Scans

Medical imaging MLscore 10 arXiv · preprint2026-11-23Mohammed Baharoon, Sung Eun Kim, Mahmoud Hussain Alabbad et al.

Radiology reports describe chest CT findings in text without marking their location on the scan, so readers cannot see which abnormality each finding refers to. The model was trained on 4123 noncontrast chest CT scans with 9547 segmented report findings, including the newly annotated ReXGroundingCTv2 dataset of 1134 scans and 1895 findings in which every instance of each finding is segmented; the abstract does not state whether the data are single- or multi-centre. CTLink is a text-prompted 3D segmentation model that segments findings described in a chest CT report, and an ensemble of seven CTLink models was evaluated on the MICCAI 2026 ReXGrounding challenge test set. On the 200 ReXGroundingCT validation scans, CTLink achieved a hit rate of 0.816, instance F1 of 0.456, and mean Dice of 0.400, versus 0.533, 0.175, and 0.234 for VoxTell (P < .001 for all), and on the challenge test set the seven-model ensemble had the highest hit rate (0.782) and instance F1 (0.386) among 48 teams. On 50 test scans segmented twice by independent radiologists, CTLink matched or exceeded inter-rater agreement, with diffuse non-focal findings the main limitation; model weights and the dataset are publicly released.

PDFarXivSummary
Deep dive (from full text via arxiv-pdf)

CTLink addresses the problem that radiology reports describe chest CT findings in free text without marking them on the scan, so referring clinicians must search for each described abnormality. The authors build a text-prompted 3D segmentation model that takes a single report sentence as prompt and outputs a binary mask of the described finding in the native CT voxel grid. The architecture is identical to VoxTell v1.1 (3D U-Net residual encoder plus transformer prompt decoder with a frozen Qwen3-Embedding-4B sentence encoder), fine-tuned from VoxTell weights on 4123 noncontrast chest CT scans and 9547 findings, including a new exhaustively annotated set, ReXGroundingCTv2 (1134 scans, 1895 findings, every instance segmented). On the 200-scan ReXGroundingCT validation set, CTLink reaches hit rate 0.816, instance F1 0.456, and mean Dice 0.400 versus 0.533/0.175/0.234 for VoxTell (P<.001). On the 300-scan MICCAI 2026 ReXGrounding challenge test set, a seven-model ensemble achieves the best hit rate (0.782) and instance F1 (0.386) among 48 teams. On 50 test scans segmented twice by radiologists, CTLink matches or exceeds inter-rater agreement. The genuinely new contributions are the exhaustive annotation dataset and the empirical demonstration that data quality/quantity, not architecture or training tricks, drives the gains; the model itself is a fine-tuned VoxTell with post-processing.

Datasets

  • CT-RATE (source of all scans): noncontrast chest CT + reports, single center (Istanbul Medipol), public; underlying pool for all splits
  • ReXGroundingCT v1 training set (corrected masks by challenge Team 4): 2989 scans, 7652 findings, public, training
  • ReXGroundingCTv2 training set (new): 1134 scans, 1895 findings, exhaustively annotated, public, training
  • ReXGrounding challenge validation set: 200 scans, 381 findings, public, ablation/model selection
  • ReXGrounding challenge test set: 300 scans, 582 findings, public reference masks, challenge comparison; 50 scans with second radiologist segmentation for inter-rater study

Method

  • VoxTell v1.1 architecture: residual-encoder 3D U-Net image encoder + transformer prompt decoder; frozen Qwen3-Embedding-4B sentence encoder; text features fused with decoder features at every stage
  • One scan–finding pair per sample; random 192^3 crops (50% centered on finding); Dice + BCE loss with deep supervision; AdamW, lr 1e-5, 500-iter warm-up, cosine decay, 50k iterations, batch size 2; fine-tuned from VoxTell weights
  • Ablation variants: v2 oversampling (3x), larger 192x256x256 crops, longer training (75k iters, effective batch 4)
  • Post-processing: lobe restriction via dilated TotalSegmentator lobe masks, higher threshold (0.9) for non-focal findings, emphysema gating at -910 HU, removal of components <5% of largest
  • Seven-model ensemble (5 seeds + 2 variants) with probability averaging and adjusted thresholds (0.35/0.8); baselines: published VoxTell, 48 challenge teams

Validation

  • Internal validation on 200 ReXGrounding challenge validation scans (ablation); external-style comparison on 300 held-out challenge test scans; no prospective evaluation
  • Metrics from official challenge evaluator: hit rate (Dice>=0.1), instance F1 (one-to-one component matching at Dice>=0.2), mean per-scan Dice
  • 95% CIs from 2000 bootstrap replicates resampling scans; two-sided paired bootstrap tests, P<.05; inter-rater comparison on 50 scans/96 findings; LLM-based phrasing robustness analysis

Results

  • Validation: hit rate 0.816, instance F1 0.456, Dice 0.400 vs VoxTell 0.533/0.175/0.234 (P<.001)
  • Challenge test: ensemble hit rate 0.782, instance F1 0.386, Dice 0.373; best hit rate and instance F1 of 48 teams; single CTLink instance F1 0.378 vs winner 0.341 (P=.005)
  • Ablation: fine-tuning on corrected v1 masks raised hit rate 0.533->0.761; adding v2 ->0.793 (P=.03) and Dice 0.353->0.388 (P<.001); post-processing raised instance F1 0.373->0.456 (P<.001)
  • Inter-rater (96 findings): CTLink Dice 0.374 vs second radiologist 0.321 (P=.02); ensemble hit rate 0.854 vs 0.760 (P=.045)
  • Category performance: focal hit rate 0.73 (atelectasis/consolidation) to 0.84 (linear opacities); non-focal 0.33 (septal thickening) to 0.60; focal >=10 mL hit rate 0.87

Limitations

  • Non-focal/diffuse findings remain hardest, partly due to inconsistent radiologist outlines (second reader hit rate only 0.41 for non-focal vs 0.84 focal)
  • Single-center CT-RATE data, noncontrast only; generalization to contrast-enhanced scans, other scanners and populations unknown
  • Single annotator per reference mask; radiologist comparison limited to 50 scans/96 findings; only lungs, airways, and pleura covered
  • Post-processing thresholds and ensemble members were tuned on validation and the public half of the test set, a potential optimistic bias not fully addressed

Availability

Code at github.com/rajpurkarlab/CTLink, weights at huggingface.co/rajpurkarlab/CTLink, ReXGroundingCT/v2 annotations at huggingface.co/datasets/rajpurkarlab/ReXGroundingCT (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0); scans via CT-RATE.

Reliable and grounded reporting of any computed tomography scan with FORA

Medical imaging MLscore 10 arXiv · preprint2026-11-24Sezgin Er, Utku Turkbey, Alperen Gur et al.

AI systems for CT are typically limited to specific anatomical regions, exam types, or predefined abnormalities, restricting comprehensive and spatially grounded interpretation. FORA was trained on 304,962 routine CT examinations supervised by their accompanying reports (cohort source and single- versus multi-centre status not stated in the abstract) and evaluated on 36,619 held-out CT examinations across 28 CT protocols and 12 examination types. The method combines multiple-instance learning with an interpretable concept bottleneck linking spatial evidence, 1,028 hierarchically organized radiological concepts, and report generation, learning localization without manually annotated lesions. On held-out CT, FORA reached a macro AUROC of 0.918 for concept recognition, exceeding the strongest of 18 existing CT models by 0.21, ranked first on three public benchmarks (AUROC 0.907 versus 0.826 for Merlin on Merlin's own test set without training on that institution's data), and its strongest spatial evidence fell within annotated lesions at rates up to 348 times greater than chance on pulmonary embolus, renal mass, and rib fracture localization benchmarks, with more than threefold higher macro-averaged finding recall than the strongest existing report generator. The work indicates that a single model can combine broad abnormality recognition, spatial localization, and report generation across CT examination types using only routine reports as supervision.

PDFarXivSummary
Deep dive (from full text via arxiv-pdf)

FORA addresses the problem that CT AI systems are typically narrow—trained for specific organs, examination types, or predefined abnormalities—and that report generators can produce plausible text without guaranteeing that findings are recognized or spatially supported. The authors propose a single general-purpose CT model that predicts 1,028 hierarchically organized radiological concepts (derived from Radiopaedia and report mining), localizes each concept via attention-based multiple-instance learning, and generates reports conditioned on the predicted concepts, their spatial evidence, and visual tokens. Supervision comes only from routine reports (304,962 training examinations; 36,619 held-out), with no lesion-level annotations. Headline results: macro AUROC 0.918 for concept recognition versus 0.705 for the strongest of 18 comparison models; first place on three public benchmarks (Merlin test set AUROC 0.907 vs 0.826 for Merlin itself, despite no training on that institution's data); top-focus localization hit rates 58.3–72.8% on independently annotated lesions (up to 348× chance); and >3× higher macro finding recall than the strongest of seven report generators. The genuinely new contribution is the combination—a concept bottleneck with MIL-derived spatial evidence used both as an interpretable intermediate representation and as input to report generation—trained at scale from reports alone. The architecture was first validated on chest radiographs. The work is methodologically careful but remains retrospective and single-network in training data; clinical utility is not yet demonstrated.

Datasets

  • FORA internal CT cohort: 341,581 examinations / 238,469 patients, one hospital network, 28 protocols; private; 304,962 train, 36,619 held-out test (patient-level split)
  • FORA chest radiograph cohort: 286,560 studies; private; 257,595 train, 28,965 validation
  • Merlin test set: 5,125 CT studies, 30 findings; public; external test (different institution)
  • CT-RATE: 1,564 validation studies, 18 findings; public; external test (same hospital network, patients excluded from training)
  • RAD-ChestCT: 3,630 chest CT scans, 16 findings; public; external test (different institution)
  • FUMPE: 35 CT angiography scans (33 with emboli); public; localization test only
  • KiTS23: 489 scans with renal masses (248 with cysts); public; localization test only
  • RibFrac: 80 scans (60 with fractures); public; localization test only
  • ReXGroundingCT: 414 CT-RATE validation studies, 1,139 annotated instances, 11 categories; public; localization test only
  • Chest radiograph public tests: RSNA Pneumonia, Open-I, ChestX-Det10, ChestX-ray14, PadChest-GR; public; external test

Method

  • 3D ViT-L image encoder (24 blocks, 1,024-dim, 302.9M params, 2×16×16-voxel tokens) trained from scratch by self-supervised distillation (DINO/iBOT/KoLeo + Gram anchoring) across multiple HU windows and lung MIPs
  • Concept bottleneck: 1,028 report-derived concepts predicted by gated attention MIL over all image tokens, with organ routing (63 CADS-derived regions) via learned attention penalties; per-concept Platt scaling and Youden thresholds fitted on training data only
  • Spatial evidence = attention-weighted token contribution to the study logit; foci from connected components of the 50%-mass evidence region
  • Report generation: Gemma-4-31B with LoRA (rank 16) conditioned on a finding plan (calibrated probabilities, evidence foci, measurements), 512 resampled visual tokens, and zoomed evidence images; SFT then DPO (β=0.1) on preference pairs
  • Baselines: 18 released CT models (CoLiPri, CT-CLIP, Merlin, RADAR, Pillar-0, NV-Reason-CT, CT-CHAT, RadFM, Med3DVLM, MedGemma, etc.) and 7 report generators, all evaluated with released weights/prompts without retraining

Validation

  • Retrospective internal held-out test (patient-level split) plus external public benchmarks (Merlin, CT-RATE, RAD-ChestCT) and independent localization datasets; no prospective or reader study
  • Metrics: macro AUROC, AUPRC, balanced accuracy, CRG, LLM-judge score, top-focus hit rate, evidence overlap; 95% CIs from patient-level bootstrap/linearization
  • Two-sided paired tests with Holm correction; calibration assessed via reliability curves and expected calibration error

Results

  • Concept recognition: macro AUROC 0.918 (95% CI 0.916–0.919) vs 0.705 for CoLiPri; AUPRC 0.393 vs 0.146; balanced accuracy 0.837 vs 0.630; CRG 0.705 vs 0.431 (all P<0.001)
  • Higher average precision in 2,040/2,132 concept–examination-type pairs (95.7%); positive median AUROC advantage in all 12 CT types (0.07 chest to 0.21 sinus/orbit)
  • External benchmarks: Merlin test AUROC 0.907 vs 0.826 (Merlin); RAD-ChestCT 0.816 vs 0.796; CT-RATE 0.891 vs 0.871 (NV-Reason-CT)
  • Localization top-focus hit rates: 69.7% pulmonary emboli (FUMPE, ~348× chance), 72.8% renal masses and 66.1% renal cysts (KiTS23), 58.3% rib fractures (RibFrac), 27.6% ReXGroundingCT (~33× chance); 80%-evidence overlap 80.8–97.0%
  • Report generation: macro recall 0.215 vs 0.066 (NV-Reason-CT), precision 0.285 vs 0.132, CRG 0.467 vs 0.387, judge score 0.570 vs 0.405; judge preferred FORA in 73.8% of pairwise comparisons

Limitations

  • Single hospital network for training, reports translated from Turkish to English; generalization to other populations, protocols and reporting practices unverified
  • Concept labels inherit report omissions, uncertainty and institutional conventions; unmentioned findings treated as negative, which is not equivalent to absence
  • Localization validated only for concepts with independent annotations; attention-based evidence is not causal (no occlusion/interventional tests)
