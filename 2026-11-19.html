Confocal microscopy segmentation via prototype-driven semi-supervised learning.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Medical Image Analysis · IF 11.8journal list2026-11-16H Li, W Li, Y Liu et al.

Segmenting corneal nerves and Langerhans cells in in vivo corneal confocal microscopy is important for diagnosing ocular and systemic diseases, but deep learning translation is limited by domain shift and scarce labeled data across multi-center cohorts. The work evaluates seven datasets totaling 10,987 images spanning 22 diseases, described as the first multi-center, multi-disease dataset with high-quality annotations, and also tests on retinal optical coherence tomography angiography. The proposed Semi-NC framework is semi-supervised, cycling through multi-scale prototype feature extraction, feature amplification regularization, category-specific prototype pool construction and updating, and contrastive pixel-to-prototype alignment to learn domain-invariant representations from labeled and unlabeled data. The abstract reports that Semi-NC outperforms fully-supervised baselines with minimal labels and shows strong in-distribution and out-of-distribution generalization plus self-evolution on unseen data, though it gives no specific quantitative metrics. Its contribution is a prototype-based semi-supervised approach with cross-modality validation and a planned open-source multi-center dataset and code.

DOISummary
Deep dive (from full text via elsevier)

This study asks whether a state-of-the-art meningioma segmentation model (DeSURVAE, originally built for the BraTS 2025 Lighthouse challenge) remains accurate under realistic multi-center, multi-scanner conditions, and which factors drive its failures. The authors pooled 2317 contrast-enhanced T1-weighted MRI scans (3269 tumors) from Auckland Hospitals (817 scans, 21 scanner configurations, 1.5T/3T), BraTS 2023 pre-operative (1000 multimodal cases, T1W+C only, ET+NETC merged into tumor core) and BraTS 2024 radiotherapy (500 T1W+C cases, GTV labels). DeSURVAE is a hybrid dual-encoder (residual CNN + Swin transformer) dual-decoder network with a VAE-regularized bottleneck, trained from scratch in four scenarios: single-cohort, pooled fusion, cross-cohort transfer, and cross-fold transfer, against SegResNet and Swin UNETR baselines. Headline results: fusion training gave the best and most stable performance (0.81±0.23 DSC, 29.51±79.24 mm HD95 pooled), DeSURVAE beat SegResNet by up to 0.05 DSC/7.4 mm HD95 and Swin UNETR by up to 0.07 DSC/16.7 mm HD95, and BraTS 2024 was consistently the hardest domain. Tumor volume was the dominant performance driver (rs=0.57-0.63; volume-quintile beta 0.4-0.6), with location and scanner effects secondary. Genuinely new contributions are the scale of the multi-center confounder analysis, the finding that a 65% (not 50%) sigmoid threshold is optimal, the demonstration that DeSURVAE reaches 0.80 DSC for pre-operative tumors as small as 0.4 cm3 (versus 2-3 cm3 in prior literature), and the tumor-scale-matched 7x6x8 spatial grid for location analysis. The architecture itself is not new; the value is in the rigorous robustness/confounder characterization.

It retrospectively analyzed 879 participants from two centres (741 with locally advanced colorectal cancer, 138 with benign lesions), using pretreatment contrast-enhanced CT and clinical data, with training at centre A (n=615) and independent testing at centre B (n=264). Multimodal multitask deep learning for predicting tertiary lymphoid structures and peritoneal recurrence in gastric cancer: a multicentre development, validation, and testing study.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 The Lancet Digital Health · IF 24.1journal list2026-11-19Z Li, T Zhang, W Wang et al.

Histological assessment of tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) in gastric cancer is invasive and biased, limiting patient stratification, and peritoneal recurrence remains a common fatal complication after curative surgery. The study used retrospective data from eight Chinese cohorts (one training, two internal validation, three external validation, and two immunotherapy cohorts of patients on anti-PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors) from four hospitals, with TLS status classified as TLS0/TLS1/TLS2 by haematoxylin-eosin and immunohistochemistry staining. CTransNet, a multitask hybrid model combining a ResNet50 CNN and a Transformer, predicted patient-level TLS status and peritoneal recurrence-free survival from radiological data. AUCs for TLS classification were 0.910 (95% CI 0.882-0.938) for TLS0, 0.925 (0.899-0.951) for TLS1, and 0.953 (0.933-0.974) for TLS2, with a training-cohort concordance index of 0.654 (0.620-0.688) for peritoneal recurrence-free survival; low-score patients had higher 5-year peritoneal recurrence (66.69% vs 26.28%, p<0.0001), and high-score patients showed higher immunotherapy objective response rates (54.5% vs 5.6% and 53.1% vs 9.7%). The work links non-invasive imaging prediction of TLS maturation to recurrence and treatment response, suggesting a radiological route to stratifying gastric cancer patients for adjuvant chemotherapy and immunotherapy.

Deep dive (from full text via elsevier)

This multicentre retrospective study addresses a clinically important gap: tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) are prognostic and predictive of immunotherapy response in gastric cancer, but are assessed only by invasive histology with sampling bias. The authors propose CTransNet, a multitask CNN–Transformer model that fuses contrast-enhanced abdominal CT, PyRadiomics features, and clinical variables to jointly classify patient-level TLS status (TLS0 absent, TLS1 primary follicle-like, TLS2 secondary follicle-like with germinal centres) and predict peritoneal recurrence-free survival (PRFS). Development used 400 patients from Nanfang Hospital (2005–2011), with two internal validation cohorts, three Chinese external cohorts (SYSUCC with and without TLS labels, YNCH), two non-Asian cohorts (Stanford, TCGA), and two immunotherapy cohorts (n=356). TLS classification reached AUCs of 0.910/0.925/0.953 for TLS0/1/2 in training, with roughly 78–80% correct classification across cohorts. PRFS C-indices were modest (0.633–0.715) but consistent, and model score remained an independent prognostic factor after adjustment. High scores were associated with higher immunotherapy objective response (54.5% vs 5.6%) and outperformed combined positive score (AUC 0.769 vs 0.593). Genuinely new elements are the joint TLS-classification/survival multitask formulation, the CNN–Transformer fusion of imaging, radiomics and clinical data, and the demonstration that multitask learning substantially improves TLS accuracy over single-task models (0.782–0.795 vs 0.367–0.625). The prognostic discrimination itself is only moderate, and the treatment-benefit analyses are associative post-hoc comparisons, so the clinical claims should be read as hypothesis-generating rather than practice-changing.
