Comprehensive multi-organ and body composition phenotyping identifies metabolic signatures associated with incident type 2 diabetes

Medical imaging MLscore 9 European Radiology · IF 4.7journal list2026-11-11Kim, Songsoo, Kim, Dong Won, Kim, Sungjun et al.

Clinical risk scores do not capture the multi-organ pathophysiology underlying type 2 diabetes, so the authors asked whether whole-body MRI organ size and fat quantification improve prediction of incident disease. They analysed 46,129 UK Biobank participants without diabetes at imaging, using Dixon MRI with automated segmentation to extract organ volume indices and fat fractions, and trained a deep learning model on independently selected biomarkers from adjusted Cox models. During follow-up 600 participants developed T2DM; eight biomarkers independently predicted it, including liver volume index (HR 1.28, 95% CI 1.19-1.38), pancreas fat fraction (HR 1.19, 95% CI 1.10-1.28) and gluteus fat fraction (HR 1.17, 95% CI 1.08-1.27), and gluteal intermuscular adipose tissue grades were higher in weak-for-volume participants than strength-matched controls (median 2.0 vs 1.0, p=0.02). The deep learning model reached a concordance index of 0.814 (95% CI 0.797-0.832) and, applied sequentially with clinical criteria, reduced false-positive classifications by 43.0% while preserving 97.3% of true positives. The work indicates that muscle fat fraction may better reflect metabolic risk than muscle volume in IMAT-rich regions, and that automated whole-body MRI phenotyping adds predictive value beyond an established clinical risk score.

Evaluation of a Rapid and Practical Deep Learning Framework for T1-weighted MRI-based Brain Age Estimation.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Radiology: Artificial Intelligence · IF 20.1journal list2026-11-11HW Kim, HW Roh, CH Hong et al.

Brain-age estimation from T1-weighted MRI requires models that are both accurate and practical, and the clinical meaning of the brain-age gap (BAG) needs validation. The study used T1-weighted MR images from 4,346 cognitively normal adults for training, validation, and internal testing, plus two external test cohorts (n = 315 and 209) and a clinical cohort (BICWALZS, n = 954) spanning cognitively normal individuals and various dementias, drawn from multiple cohorts. Preprocessing relied only on freely available Python tools, and a deep learning model was trained to predict chronological age, with residualized BAG then related to diagnosis, amyloid PET positivity, white matter hyperintensity severity, stroke history, and longitudinal worsening using ANCOVA, logistic regression, and Cox models. Age prediction reached R2 = 0.95 and MAE 3.76 years internally and R2 = 0.69-0.90 with MAE 4.82-5.15 years externally; BAG increased across diagnostic groups (P < .001), was associated with WMH severity (OR 1.64) and stroke history (OR 1.46), was not associated with amyloid positivity after full adjustment (P = .17), and predicted longitudinal clinical worsening (HR 1.92). The work is notable for a low-resource, freely available pipeline and for linking BAG to cerebrovascular burden and clinical progression rather than amyloid status.

Memory-guided mutual information optimization in retinal imaging for early stroke detection and subtyping.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Medical Image Analysis · IF 11.8journal list2026-11-09S Zhou, L Cao, L Mou et al.

Scalable tools for identifying individuals at risk of ischemic cerebrovascular disease (ICVD) before neurological symptoms appear are lacking, limiting effective stroke prevention. The study used retinal Optical Coherence Tomography Angiography (OCTA) images from a multi-center cohort of 24,438 images to address auxiliary ICVD detection, etiological subtyping (large artery atherosclerosis vs. small vessel disease), and symptomatic-status classification (asymptomatic vs. symptomatic LAA). The RetCEVD framework introduces a memory-guided mutual information optimization strategy that maximizes shared disease-relevant information within a diagnostic category while suppressing redundant or confounding dependencies across categories, yielding compact discriminative representations of subtle retinal microvascular abnormalities. Reported AUCs were 0.95 internal and 0.94 external for ICVD detection, 0.83 internal and 0.81 external for etiological subtyping, 0.75 internal and 0.76 external for symptomatic-status classification, and 0.71 for two-year ALAA follow-up predicting subsequent stroke events. The work matters because it evaluates clinical interpretability, including learned pathological features and alignment with established clinical patterns, and positions retina-based OCTA as a low-cost screening and triage tool for directing patients to confirmatory neurovascular imaging.

Normalized membrane ratio of TROP2 as a predictor of clinical outcomes with datopotamab deruxtecan in non-small-cell lung cancer

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Nature Medicine · IF 50.0journal list2026-11-11Garassino, Marina C., Ahn, Myung-Ju, Schick, Markus et al.

Conventional pathologist-scored TROP2 immunohistochemistry does not predict response to TROP2-directed antibody-drug conjugates in NSCLC, so a better biomarker is needed. The authors developed TROP2 quantitative continuous scoring, a supervised computational pathology method that measures TROP2 in individual tumor cells and subcellular compartments on IHC slides, and derived a normalized membrane ratio (membrane relative to membrane plus cytoplasmic TROP2); the cut-point was set in the biomarker-evaluable population of TROPION-PanTumor01 (n = 115) and validated in the biomarker-evaluable population of TROPION-Lung01 (n = 352), focusing on 221 patients with nonsquamous NSCLC without actionable genomic alterations. In Dato-DXd-treated patients, progression-free survival was longer for NMR-positive versus NMR-negative samples (median 7.2 versus 4.0 months; HR 0.40, 95% CI 0.25-0.64), with no such difference in docetaxel-treated patients (median 4.1 versus 4.4 months; HR 0.94, 95% CI 0.59-1.49; treatment-by-biomarker interaction P = 0.0098). The work indicates that subcellular localization of TROP2, captured by computational quantification, may serve as a predictive biomarker for Dato-DXd where conventional expression scoring does not.

Deep dive (from full text via springer-oa-api)

This paper addresses a concrete clinical problem: TROP2-directed antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs) such as datopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd) show only modest, all-comer benefit in pretreated NSCLC, and conventional pathologist-scored TROP2 immunohistochemistry (H-score) has repeatedly failed to predict response. The authors apply a supervised computational pathology pipeline (quantitative continuous scoring, QCS) to whole-slide TROP2 IHC images, using two cascaded deep networks—one segmenting tumor epithelium (DualScaleNet) and one segmenting each tumor cell into membrane, cytoplasm and nucleus (DeepLabV3+)—to derive single-cell staining intensities. From seven feature classes, a normalized membrane ratio (NMR = membrane/cytoplasm intensity) emerged as the strongest correlate of outcome: lower NMR (relatively more cytoplasmic TROP2) predicted benefit. Discovery in the phase 1 TROPION-PanTumor01 cohort (n=115) gave median PFS 5.5 vs 2.9 months (HR 0.39) and ORR 22.6% vs 3.2%. The signature was then locked and optimized in the phase 3 TROPION-Lung01 nonsquamous, non-AGA subgroup (n=221), yielding a Q75/≤0.56 rule: PFS 7.2 vs 4.0 months (HR 0.40), OS 17.0 vs 10.8 months (HR 0.45), with significant treatment-by-biomarker interaction and no such separation under docetaxel. Preclinical CRISPR isogenic cell lines and a 15-line panel link low NMR to datopotamab internalization and Dato-DXd sensitivity. What is genuinely new is a mechanistically motivated, subcellular-localization biomarker derived from routine IHC, with interaction-based predictivity testing, replicate-slide reproducibility and a regulatory device pathway—rather than another expression-level score.

  • TROPION-Lung01 (NCT04656652), phase 3, Dato-DXd vs docetaxel, pretreated advanced/metastatic NSCLC; TROP2 IHC FLA slides, DP600 scans; full BEP n=352 (Dato-DXd 172, docetaxel 180), NSQ non-AGA focus n=221 (108 vs 113); retrospective optimization/validation set

  • Preclinical: NCI-H322 isogenic TROP2 knockout/overexpression lines and xenografts (n=8 per arm); NSCLC cell-line panel (n=15 for QCS/cytotoxicity, up to 22 for binding/internalization); cell pellets and FFPE blocks

Method

  • QCS computational pathology: cascaded fully supervised deep learning—ART (DualScaleNet, EfficientNet/ResNet dual-resolution) for tumor vs nontumor region segmentation, then SSTC (DeepLabV3+ with EfficientNet) for single-cell membrane/cytoplasm/nucleus segmentation with seeded watershed instance recovery
  • Biomarker = TROP2 NMR (membrane/cytoplasm staining-intensity ratio), summarized per sample by a quantile (Q75) and cut-point (≤0.56); positivity if ≥75% of tumor cells have NMR ≤0.56

  • Comparators: pathologist H-score/visual TROP2 scoring, membrane-only and cytoplasm-only QCS features, docetaxel arm, and non-Dato-DXd first-line trials

Validation

  • Retrospective: discovery in phase 1, optimization and internal validation in phase 3 TROPION-Lung01; no prospective validation yet (ongoing phase 3 trials cited)
  • Predictivity assessed via Cox treatment-by-biomarker interaction terms (Wald tests) for PFS/OS plus ORR; bootstrap CIs, cross-validated Kaplan–Meier, and adjacent-slide replicate reproducibility
  • Specificity checks in NEPTUNE/POSEIDON (no PFS separation by NMR status) and in the docetaxel arm; preclinical cell-line and xenograft validation

  • TROPION-PanTumor01: PFS 5.5 vs 2.9 months, HR 0.39 (95% CI 0.22–0.68), P=0.00054; ORR 22.6% vs 3.2%; 73% NMR+

The COSAS 2024 challenge: Cross-Organ and Cross-Scanner Adenocarcinoma Segmentation for domain generalization in computational pathology.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Medical Image Analysis · IF 11.8journal list2026-11-09B Meng, Q Da, X Long et al.

Automated histopathology segmentation models degrade under domain shifts caused by organ-specific morphology and scanner variability, and the COSAS 2024 challenge was organized to study these two sources separately through cross-organ and cross-scanner adenocarcinoma segmentation tasks. The benchmark consists of 580 pixel-level annotated histopathology patches covering six organ sites and six scanner platforms, with 30 international teams submitting valid solutions; the abstract does not state the cohort source or whether data came from single or multiple centres. Submissions used convolutional, transformer-based, and hybrid architectures with varied training, augmentation, and inference strategies, and were compared against organizer-run conventional segmentation networks and foundation-model baselines. Performance varied substantially across domains and between the two tasks, the relative strengths of reference models were task dependent with no model family uniformly strongest, and leading submissions reached competitive performance through different combinations of augmentation, multi-domain training, model design, and inference-time refinement. The challenge provides a controlled benchmark for cross-domain adenocarcinoma segmentation and practical guidance on designing AI systems that tolerate biological and technical variability in diagnostic workflows.
