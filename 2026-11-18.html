Bone Phenotype by HR-pQCT Relates to Risk of Skeletal Complications After Spinal Fusion Surgery.

Bone and skeletalscore 9 JBMR · IF 5.9journal list2026-11-18AS Dash, M Wood, A Jepson et al.

The problem is that DXA has limited ability to detect bone quality deficits relevant to post-operative skeletal complications in spine fusion patients, despite osteoporosis being a known risk factor. The data come from a prospective cohort of 167 spine fusion patients (mean age 63±11 years, 54% female) with normal mean DXA Z-scores, imaged pre-operatively by HR-pQCT (XtremeCT II) at the distal radius and tibia; the abstract does not state whether the cohort was single- or multi-centre. Patients were classified into three previously characterized microarchitecture phenotypes (Low Density n=42, Low Volume n=44, Healthy n=81) and followed for a median of 42 months for skeletal complications. Complications occurred in 40% overall, and 60% of Low Density patients had complications versus 30% of Low Volume and 37% of Healthy; Low Density carried higher risk than Low Volume (HR 2.46, 95% CI 1.26-4.82, p=0.009) and a trend versus Healthy (HR 1.66, 95% CI 0.97-2.82, p=0.06), with unadjusted HR 1.90 (95% CI 1.16-3.12, p=0.01) and sex-adjusted HR 2.18 (95% CI 1.29-3.69, p=0.004) versus the combined Low Volume and Healthy groups. The authors describe these as the first findings relating HR-pQCT microarchitectural phenotype to post-operative outcomes, suggesting pre-operative phenotyping may improve identification of high-risk fusion candidates.

An accessible efficient intervertebral disc vision-language model training strategy with LLM-based retrieval method for spine MRI report generation.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 European Journal of Radiology · IF≈3.72026-11-05X Chen, P Wang, Y Wang et al.

Automated radiology report generation from lumbar spine MRI is constrained by limited data and the high cost of applying multimodal large language models to spinal reporting. The study used lumbar spine MRI from 1125 patient samples, focusing on the axial plane of a single intervertebral disc and adjacent vertebrae, with disc-corresponding images and reports constructed via an LLM-based data processing pipeline; the number of centres is not stated in the abstract. Mature multimodal LLMs were fine-tuned under these data and hardware constraints, and the strategy was evaluated with NLP metrics (BLEU 0.24, ROUGE-L 0.53, CIDEr 1.28) and on clinical pathological classification, with F1 scores of 0.93 for disc pathology, 0.81 for canal stenosis, and 0.91 for dehydrated disc. Diagnostic performance was comparable to experienced radiologists for canal stenosis and dehydrated disc, and the LLM-based data evaluation pipeline was reported as stable and reliable. The contribution is a low-cost, disc-level training and data-construction strategy for spine MRI report generation, with the authors noting room for improvement in data volume and training scale.

Prediction of Postoperative Neurological Function Recovery in Cervical Spondylotic Myelopathy Using MRI-based Delta-Radiomics Combined with Interpretable Machine Learning Model.

Radiomicsscore 9late · published 2026-11-17 Journal of Imaging Informatics in Medicine · IF≈3.92026-11-17S Zhao, X Gao, Y Zhang et al.

Postoperative neurological recovery in cervical spondylotic myelopathy is highly variable, and existing prognostic methods have limitations. The study retrospectively enrolled 237 patients from Center 1 (split 165 training / 72 internal test) and 96 patients from Center 2 as an independent external validation set, using pre- and postoperative T2WI MRI. Radiomic and delta-radiomic features were extracted and screened with LASSO and Spearman correlation, and three models were built across five machine learning algorithms, with the XGBoost-based delta-radiomic model achieving the highest AUCs of 0.899 (internal test) and 0.802 (external validation), significantly better than the preoperative radiomic and clinical-routine imaging models. Decision curve analysis showed higher net benefit across wide threshold ranges, and SHAP identified wavelet-domain texture features as risk factors and gradient-domain kurtosis features as having bidirectional effects. The approach is notable for using routine short-term postoperative MRI (within 7 days) to predict 12-month neurological recovery in a dual-center, externally validated, interpretable model.

Preoperative prediction of HER2 status in breast cancer using multiparametric MRI deep learning and clinical features.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Magnetic Resonance Imaging · IF≈2.42026-11-18W Wang, C Li, J Yang et al.

Preoperative HER2 status in breast cancer is currently determined by core needle biopsy and pathology, so noninvasive prediction from imaging would be clinically useful. The retrospective single-centre study used 257 pathologically confirmed breast cancer patients (98 HER2-positive, 159 HER2-negative) with preoperative T1WI, T2WI, DWI and DCE-MRI, split 7:3 into training (n=174) and internal test (n=83) cohorts. Clinical, radiomics and deep learning models were built, then combined into an mpMRI model fusing radiomics and deep learning features across all four sequences, and an mpMRI_Cli model adding significant clinical features. In the internal test cohort, single-sequence AUCs ranged from 0.666 (T1WI) to 0.728 (DCE-MRI), the clinical, radiomics and deep learning models reached 0.571, 0.764 and 0.791, the mpMRI model achieved AUC 0.910 (95% CI 0.837-0.966) with accuracy 0.831, sensitivity 0.742 and specificity 0.885, and the mpMRI_Cli model achieved AUC 0.923 (95% CI 0.860-0.972) with accuracy 0.819, sensitivity 0.839, specificity 0.808 and Brier score 0.122, with the AUC difference between the two not significant after Holm correction (P=0.744). The work indicates that combining multiparametric MRI radiomics and deep learning features improves noninvasive HER2 prediction, though external and multicentre validation is still needed.

Cross-modality Knowledge Transfer Between fMRI and EEG for Improved Single-modality Alzheimer's Disease Diagnosis.

Medical imaging MLscore 8 IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging · IF 10.0journal list2026-11-18X Lin, Y Liu, W Yu et al.

Single-modality Alzheimer's disease diagnosis is limited because fMRI captures spatial network organization while EEG captures temporal dynamics, yet paired fMRI-EEG data are impractical to acquire in practice. The study uses 14 multicentre datasets totaling 2,766 fMRI subjects and 1,842 EEG subjects, covering AD-NC, MCI-NC, and AD-MCI classification tasks. The proposed FE-KTNet learns modality-specific representations from fMRI and EEG functional connectivity networks, performs scale-wise bidirectional cross-modality fusion on paired data, then transfers the fused representations back to each single-modality encoder via scale-wise feature alignment and logit-level knowledge distillation. Compared with corresponding baselines, FE-KTNet raised fMRI-only and EEG-only accuracy by 3.4% and 6.1% for AD-NC, 5.2% and 8.0% for MCI-NC, and 3.5% and 7.1% for AD-MCI classification, achieving the best overall performance among evaluated methods. The approach is notable for improving single-modality diagnosis by transferring cross-modality knowledge learned from paired data, avoiding the need for paired acquisitions at test time.

Multicenter CT-Based Deep Learning for Preoperative Nodal Risk Stratification in Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Thyroid · IF≈6.12026-11-18S Xu, X Yan, Y Su et al.

Preoperative risk stratification for lymph node metastasis in papillary thyroid carcinoma is difficult, and the authors developed a CT-based deep learning model (ThyLNT) to address it. The retrospective data comprised 1560 PTC patients from six hospitals, with a Tongji Hospital cohort split into training and internal validation and five external cohorts for independent testing. Medical imaging MLscore 9 Magnetic Resonance Imaging · IF≈2.42026-11-18W Wang, C Li, J Yang et al.

Preoperative HER2 status in breast cancer is currently determined by core needle biopsy and pathology, so noninvasive prediction from imaging would be clinically useful. The retrospective single-centre study used 257 pathologically confirmed breast cancer patients (98 HER2-positive, 159 HER2-negative) with preoperative T1WI, T2WI, DWI and DCE-MRI, split 7:3 into training (n=174) and internal test (n=83) cohorts. Clinical, radiomics and deep learning models were built, then combined into an mpMRI model fusing radiomics and deep learning features across all four sequences, and an mpMRI_Cli model adding significant clinical features. In the internal test cohort, single-sequence AUCs ranged from 0.666 (T1WI) to 0.728 (DCE-MRI), the clinical, radiomics and deep learning models reached 0.571, 0.764 and 0.791, the mpMRI model achieved AUC 0.910 (95% CI 0.837-0.966) with accuracy 0.831, sensitivity 0.742 and specificity 0.885, and the mpMRI_Cli model achieved AUC 0.923 (95% CI 0.860-0.972) with accuracy 0.819, sensitivity 0.839, specificity 0.808 and Brier score 0.122, with the AUC difference between the two not significant after Holm correction (P=0.744). The work indicates that combining multiparametric MRI radiomics and deep learning features improves noninvasive HER2 prediction, though external and multicentre validation is still needed.

Real-time instance segmentation of spine endoscopy images using a YOLO-V11 deep convolutional neural network.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 PloS One · IF≈3.52026-11-18HJ Jung, NE Nam, C Lee et al.

Existing computer vision work for spine endoscopic surgery largely lacks real-time multi-class segmentation of the full surgical scene, including instruments, anatomy, and bleeding. The study used 2,181 annotated spine endoscopy frames (1,741 training, 216 validation, 224 held-out test) across nine categories including instruments, bone, ligamentum flavum, dura, soft tissue, vessels, fat, the radiofrequency ablation wand, and bleeding focus; the abstract does not state the number of centres or patient cohort size. A YOLO-V11 instance segmentation model was trained and compared across variants, evaluated over five random seeds per variant on an NVIDIA A100 GPU. Radiomicsscore 9late · published 2026-11-17 Journal of Imaging Informatics in Medicine · IF≈3.92026-11-17S Zhao, X Gao, Y Zhang et al.

Postoperative neurological recovery in cervical spondylotic myelopathy is highly variable, and existing prognostic methods have limitations. The study retrospectively enrolled 237 patients from Center 1 (split 165 training / 72 internal test) and 96 patients from Center 2 as an independent external validation set, using pre- and postoperative T2WI MRI. Radiomic and delta-radiomic features were extracted and screened with LASSO and Spearman correlation, and three models were built across five machine learning algorithms, with the XGBoost-based delta-radiomic model achieving the highest AUCs of 0.899 (internal test) and 0.802 (external validation), significantly better than the preoperative radiomic and clinical-routine imaging models. The approach is notable for using routine short-term postoperative MRI (within 7 days) to predict 12-month neurological recovery in a dual-center, externally validated, interpretable model.

Bone and skeletalscore 9 JBMR · IF 5.9journal list2026-11-18AS Dash, M Wood, A Jepson et al.

The study used 239 anteroposterior full-spine radiographs from 239 participants, with a development cohort of 192 evaluated by patient-level stratified five-fold cross-validation and an independent temporal validation cohort of 47 consecutive later cases; the source and whether data were single- or multi-centre are not stated in the abstract. Bias and equity in large language model-based applications for health care: a scoping review.

EHR and clinical NLPscore 7review The Lancet Digital Health · IF 24.1journal list2026-11-18J Liao, H Li, R Yang et al.

The review addresses the limited systematic understanding of bias and equity research in large language model (LLM) applications for health care, and the scarcity of actionable mitigation strategies despite widespread discussion of these risks. It synthesises 201 English-language studies identified through searches of PubMed, Embase, Web of Science and Scopus for articles published between 1 January 2018 and 10 March 2026; the abstract does not specify the number of centres involved. The scoping review mapped trends and gaps across the included studies, finding a strong focus on particular medical contexts and demographic factors and a predominant reliance on proprietary models that constrain transparency in bias evaluation. It reports a disconnect between bias detection and mitigation and that the potential of LLMs to promote health equity remains underexplored, without giving further quantitative results. The authors argue the field needs large-scale, endpoint-driven studies grounded in real-world clinical workflows, standardised multidimensional bias evaluation frameworks supported by professional societies, and lifecycle bias governance across proprietary and open-weight models.

Subclinical heterogeneity in cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic (CKM) syndrome is underexplored, and early detection of physiological deviation is needed to prevent irreversible organ damage. The study used the US WEAR-ME cohort of 819 participants, combining continuous wearable data with blood biomarkers (Chol/HDL, eGFR, HOMA-IR); the abstract does not state whether it is single- or multi-centre. A normalized deviance score identified significant health impairment in 29.0% of the cohort, with cardiovascular deviation most prevalent (13.3%), followed by metabolic (9.1%) and renal (6.25%), and dual metabolic-cardiovascular impairment in only 2.2%; metabolic and cardiovascular disruptions were associated (r = 0.26, p < 0.001) while early kidney impairment manifested independently. The work argues for multi-system subtyping, showing that wearable-derived phenotypes plus routine blood biomarkers can support early, targeted intervention in CKM syndrome.
