ASAM: Adaptive cortical surface representation via dynamic local aggregation and hybrid Attention-Mamba global modeling.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Medical Image Analysis · IF 11.8journal list2026-11-12SD Tran, HJ Yoon, JK Gahm et al.

Modeling cortical surface geometry from structural MRI is limited by Transformer and Mamba architectures that rely on fixed or absent neighborhood structures, restricting representation of localized cortical variation, with Transformers computationally costly and Mamba lacking explicit local spatial context on non-Euclidean domains. The method, ASAM, is a parallel hybrid Transformer-Mamba framework with two modules: Dynamic Multi-Hop Selection, which adaptively sets receptive fields per cortical patch, and Dynamic Feature Merging, which builds context-sensitive adjacency matrices from geodesic distances and learned affinities. Evaluations on public neuroimaging datasets (ADNI CN vs. MCI classification and cross-dataset brain age prediction) show superior or highly competitive performance against Transformer- and Mamba-based baselines, though the abstract gives no numerical results. The work's contribution is dynamic adjustment of neighborhood scales to produce geometry-aware cortical surface representations, with the authors noting that multi-center validation is still needed for clinical translation.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via elsevier)

ASAM addresses a concrete gap in cortical surface learning: how to combine the global receptive field of Mamba's linear-time state-space recurrence with the short-range selectivity of attention, without the O(N^2) cost of Transformers or the spatial blindness of sequence-ordered Mamba on non-Euclidean meshes. The clinical framing is neurodegenerative risk stratification (CN vs. MCI classification) and brain-age prediction from T1-weighted MRI processed with FreeSurfer into fsaverage-registered icospheres, tokenized into 320 triangular patches per hemisphere with thickness, curvature, and sulcal depth as vertex features. The architecture is a parallel two-branch hybrid: a Mamba branch preceded by a Local Feature Learning block, and a channel-attention/attention branch, fused by a prediction head. The LFL block contains Dynamic Multi-Hop Selection (a GRU-based gated survival process that expands each patch's geodesic k-hop neighborhood until marginal state change falls below a threshold) and Dynamic Feature Merging (a convex blend of geodesic-Gaussian static affinities and learned query-key dynamic affinities). The genuinely new elements are the per-patch adaptive receptive field and the geodesic-plus-semantic soft adjacency, which directly target Mamba's lack of mesh awareness; the parallel (rather than sequential) Attention-Mamba fusion is a reasonable but less novel design choice. Opportunistic Risk Assessment of Major Osteoporotic Fractures from Routine Chest Radiographs Using Deep Learning.

Bone and skeletalscore 10 NEJM AI · IF≈27.22026-11-16Y Kim, CM Park, H Ko et al.

Bone mineral density and FRAX incompletely capture fracture risk in the osteopenic range, where much of the fracture burden occurs, and routine chest radiographs may carry relevant risk information without added imaging burden. The authors trained a convolutional neural network survival model on 53,109 adults at a tertiary hospital and externally validated it in 85,284 patients from two independent institutions, using chest radiographs obtained within 1 year of DXA scans, so all participants had been referred for densitometry. The primary outcome was incident major osteoporotic fracture, with discrimination measured by C-index and censoring-adjusted AUROC at 2, 3, 5, and 10 years against FRAX with BMD and lowest BMD T-score. In external validation (mean age 58.6 years, 68.9% female, 77-month follow-up), the DL model reached a C-index of 0.843 and 10-year AUROC of 0.855 versus 0.779 and 0.785 for FRAX with BMD (both P<0.01), and in the osteopenia subgroup (n=35,358) the 10-year AUROC was 0.779 versus 0.659 for FRAX with BMD. The work indicates that routine chest radiographs can support fracture-risk discrimination beyond FRAX, particularly in osteopenia, though because all participants had been referred for densitometry, absolute-risk and case-finding estimates require prospective evaluation in an unselected radiography population.

Multichannel Deep Learning Radiomics for Predicting Chemoimmunotherapy Response in Gastric Cancer: Multicenter Validation and Chemotherapy-alone Specificity Assessment.

Radiomicsscore 9 Academic Radiology · IF≈3.32026-11-16C Ran, C Wang, G Chu et al.

The study addresses prediction of pathologic significant response to neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in locally advanced gastric cancer, and whether a CT-based model captures immunotherapy-specific rather than general chemosensitivity. It used a multicentre cohort of 455 patients: 411 receiving chemoimmunotherapy (training, internal validation, and external test set 1) and 44 receiving chemotherapy alone (test set 2), with CT images segmented in 3D. The method stacked the largest axial, coronal, and sagittal slices as three-channel input to a pretrained ResNet50 for deep features, combined these with 3D radiomic features, reduced them via LASSO, and modelled with a support vector machine; a combined model added mismatch repair status, PD-L1 combined positive score, and tumour grade. The multichannel model outperformed single-plane models (AUCs 0.783, 0.749, 0.706), and the deep learning radiomics model achieved the highest AUCs (0.827, 0.802, 0.776), surpassing radiomics-only (0.756, 0.712, 0.691) and multichannel deep models, while its AUC fell to 0.597 in the chemotherapy-alone cohort. The drop in the chemotherapy-alone cohort is presented as evidence that the imaging phenotypes relate more to immunotherapy-associated response than to general chemosensitivity, supporting a noninvasive pretreatment stratification tool with SHAP-based interpretability.

Machine learning model based on CT radiomics for the differential diagnosis of brucellar and tuberculous spondylitis: a model construction and validation study.

Radiomicsscore 9late · published 2026-08-19 BMC Medical Imaging · IF≈3.82026-08-19X He, Y Chen, M Yue et al.

Differential diagnosis of tuberculous versus brucellar spondylitis is difficult because clinical and conventional imaging features overlap, and CT-based radiomics combined with clinical variables had not been systematically tested for this task. The data come from a single-centre retrospective cohort of 327 surgically and pathologically confirmed patients (259 TS, 68 BS) who underwent CT between January 2017 and December 2022. The method extracted 1,051 PyRadiomics features from sagittal CT images under IBSI standards, applied ComBat harmonization, selected features via mRMR and LASSO, and compared five classifiers (logistic regression prespecified as primary) across clinical, radiomics, and combined models using stratified 10-fold nested cross-validation. The logistic regression combined model reached a mean cross-validated AUC of 0.882 (95% CI 0.837-0.927) with sensitivity 0.867 and specificity 0.671, versus AUC 0.847 for radiomics alone and 0.786 for clinical alone, with the combined-versus-radiomics gain (ΔAUC 0.035) not statistically significant (DeLong P = 0.068). The work adds a CT-based, IBSI-compliant radiomics benchmark for this differential, but its imperfect specificity (about one-third of TS cases potentially misclassified as BS), single-centre retrospective design, and restriction to surgically treated patients limit generalizability pending multi-centre external validation.
