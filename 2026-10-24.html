Integrating Magnetic Resonance Imaging-Based Deep Learning and Radiomics to Predict Treatment Response in Hepatocellular Carcinoma Receiving Combined Systemic and Locoregional Therapies: A Multicenter Study.

Radiomicsscore 10 Liver cancer · IF≈5.32026-09-07LN Wang, SQ Zhou, F Li et al.

Predicting which hepatocellular carcinoma patients will respond to combined systemic and locoregional therapy is difficult because no reliable tool exists. The retrospective multicenter study used pretreatment contrast-enhanced MRI from 334 HCC patients across nine centers in China, split into training (n=213), internal validation (n=53), and external validation (n=68) cohorts. Tumors were segmented automatically with nnU-Net, radiomics features were extracted, and deep learning features came from a ResNet CNN; radiomics, deep learning, and combined models were then built and validated for tumor response. The combined model achieved AUCs of 0.997 in training, 0.900 in internal validation, and 0.871 in external validation, and it improved reclassification over the radiomics model (NRI 43.9% training, 38.7% internal, 3.2% external) and the deep learning model (NRI 80.8%, 72.7%, 50.4%; all p<0.05), with significant associations to progression-free and overall survival. The work is notable for combining radiomics and deep learning features from multi-sequence MRI in a multi-center external validation setting and linking predictions to survival outcomes.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via europepmc)

This multicenter retrospective study addresses a concrete clinical question in unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC): can pretreatment MRI predict which patients will achieve an objective response (OR, per mRECIST) to first-line combined systemic (targeted therapy plus immunotherapy) and locoregional therapy, thereby avoiding unnecessary toxicity in likely non-responders? The authors pooled 334 patients from 9 Chinese tertiary centers (213 training, 53 internal validation, 68 temporally independent external validation from two centers). Tumors were auto-segmented with nnU-Net (Dice 0.889 on a 54-patient test set), and features were extracted from seven MRI sequences: 1,118 PyRadiomics features per sequence (7,826 total) and ResNet-based deep features via 2.5D and 3D frameworks (ten single-sequence models). Per-sequence models were fused by logistic regression into multi-sequence radiomics and deep learning models, then combined by logistic-regression fusion of the two output probabilities. The combined model achieved AUCs of 0.997 (training), 0.900 (internal validation) and 0.871 (external validation), exceeding either unimodal model, with significant net reclassification versus deep learning (NRI 72.7% internal, 50.4% external) but not versus radiomics externally (NRI 3.2%, p=0.806). Prediction scores were associated with OS and PFS, including in the external cohort. Genuinely new: a head-to-head radiomics-versus-deep-learning-versus-fusion comparison with temporal external validation in this specific combination-therapy setting; the fusion gain is real but modest and largely driven by internal-cohort reclassification.

Datasets

  • Nine Chinese tertiary centers, contrast-enhanced multiparametric MRI (7 sequences), 334 patients total, private, retrospective
  • Training cohort: 213 patients, private, model fitting
  • Internal validation cohort (IVC): 53 patients, random 8:2 split, private, internal test
  • External validation cohort (EVC): 68 patients from two centers, temporally independent, private, external test
  • nnU-Net segmentation test set: 54 patients, private, segmentation evaluation only

Method

  • nnU-Net automatic tumor segmentation on 7 MRI sequences (arterial, portal venous, delayed, T2WI, DWI, T1 in-phase/out-of-phase)
  • Radiomics: PyRadiomics v3.0.1, 1,118 features/sequence (7,826 total), Z-score normalization, mRMR + LASSO selection, decision tree/logistic regression/random forest/SVM per sequence, logistic-regression fusion across sequences
  • Deep learning: ResNet34-based 2.5D and 3D models (ten single-sequence models), ASLSingleLabel loss (smoothing 0.1, gamma_neg 4), AdamW lr 1e-5, warmup cosine, batch 32, real-time augmentation, logistic-regression fusion
  • Combined model: logistic-regression fusion of multi-sequence radiomics and deep learning output probabilities
  • Baselines: single-sequence radiomics and deep learning models, multi-sequence radiomics, multi-sequence deep learning, clinical-feature logistic regression

Validation

  • Two-tier: internal validation cohort (random 8:2 split) then temporally independent external validation cohort from two centers; retrospective, not prospective
  • AUC with 95% CI (1,000 stratified bootstrap), accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, precision, F1; Youden-index cutoff from training; calibration curves; decision curve analysis
  • Net reclassification index (NRI) for model comparisons; Kaplan-Meier/log-rank for OS and PFS associations; two-sided p<0.05

Results

  • Combined model AUC 0.997 training, 0.900 IVC, 0.871 EVC; multi-sequence radiomics 0.786 IVC / 0.849 EVC; multi-sequence deep learning 0.829 IVC / 0.740 EVC
  • Combined vs deep learning NRI 72.7% (IVC, p<0.001) and 50.4% (EVC, p=0.016); combined vs radiomics NRI 38.7% (IVC, p=0.038) but only 3.2% (EVC, p=0.806)
  • Multi-sequence vs best single-sequence: radiomics 0.786 vs 0.736 IVC (p=0.249, NRI 56.1%); deep learning 0.829 vs 0.771 (p=0.207, NRI 75.9%)
  • ORR comparable across cohorts: 57.3% training, 56.6% IVC, 58.8% EVC (p=0.965); no baseline clinical feature significantly associated with OR
  • Combined-model scores associated with OS (p=0.009) and PFS (p=0.008) in EVC; radiomics scores associated with OS/PFS in EVC (p=0.009/0.008); deep learning only with PFS (p=0.045)

Limitations

  • Authors: small sample size, retrospective design with bias risk, no interpretability analysis, increased computational burden, treatment heterogeneity confounding
  • Authors: model not ready for standalone clinical use without prospective validation and human-in-the-loop oversight
  • Obvious omission: no comparison against a clinical-variable-only model in validation (clinical features were null in training), and no DeLong tests for AUC differences between models
  • Obvious omission: external cohort differs in treatment mix (more bevacizumab, HAIC+TACE) and baseline PIVKA-II, and the fusion benefit over radiomics did not replicate externally

Distribution matching based personalized federated learning for multi-center, cross-modal liver image registration.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Medical Image Analysis · IF 11.82026-10-19R Zheng, H Yu, X Wu et al.

Cross-modal registration of liver DWI and DCE MRI is difficult because of strong contrast differences, respiratory motion, limited single-centre training data and inter-centre heterogeneity. The framework was evaluated on six datasets from five medical centres, acquired on scanners from three vendors under low- and high-b-value settings. The method combines a multi-task synthesis-registration network (SynReg), which couples image synthesis with deformable registration to reduce cross-modal appearance discrepancy, with a personalized decentralized distribution matching (PDDM) framework that uses signal-to-noise ratio distributions as a lightweight compatibility descriptor to select compatible external cases for client-specific collaboration, keeping synthesis local and selectively aggregating only the registration network. PDDM consistently outperformed single-centre training and representative personalized federated learning methods and was closely competitive with centralized pooled-data training, and it showed the most consistently favourable overall performance across centres, b-value settings and registration metrics compared with alternative matching strategies. The work indicates that compatibility-aware selective collaboration is a practical strategy for personalized multi-centre liver registration, though the abstract reports no numerical metric values.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from abstract only)

This paper addresses deformable registration between liver diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) and dynamic contrast-enhanced (DCE) MRI, a clinically relevant pairing for tumor diagnosis and quantitative multi-modal analysis. The core difficulties are large inter-modal contrast differences, respiratory motion, small single-center training cohorts, and inter-center heterogeneity. The authors propose SynReg, a multi-task network that jointly performs cross-modal image synthesis and deformable registration in one architecture, using synthesis to narrow the appearance gap between DWI and DCE. On top of SynReg they build PDDM (personalized decentralized distribution matching), a personalized federated learning scheme that performs compatibility-aware selective collaboration: each client identifies compatible external training cases via a lightweight descriptor based on signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) distributions, keeps synthesis components local, and selectively aggregates only the registration network with compatible clients. Evaluation spans six datasets from five centers and three MRI vendors, under low- and high-b-value settings. PDDM reportedly outperformed single-center training and representative personalized federated learning baselines, and was closely competitive with centralized pooled-data training; among matching strategies it was the most consistently favorable across centers, b-value settings, and metrics. The genuinely new element is the compatibility-aware, SNR-descriptor-driven selective aggregation combined with local synthesis, rather than the synthesis-registration backbone itself. Because only the abstract was available, quantitative effect sizes and ablation details could not be verified.

  • PDDM: personalized decentralized federated learning with compatibility-aware selective collaboration
  • SNR-distribution-based lightweight compatibility descriptor for client matching
  • Synthesis components kept local; registration network selectively aggregated across compatible clients
  • Baselines: single-center training, representative personalized federated learning methods, centralized pooled-data training, alternative matching strategies

  • Registration metrics reported (specific metrics not enumerated in abstract)

  • PDDM consistently outperformed single-center training and representative personalized federated learning methods on registration metrics (exact values not given in abstract)
  • PDDM closely competitive with centralized pooled-data training (exact gap not reported)

  • Scalability, communication cost, and privacy guarantees of the decentralized scheme not addressed in the abstract

Impact of dose reduction and multi-parametric harmonization on multi-organ radiomics robustness in total-body PET imaging: Methodological implications for quantitative theranostics.

Radiomicsscore 9 Medical physics · IF≈3.12026-11-01MS Azimi, M Cheraghi, H Zaidi

PET radiomic features are sensitive to count statistics and acquisition protocol variability, which limits their quantitative reliability in low-dose and multi-centre total-body PET imaging. The study retrospectively analysed a two-institution total-body PET dataset of 550 patients, simulating seven dose levels (standard plus dose reduction factors of 2, 4, 10, 20, 50 and 100) from list-mode data and extracting 107 IBSI-compliant radiomic features from SUV-normalised images after deep learning-based automatic segmentation. Reproducibility was measured with ICC(2,1) across dose levels, organs and five ComBat harmonisation configurations (scanner manufacturer, pixel spacing, radiopharmaceutical, scatter correction, and a non-harmonised baseline). Overall, 32.7% of features reached good or excellent reproducibility (ICC ≥ 0.75) in at least one anatomical region; selected first-order and intensity features held up under moderate dose reduction (DRF ≤ 20), while severe count reduction (DRF 50-100) destabilised higher-order texture features, especially in vascular and lower-contrast soft-tissue regions. Harmonisation changed reproducibility patterns in an organ- and feature-dependent way but did not uniformly improve cross-dose agreement or offset noise-induced instability, pointing to the need for dose-aware feature selection in multi-centre PET radiomics.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via europepmc)

This retrospective multi-center study asks whether total-body PET radiomic features remain reproducible under simulated dose reduction and whether metadata-guided ComBat harmonization can rescue cross-center consistency—a prerequisite question for low-dose longitudinal theranostic imaging. The authors pooled 550 whole-body PET/CT scans from two digital total-body systems (uEXPLORER, Biograph Vision Quadra) across four tracers ([18F]FDG, [18F]PSMA, [68Ga]DOTA-TOC, [68Ga]DOTA-TATE). List-mode data were temporally subsampled at dose-reduction factors (DRF) 2–100 (50% to 1% of counts), yielding intrinsically co-registered reduced-count reconstructions. A pretrained nnU-Net v2 segmented 21 organs on the full-count image, with masks fixed across dose levels; 107 IBSI-compliant PyRadiomics features were extracted per organ. Reproducibility was quantified with ICC(2,1) across six predefined scenarios, plus a reference-based first-failure analysis (ICC<0.75 vs full dose). Headline findings: only 35/107 features (32.7%) reached ICC≥0.75 in at least one organ; median family-level cross-dose ICC was ~0.31–0.40 unadjusted; no ComBat configuration (manufacturer, pixel spacing, scatter correction, tracer) improved cross-dose reproducibility (medians 0.25–0.27), and harmonization settings agreed well with each other at fixed dose (median ICC 0.87–0.93). The genuinely new contribution is the joint dose×organ×harmonization characterization on total-body PET, showing that batch harmonization and stochastic noise are partially independent variability sources—an important negative result for multi-center radiomics pipelines.

Datasets

  • Bern/Ruijin multi-center total-body PET/CT cohort: 550 patients, [18F]FDG, [18F]PSMA, [68Ga]DOTA-TOC, [68Ga]DOTA-TATE; uEXPLORER and Biograph Vision Quadra; private (data-sharing agreement); all patients used for feature extraction and ICC analysis (no separate train/val/external test split)
  • Retrospective reduced-count reconstructions: DRF 2, 4, 10, 20, 50, 100 per scan from list-mode temporal subsampling; derived from same cohort; used as repeated measures for ICC

Method

  • Pretrained nnU-Net v2 (3D full-resolution) PET-based multi-organ segmentation; 21 organs; no retraining or fine-tuning; single full-count mask applied unchanged to all dose levels
  • PyRadiomics 3.1.0, IBSI-compliant, 107 features (14 shape, 18 first-order, 24 GLCM, 16 GLRLM, 16 GLSZM, 14 GLDM, 5 NGTDM); native voxel spacing, no resampling; fixed bin width 0.4 SUV
  • ComBat feature-level harmonization (no covariates) with four batch definitions: manufacturer, pixel spacing, scatter correction, radiopharmaceutical (exploratory); plus non-harmonized baseline
  • ICC(2,1) two-way random-effects absolute-agreement model across six predefined scenarios; reference-based first-failure DRF (ICC<0.75) analysis
  • Descriptive/exploratory statistics in Python 3.11; no inferential hypothesis testing

Validation

  • Internal only: cross-dose and cross-harmonization ICC within the same 550-patient two-center cohort; no external test cohort or prospective validation
  • Metrics: ICC(2,1) with 95% CIs; family/organ-level medians; threshold ICC≥0.75 for good/excellent reproducibility
  • No statistical testing (authors state analyses were descriptive and reproducibility-oriented)

Results

  • 35/107 features (32.7%) achieved ICC≥0.75 in ≥1 organ; highest GLCM Cluster Prominence in bladder ICC=0.906 (95% CI 0.857–0.948); first-order Mean ICC 0.845 colon, 0.893 erector spinae, 0.820 femur
  • Median cross-dose ICC unadjusted 0.364 vs 0.265 (manufacturer), 0.258 (pixel spacing), 0.258 (scatter correction), 0.253 (tracer) — no harmonization improved cross-dose reproducibility
  • First-failure analysis: first-order and GLCM features showed no failure through DRF 100 in 15/21 and 18/21 organs; Total Energy and Autocorrelation robust through DRF 100 in all organs; Mean/Sum Average/Joint Average failed at DRF 4 in kidneys and liver; Energy failed at DRF 2 in aorta, esophagus, lungs
  • Inter-harmonization agreement at fixed dose: median ICC 0.934 (standard), 0.908 (DRF 20), 0.869 (DRF 50), 0.906 (DRF 100); ≥0.75 in 126/126, 101/101, 101/101, 118/122 combinations respectively
  • Poorly reproducible features (ICC<0.50) concentrated in GLSZM/GLRLM/GLDM (Zone Percentage, Zone Variance, LAHGLE, LALGLE, SALGLE, SDLGLE, SZNUN, RLNUN) and first-order 10th Percentile

Limitations

  • Temporal-window subsampling simulates shorter acquisitions, not true random event thinning or prospectively reduced injected activity; ignores motion and temporal tracer kinetics
  • Only two centers/scanners; native voxel spacing without isotropic resampling may inflate texture variability and limit comparability with standardized pipelines
  • Fixed full-count segmentation mask excludes dose-dependent segmentation uncertainty; ICC values are best-case conditional estimates and shape-feature stability is artifactual
  • ICC-only robustness metric (no CV or test–retest); no clinical outcome or downstream model validation; feature redundancy/collinearity (e.g., Sum Average vs Mean) not assessed; tracer-as-batch ComBat may remove biological signal

Imaging data not publicly available (ethical/institutional restrictions); analysis code available upon request from corresponding author.

Radiomics-Assisted Preoperative Subtyping of Adult-Type Diffuse Gliomas: A Retrospective Study Aligned With the WHO 2021 Classification.

Radiomicsscore 9 CNS neuroscience & therapeutics · IF≈6.32026-10-01Y Liang, J Shi, C Zhao et al.

Accurate preoperative subtyping of adult-type diffuse gliomas is needed because surgical management differs by subtype under the WHO 2021 classification. The retrospective single-center study used MRI from 382 patients with adult-type diffuse gliomas, split randomly into a training set of 267 and an independent test set of 115, with tumor masks automatically segmented and then refined by neurosurgeons. After standardized preprocessing and dimensionality reduction, LASSO regression retained 50 radiomics features, and 14 machine learning classifiers were compared, with Random Forest selected for optimization and validation on the independent test set. The optimized model achieved a macro-average AUC of 0.9084 on the independent test set, with texture features contributing most strongly to prediction. The work is limited by its retrospective single-center design, and the authors state that multicenter prospective external validation is required before widespread clinical use.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via europepmc)

This retrospective single-center study addresses a clinically motivated question: can routine preoperative MRI radiomics non-invasively distinguish the three adult-type diffuse glioma (ADG) subtypes defined by the WHO 2021 integrated histomolecular framework (astrocytoma IDH-mutant, oligodendroglioma IDH-mutant/1p19q-codeleted, and IDH-wildtype glioblastoma) before surgery, when histopathology is unavailable and surgical strategy must be chosen? The authors assembled 382 patients (146 astrocytoma, 126 oligodendroglioma, 110 GBM) imaged on 3.0T Siemens scanners with T1W, T2W, T1CE and FLAIR; tumors were auto-segmented with DeepBraTumIA and manually refined. After resampling, Z-score normalization, fixed-bin discretization (128 levels, bin width 4), wavelet (LL/LH/HL/HH) and LoG (sigma 1/3/5) filtering, 2766 IBSI-compliant features were extracted. LASSO multinomial logistic regression (lambda.min, 10-fold CV) retained 50 features; 14 classifiers were screened with PyCaret defaults, and the best (random forest) was Bayesian-tuned (scikit-optimize, MCC objective, 50 iterations) with nested 10-fold CV. On a held-out 115-patient test set the tuned RF achieved accuracy 0.7565, macro-AUC 0.9084, Kappa 0.6305 and MCC 0.6337, with one-vs-rest AUCs of 0.9557 (GBM), 0.8854 (astrocytoma) and 0.8701 (oligodendroglioma); most errors were astrocytoma/oligodendroglioma confusions (~20% each way). Genuinely new elements are modest: the main contribution is re-aligning a radiomics pipeline with the 2021 WHO labels and benchmarking 14 tuned classifiers; the methodology (handcrafted features, LASSO, RF) is conventional, and validation is internal only.

Datasets

  • Single-center retrospective cohort, Beijing Tiantan Hospital, Oct 2018–Jun 2021; 3.0T MRI (T1W, T2W, T1CE, FLAIR); n=382 (A=146, O=126, GBM=110); private; random 7:3 split into training (n=267) and independent test (n=115)

Method

  • Handcrafted IBSI radiomics: 18 first-order + 68 texture (GLCM/GLSZM/GLRLM/GLDM) per image across original, 4 wavelet sub-bands and 3 LoG scales per sequence, plus 14 shape features = 2766 features/ROI
  • Segmentation via DeepBraTumIA (enhancing tumor + T2/FLAIR hyperintensity), manually revised by two neurosurgeons with senior adjudication
  • Feature selection: LASSO-penalized multinomial logistic regression, lambda.min from 10-fold CV, 50 non-zero features retained
  • Classifier screening: 14 algorithms (RF, LDA, NB, LGBM, ET, GBC, CatBoost, LR, KNN, linear SVM, QDA, AdaBoost, DT, Dummy) with PyCaret defaults; RF selected on macro-AUC
  • RF hyperparameter tuning via Bayesian optimization (scikit-optimize, MCC objective, 50 iterations) with nested 10-fold CV

Validation

  • Internal only: 10-fold cross-validation in the training set plus a single random 70/30 hold-out test set; no external or prospective cohort
  • Metrics: accuracy, one-vs-rest and macro/micro-averaged AUC, precision, recall, F1, Cohen's Kappa, MCC, PR/average precision, normalized confusion matrices
  • Permutation feature importance for interpretability; no formal statistical comparison tests between models reported

Results

  • Test set (n=115): accuracy 0.7565, macro-AUC 0.9084, precision 0.7718, recall 0.7565, F1 0.7595, Kappa 0.6305, MCC 0.6337
