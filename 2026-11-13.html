OPXrayNet: a high-precision, explainable AI framework for transformative opportunistic osteoporosis screening utilizing multi-site X-ray imaging.

Bone and skeletalscore 10 European Radiology · IF 4.7journal list2026-11-13Q Jin, Q Jia, X Zhou et al.

Opportunistic osteoporosis screening from routine skeletal X-rays is limited by single-site analyses and poor interpretability of existing deep-learning methods. The retrospective multicentre study used 16,347 annotated X-ray images from 2,186 patients (mean age 69.0 ± 10.5 years, 72.3% female), with osteoporosis defined by DXA T-score ≤ −2.5. OPXrayNet combined YOLOv8 region-of-interest detection, a spatial-frequency joint focusing architecture, a feature pyramid network for multi-scale fusion, and an expert-guided loss module. It reached 80.1% accuracy on the internal test set (n = 3,892) and an AUC of 0.844 (95% CI 0.821–0.867) on external validation, with a numerical 3.9% difference versus other models, and feature heatmaps indicated focus on clinically critical bone structures. The work is notable for extending automated, interpretable osteoporosis screening to multi-site X-ray data, supporting scalable use in primary care without added cost or radiation.

Deep dive (from full text via springer-oa-api)

OPXrayNet addresses opportunistic osteoporosis (OP) screening from routine multi-site skeletal radiographs, using DXA T-scores (WHO thresholds) as the reference standard in a retrospective three-hospital cohort (Xinjiang, Caoxian, Quzhou; Jan 2021–Nov 2024; 2186 patients, mean age 69.0±10.5 y, 72.3% female; 16,347 annotated ROIs across up to ten anatomical sites). The pipeline is fully automated: YOLOv8 (with DFL/CIoU losses and k-means++ anchor tuning) localizes skeletal ROIs (mAP@0.5 0.98, precision 0.99); a space-frequency focusing architecture (SFFA) applies Sobel, dynamic gamma, wavelet, Fourier and CLAHE transforms with a self-attention fusion network; a ShuffleNetV2+FPN head performs three-class classification; and an expert-guided loss (EGLM) up-weights samples with radiologist-annotated OP signs. Missing ROIs are handled by repeated filling to a fixed 10-channel input. Headline results: external (QZ, n=854) accuracy 0.778, three-class macro-AUC 0.844 (95% CI 0.818–0.868), OP-vs-rest AUC 0.866; at FPR≤10%, sensitivity 85%/specificity 90%. Ablation raised Dataset A accuracy from 0.675 to 0.744. Genuinely new elements are the multi-site whole-image automation without manual ROIs, the multi-transform spatial-frequency enhancement with attention fusion, and the expert-sign-weighted loss; however, gains over baselines are modest (e.g., 0.778 vs 0.747 Swin on external accuracy) and the authors explicitly refrain from significance claims due to uncorrected multiple comparisons.

Datasets

  • Hospital XJ (First Affiliated Hospital of Xinjiang Medical University), multi-site skeletal X-ray + DXA, private, 8:2 patient-level train/internal test
  • Hospital QZ (Affiliated Quzhou Hospital, Wenzhou Medical University), multi-site X-ray + DXA, private, used both as 8:2 internal split and as held-out external test (n=854)
  • Hospital CX (Caoxian People's Hospital), multi-site X-ray + DXA, private, 8:2 internal split and low-prevalence external test (n=594, OP prevalence 28%)
  • Dataset A / Dataset B: pooled three-hospital data split 8:2 at patient level (train/val vs independent test)
  • Annotated ROI corpus: 16,347 ROI images from 12,099 imaging records, 10 skeletal regions, private, used for YOLOv8 detection training and classification

Method

  • YOLOv8 detector with Distribution Focal Loss + CIoU and k-means++ anchor optimization for automatic multi-site ROI localization (no manual ROIs at inference)
  • SFFA: five parallel image transforms (Sobel, dynamic gamma DGSC, wavelet multi-scale, Fourier, CLAHE) generating 50 feature maps, fused by a self-attention network combining spatial attention, channel attention, 8-head MHSA and pyramid pooling with dynamic weighting
  • ShuffleNetV2 backbone with FPN classification head (stages 3/4 projected to 78 channels; 64×64, 32×32, 8×8 maps) for three-class (normal/osteopenia/OP) output
  • EGLM: expert-annotated salient-feature value (EASFA, 1.0 + 0.1 per positive joint, capped 1.5) multiplying weighted cross-entropy loss
  • Fixed 10-channel input with repeated-filling for missing ROIs; Grad-CAM for interpretability; baselines: DCNN, DeepSeek-VL2, Swin-Transformer v2, MobileNetV2, ShuffleNetV2, ResNet50 (Adam, lr 1e-4)

Validation

  • Retrospective multi-center; patient-level splits; internal 8:2 per-hospital and pooled splits plus external test on held-out hospital QZ (and CX low-prevalence external test)
  • Metrics: accuracy, macro/micro AUC, OP-vs-rest AUC with 95% CI, sensitivity/specificity/PPV/NPV at FPR≤10%
  • McNemar test for accuracy and DeLong test for AUC, but no multiple-comparison correction; authors state differences are descriptive only; blinded 3-expert majority-vote Grad-CAM plausibility assessment

Results

  • External test (QZ, n=854): accuracy 0.778 vs Swin 0.747, DCNN 0.738, ShuffleNetV2 0.745, DeepSeek-VL2 0.735, ResNet50 0.726
  • External three-class macro-AUC 0.844 (95% CI 0.818–0.868) vs Swin 0.805, DCNN 0.791, MobileNetV2 0.747, ResNet50 0.738; micro-AUC 0.853
  • OP-vs-rest AUC 0.866 (95% CI 0.841–0.889); at FPR≤10%: sensitivity 85%, specificity 90%, PPV 86%, NPV 84%
  • Ablation on Dataset A: 0.675 (no modules) → 0.692 (+SFFA) → 0.688 (+FPN) → 0.710 (+EGLM) → 0.744 (all three); EGLM added to MobileNetV2-Large gave 0.734 (+0.063)
  • Low-prevalence external test (CX, n=594, 28% OP): accuracy 0.685, macro-AUC 0.722; Grad-CAM expert win rate 55.0% (470/854) vs 35.9% for other models

Limitations

  • Authors: training/validation limited to three institutions; broader multi-center, large-scale validation still needed
  • Authors: external data distribution differences may not reflect real clinical diversity; future work to expand populations and modalities
  • Accuracy remains modest (0.685–0.835 across settings; 0.685 at 28% prevalence), and inter-model differences are small and not statistically tested after multiple-comparison correction
  • Omitted: no prospective evaluation or clinical utility analysis; repeated-filling of missing ROIs may bias cases with incomplete imaging; DXA reference itself has measurement error; no comparison against radiologist readers; EGLM requires labor-intensive expert sign annotation

Paired DXA and pQCT of bone parameters in a clinical paediatric population: A cluster analysis.

Bone and skeletalscore 9 Bone · IF 3.5journal list2026-11-13J Tan, NH Hart, P Chivers et al.

Accurate bone health assessment in paediatric clinical populations requires criteria for when pQCT adds value beyond standard DXA, particularly TBLH, which is the preferred clinical measure. The study analysed 457 individuals from a single Western Australian paediatric centre routinely performing paired DXA and pQCT, using DXA-TBLH BMD, pQCT volumetric density at the 4% and 66% arm and leg sites, age and height. Sex-specific cluster analysis was used to define clinical phenotypes and guide decisions on adding pQCT to BMD analyses. DXA and pQCT measurements were positively correlated, especially at the 4% site, and two lower-density clusters with higher fracture frequency emerged, characterised by malabsorption and bone mineral metabolism conditions in females and bone mineral metabolism and neuromuscular conditions in males; DXA-pQCT variation was greatest in these clusters, with DXA lower, and females showed greater variability with height as an influencing factor. The findings support combined DXA and pQCT assessment in paediatric bone health, especially in high-risk groups.

Explainable deep learning localizes age-discriminative candidate regions on hand radiographs of adult women.

Bone and skeletalscore 9 Skeletal Radiology · IF≈2.52026-11-13HS Jang, J Cho, DH Kim et al.

The study asks whether deep learning can distinguish younger from older adult women on hand radiographs and which anatomical regions drive that discrimination. It used 2,221 posteroanterior hand radiographs from a single centre (1,134 women aged 20-45 and 1,087 aged 55-80, 2020-2023), with five architectures evaluated on the same 334 held-out test radiographs across five random-seed runs. DenseNet121 was selected for Grad-CAM and ray-based retraining analyses, and manual cortical measurements of test radiographs produced metacarpal and proximal phalangeal indices for the rays highlighted by Grad-CAM. DenseNet121 reached a mean AUC of 0.996 (95% CI 0.992-0.999), Grad-CAM activation centred on the third proximal phalanx with additional activation in the second and fourth rays, and the third proximal phalangeal index had the highest AUC among the six indices (0.956; 95% CI 0.937-0.976). The work links explainable AI localization with quantitative cortical measurements to propose the third proximal phalanx as an interpretable candidate region for age-group discrimination, offering an exploratory workflow for hand skeletal aging research.

Radiomics can predict high-risk infection stones in patients with urolithiasis.

Radiomicsscore 9 Abdominal Radiology · IF≈2.62026-11-13H Ye, J Lin, Y Hong et al.

The study addresses preoperative identification of high-risk infection stones in patients with urolithiasis, a distinction that guides treatment decisions. It is a single-centre retrospective analysis of 576 patients using preoperative CT images. Radiomic features were extracted and reduced via multicollinearity elimination and Lasso regression to build a six-feature Rad-infection score, which was compared with routine clinical biomarkers using the DeLong test and decision curve analysis, and then combined with clinical predictors in a nomogram. The Rad-infection score achieved AUCs of 0.751 in training and 0.734 in validation, outperforming conventional biomarkers, while the radiomic nomogram reached an AUC of 0.867 (five-fold cross-validation mean 0.849), with gender, URBC, urine culture and the Rad-infection score as independent factors. The work is notable for showing that CT-based radiomics adds predictive value beyond standard clinical indicators for this stone subtype.

Reverse Reconstruction of the Patient-Specific Anatomy of the Knee: Deep Learning-Based Point Cloud Completion Model.

Bone and skeletalscore 9 The Journal of Arthroplasty · IF≈3.32026-11-13L Kong, J Liu, H Kang et al.

The problem addressed is reconstructing patient-specific knee bone anatomy from incomplete point clouds, such as those arising from clinically common bone defect patterns, to support personalized diagnosis and arthroplasty planning. The data comprised a CT database of 140 normal knees, from which 10 clinically common defect patterns (7 femoral, 3 tibial) were generated; the abstract does not state whether the data came from a single or multiple centres. Multiple deep learning point cloud completion methods were compared using virtual testing of morphological consistency (Chamfer Distance, Density-Aware Chamfer Distance, F-score) and anatomical parameter consistency, with the best method then tested in a preliminary clinical feasibility assessment of patients with unilateral post-traumatic osteoarthritis by comparing reconstructed affected-side models to intact contralateral knees. In virtual testing, the CRA-PCN model performed best, achieving Chamfer Distance below 0.45 mm across all defect patterns with anatomical parameter deviations within 0.5 mm for linear and 0.2° for angular measurements; in the clinical feasibility test, reconstructed morphology showed a Chamfer Distance of approximately 1.2 mm versus the contralateral side, and joint line angle restoration was best for extensive defects, with optimal deviations within 0.2° femorally and about 0.7° tibially. The work is relevant because it provides quantitative anatomical references for personalized arthroplasty and helps identify deformity origins in osteoarthritis patients, though the authors note that large-scale prospective validation is still required.

Machine learning prediction of meningioma grade using preoperative MRI: a large multicenter study.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Journal of Neurosurgery · IF≈3.92026-11-13SM Adil, TJ Zachem, P Warman et al.

Preoperative knowledge of intracranial meningioma grade could improve surgical planning, but reliable noninvasive prediction remains an unmet clinical need. The study used retrospective data from the BraTS Meningioma Challenge: 909 patients from 6 academic medical centers, split into 699 for training and internal validation and 210 for one-time external validation, with tumors segmented into three regions and radiomic features extracted across four MRI sequences. Eight machine learning algorithms were tested across 2889 candidate features, and the final model was a support vector machine using 20 features that predicted binarized WHO grade (1 vs 2 or 3). On unseen test data it achieved an AUROC of 0.824 (95% CI 0.755-0.883) with favorable calibration, and feature importance analysis showed that topological complexity added complementary predictive value to conventional radiomic features. The externally validated model is publicly available, and the inclusion of fractal and topological features alongside standard radiomics is what distinguishes it from prior work.

18F-FDG PET/CT radiomics for differentiating peripheral tuberculosis from lung cancer: development and validation.

Radiomicsscore 9 BMC Pulmonary Medicine · IF≈3.62026-08-19J Cheng, H Liu, X Zhu et al.

Distinguishing peripheral pulmonary tuberculosis from primary peripheral lung cancer is clinically difficult, and the study asks whether 18F-FDG PET/CT radiomics with machine learning can separate the two. The data come from a single-centre retrospective cohort of 346 patients (144 with peripheral pulmonary tuberculosis, 202 with lung cancer) imaged with pretreatment PET/CT and split 7:3 into training (n=242) and internal validation (n=104) sets, with a temporal split used only as a sensitivity analysis. Radiomic features were extracted from the scans and three classifiers (random forest, support vector machine, gradient boosting machine) were compared, with the final model built on six predictors: age, SUVmax, metabolic heterogeneity index (CV%), PET skewness, PET GLCM energy, and shape sphericity, all independent predictors in multivariate logistic regression (P<0.05). The contribution is a combined metabolic, textural, and shape PET/CT signature for this differential, though validation is internal and single-centre only.

Can radiomics outperform radiologists in predicting Ewing sarcoma response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy from longitudinal MRI?

Radiomicsscore 9late · published 2026-11-12 BMC Cancer · IF≈4.02026-11-12Ó Llorián-Salvador, D Rusche, HR Dürr et al.

Predicting histological response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy in Ewing sarcoma currently requires surgery, so an imaging-based early predictor could allow earlier treatment adaptation. The retrospective multicentre study used pre- and post-first-cycle MRI scans from 96 patients (mean age 19.9 ± 11.7 years; 41 women) treated between 2004 and 2020.
