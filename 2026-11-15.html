New trabecular bone score reference values for children and adolescents according to age and sex and their relationship with body size and fracture incidence.

Bone and skeletalscore 9 JBMR Plus · IF 3.0journal list2026-09-21DR Weber, HM Wasserman, G Gatineau et al.

Pediatric trabecular bone score (TBS) reference ranges needed updating after the manufacturer modified the TBS software algorithm to improve soft-tissue thickness correction and precision, and it was unclear whether new ranges would differ from the first pediatric dataset published in 2022 or how TBS relates to body size and fracture in healthy children. The study used 9898 lumbar spine DXA scans from healthy US children aged 5-20 years in the Bone Mineral Density in Childhood Study, a single large cohort, to generate age- and sex-specific reference ranges with TBS Insight v4.0. The new L1-L4 ranges differed from the prior software version (mean TBS 1.35 ± 0.10 vs 1.33 ± 0.12, p < .0001), with numerically improved precision (percent coefficient of variation 2.43%, 95% CI 2.18-2.74, vs 2.87%, 95% CI 2.58-3.23); TBS increased with age and differed by sex, and TBS Z-scores showed nonlinear associations with height Z-scores (peaking at r = 0.43 at 12 years in females and 0.34 at 14 years in males) and BMI Z-scores (r = 0.38 at 15 years in females and 0.31 at 15 years in males, all p < .0001). TBS Z-scores were not associated with incident low-to-moderate impact fracture over 6 years (hazard ratio 0.97, 95% CI 0.84-1.12). The work indicates that scans acquired with TBS Insight v4.0 should be interpreted against these updated pediatric references rather than the earlier ones, and that TBS in healthy children tracks growth and body size but did not predict fracture in this cohort.

A nomogram integrating clinical and radiological data to predict long-term infliximab efficacy in Crohn’s disease

Medical imaging MLscore 8 European Radiology · IF 4.7journal list2026-11-15Shi, Xuan, Chen, Xiaoshan, Rao, Shengxiang et al.

Loss of response to infliximab is common in Crohn's disease, and a reliable tool for predicting long-term efficacy is lacking. The retrospective study used computed tomography enterography imaging features plus baseline and pre-/postinduction clinical indicators from 165 Crohn's disease patients receiving regular infliximab, split into a 112-patient training cohort and a 53-patient validation cohort; the abstract does not state whether the data came from one or multiple centres. Candidate predictors were selected by LASSO and multivariable logistic regression to build a clinical-radiological nomogram, assessed with ROC analysis, decision curve analysis, calibration plots, and Kaplan-Meier analysis of predictor cut-offs. Erythrocyte sedimentation rate at week 14 and baseline maximum small bowel wall thickness were independent predictors, and the nomogram achieved AUCs of 0.834 (training) and 0.913 (validation) for loss of response, and 0.746 and 0.876 for uncomplicated and complicated loss of response, with ESR above 10.5 mm/h at week 14 or baseline SBWT-max above 7.5 mm associated with shorter response duration. The contribution is a validated tool combining a postinduction laboratory marker with a baseline imaging measurement to stratify patients by risk of uncomplicated versus complicated loss of response, supporting earlier therapy decisions.

This study addresses a clinically important question in inflammatory bowel disease management: predicting loss of response (LOR) to infliximab (IFX) in Crohn's disease patients, and distinguishing uncomplicated from complicated failure, to enable timely therapy switching. Three-dimensional automated muscle and adipose analysis to predict hepatic encephalopathy after transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt.

Medical imaging MLscore 8 European Radiology · IF 4.7journal list2026-11-15Y Zhao, M Huang, J Huang et al.

Overt hepatic encephalopathy limits the use of transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) in portal hypertension, and the authors asked whether automated 3D body composition analysis could improve pre-TIPS prediction of overt HE over conventional 2D manual assessment. The multicenter study included 593 eligible patients from seven institutions (training n=308; external validation 1 n=151; external validation 2 n=134), using L3-based 2D measurements and a fully automated 3D muscle and adipose assessment incorporating morphologic and radiomic features. Traditional 2D measurements showed poor reproducibility, with significant variation in 96.2% (25/26) of features, and the 3D Model significantly outperformed the 2D Model. The Integrated Model, combining 3D assessment with selected clinical variables, yielded AUCs of 0.78, 0.73, and 0.77 across the training and two external validation cohorts, exceeding Child-Pugh, MELD, ALBI, AMMON-OHE, and Lambda (AUCs 0.47-0.64; all p<0.05), with no significant subgroup differences. High-risk patients had 2- to 4-fold higher overt HE incidence, and the model reclassified 40.2% of patients with low pre-TIPS ammonia as high risk and 60.2% of those with high ammonia as low risk, supporting objective preprocedural risk stratification.

Associations between diabetic neuropathy and bone density and bone microarchitecture in type 2 diabetes: The Deep Phenotyping Study.

Bone and skeletalscore 9late · published 2026-11-14 Osteoporosis International · IF≈3.92026-11-14JL Graversen, AV Kvist, MB Sjogaard et al.

The study asks whether diabetic neuropathy is associated with poorer bone density, microarchitecture, and material properties in people with type 2 diabetes, and whether these associations differ by age. It is a cross-sectional analysis of 123 individuals with type 2 diabetes (50 with neuropathy, mean age 67.4 years; 73 without, mean age 62.2 years) assessed with DXA plus trabecular bone score, high-resolution peripheral quantitative CT, and microindentation, alongside neuropathy and autonomic function testing; the abstract does not state the number of centres. Associations were estimated with multivariable linear regression adjusted for age, sex, and BMI, with stratified analyses at age <65 and >=65 years. In participants under 65, neuropathy was associated with lower areal BMD at the total hip (beta = -0.15 g/cm2, 95% CI -0.24 to -0.06) and femoral neck (beta = -0.18 g/cm2, -0.26 to -0.09), lower TBS (beta = -1.56, -2.23 to -0.89), lower total, cortical, and trabecular volumetric BMD and trabecular bone volume fraction with higher cortical porosity at the tibia, and lower cortical volumetric BMD and trabecular number at the radius, while no significant associations were seen for bone material strength index or in those aged 65 and older. The work links a common diabetes complication to site-specific deficits in bone density and cortical and trabecular microarchitecture in younger adults with type 2 diabetes, suggesting a possible pathway to skeletal fragility that DXA alone would not capture.

Preoperative prediction of lymphovascular space invasion in early-stage cervical cancer using a deep learning-based 3D super-resolution MRI radiomics model.

Radiomicsscore 9late · published 2026-11-15 BMC Cancer · IF≈4.02026-11-15Y Guo, Y Ye, Q Mu et al.

Preoperative assessment of lymphovascular space invasion (LVSI) in early-stage cervical cancer is limited by the spatial resolution of standard MRI, which constrains radiomic feature quality. The retrospective study used T2-SPAIR and DWI MRI from 117 patients with early-stage cervical cancer (81 training, 36 validation), with the number of centres not stated in the abstract. A 3D Super-Resolution Generative Adversarial Network upsampled images 4-fold, radiomic features were extracted, and an SRI-based multimodal fusion model was compared with original-image and clinical models using AUC and decision curve analysis. Super-resolution improved SNR for DWI (P=0.012) and T2-SPAIR (P=0.030), and the SRI-Fusion model reached a validation AUC of 0.934 versus 0.828 for the original-image fusion model (P=0.044) and 0.626 for the clinical model (P=0.012), with SRI-DWI alone at 0.864 versus 0.727 for original DWI (P=0.041). The work indicates that GAN-based super-resolution can improve image quality and radiomic feature robustness, particularly for DWI, and that super-resolution-derived multimodal radiomics may improve preoperative LVSI prediction over conventional imaging and clinical models.

Deep dive (from abstract only)

This study addresses the preoperative prediction of lymphovascular space invasion (LVSI) in early-stage cervical cancer, a prognostic factor that guides adjuvant therapy but is difficult to assess non-invasively because standard MRI resolution limits visualization of fine intratumoral texture. The authors propose a pipeline in which a 3D Super-Resolution Generative Adversarial Network (SRGAN) upsamples T2-SPAIR and DWI sequences 4-fold, after which radiomic features are extracted from the super-resolution images (SRI) and combined into a multimodal fusion model. In a retrospective cohort of 117 patients (81 training, 36 validation), SR reconstruction improved SNR for both DWI (P=0.012) and T2-SPAIR (P=0.030). The SRI-DWI model outperformed the original-image DWI model in validation (AUC 0.864 vs 0.727, P=0.041), and the SRI-Fusion model reached the highest validation AUC of 0.934, exceeding OI-Fusion (0.828, P=0.044) and a clinical model (0.626, P=0.012). The genuinely novel element is the explicit coupling of generative super-resolution to radiomic feature robustness, with sequence-specific analysis suggesting DWI texture heterogeneity is better preserved. However, the small single-center sample, absence of external testing, and lack of ground-truth high-resolution reference images temper claims that SR adds true information rather than plausible hallucination; the work is best read as a proof-of-concept for SR-augmented radiomics rather than validated clinical utility.

  • Radiomic feature extraction from super-resolution images (SRI) versus original images (OI)
  • Multimodal SRI fusion model combining T2-SPAIR and DWI radiomic features
  • Comparators: OI-based radiomics models, single-sequence SRI/OI models, and a clinical model
  • Retrospective design; training/validation split; evaluation by AUC and Decision Curve Analysis

Validation

  • Internal split only: training (n=81) versus validation (n=36); no external or prospective validation
  • Metrics: AUC and Decision Curve Analysis; SNR comparisons with P-values
  • Pairwise AUC comparisons with reported P-values (e.g., DeLong-type testing implied but not specified in abstract)

Results

  • SNR improved after SR for DWI (P=0.012) and T2-SPAIR (P=0.030)
  • Validation AUC SRI-DWI 0.864 vs OI-DWI 0.727 (P=0.041)
  • Validation AUC SRI-Fusion 0.934, highest overall
  • SRI-Fusion vs OI-Fusion AUC 0.828 (P=0.044)
  • SRI-Fusion vs clinical model AUC 0.626 (P=0.012)

  • No external or prospective validation; generalizability untested
  • No ground-truth high-resolution images to confirm SR preserves rather than fabricates texture; hallucination risk unaddressed
  • Abstract-only report: feature selection, reproducibility, and calibration details unavailable

The new L1-L4 ranges differed from the prior software version (mean TBS 1.35 ± 0.10 vs 1.33 ± 0.12, p < .0001), with numerically improved precision (percent coefficient of variation 2.43%, 95% CI 2.18-2.74, vs 2.87%, 95% CI 2.58-3.23); TBS increased with age and differed by sex, and TBS Z-scores showed nonlinear associations with height Z-scores (peaking at r = 0.43 at 12 years in females and 0.34 at 14 years in males) and BMI Z-scores (r = 0.38 at 15 years in females and 0.31 at 15 years in males, all p < .0001). TBS Z-scores were not associated with incident low-to-moderate impact fracture over 6 years (hazard ratio 0.97, 95% CI 0.84-1.12). The work indicates that scans acquired with TBS Insight v4.0 should be interpreted against these updated pediatric references rather than the earlier ones, and that TBS in healthy children tracks growth and body size but did not predict fracture in this cohort.

Associations between diabetic neuropathy and bone density and bone microarchitecture in type 2 diabetes: The Deep Phenotyping Study.

Bone and skeletalscore 9late · published 2026-11-14 Osteoporosis International · IF≈3.92026-11-14JL Graversen, AV Kvist, MB Sjogaard et al.

The study asks whether diabetic neuropathy is associated with poorer bone density, microarchitecture, and material properties in people with type 2 diabetes, and whether these associations differ by age. It is a cross-sectional analysis of 123 individuals with type 2 diabetes (50 with neuropathy, mean age 67.4 years; 73 without, mean age 62.2 years) assessed with DXA plus trabecular bone score, high-resolution peripheral quantitative CT, and microindentation, alongside neuropathy and autonomic function testing; the abstract does not state the number of centres. Associations were estimated with multivariable linear regression adjusted for age, sex, and BMI, with stratified analyses at age <65 and >=65 years. In participants under 65, neuropathy was associated with lower areal BMD at the total hip (beta = -0.15 g/cm2, 95% CI -0.24 to -0.06) and femoral neck (beta = -0.18 g/cm2, -0.26 to -0.09), lower TBS (beta = -1.56, -2.23 to -0.89), lower total, cortical, and trabecular volumetric BMD and trabecular bone volume fraction with higher cortical porosity at the tibia, and lower cortical volumetric BMD and trabecular number at the radius, while no significant associations were seen for bone material strength index or in those aged 65 and older. The work links a common diabetes complication to site-specific deficits in bone density and cortical and trabecular microarchitecture in younger adults with type 2 diabetes, suggesting a possible pathway to skeletal fragility that DXA alone would not capture.
