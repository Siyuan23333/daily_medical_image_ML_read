Multi-phase liver CT virtual contrast enhancement and diagnosis based on phase-aware diffusion network.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging · IF 10.0journal list2026-11-10Z Di, W Zhao, R Ge et al.

Multi-phase contrast-enhanced CT is important for liver tumour diagnosis but exposes patients to extra radiation and is limited to those who can receive contrast, and existing virtual contrast enhancement methods struggle with detail-poor non-contrast CT and phase-specific feature variation. The work uses two liver CT datasets (the abstract does not give cohort sizes, sources, or whether they are single- or multi-centre) covering non-contrast and multi-phase contrast-enhanced images. The proposed PA-Diff model is a phase-aware diffusion network with phase-specific path allocation, combining independent diffusion paths with a shared encoder and decoder, plus cycle re-encoding learning that recovers target images and re-encodes them into complementary features for the loss during training; virtual contrast-enhanced images are then used for automatic hepatocellular carcinoma screening and microvascular invasion diagnosis. The abstract reports that experiments on both datasets show effective multi-phase enhancement and accurate HCC screening and MVI diagnosis but gives no quantitative metrics. Its contribution is coupling multi-phase virtual enhancement with downstream contrast-agent-free tumour diagnosis in a single framework.

This paper addresses the clinical problem that multi-phase contrast-enhanced CT (CECT), essential for liver tumor diagnosis, requires repeated contrast-agent administration and ionizing radiation and is restricted to patients who can tolerate contrast. Early identification of high-risk patients for tumor recurrence after liver transplantation in hepatocellular carcinoma is needed to guide personalized follow-up and intervention. The study used a retrospective cohort of 143 HCC patients with imaging, clinical data, and postoperative follow-up, though the abstract does not specify the imaging modality or whether the cohort was single- or multi-centre. A combined model was built on multichannel 2.5D deep learning using coronal, sagittal, and peritumoral slices of the largest tumor area, integrating clinical features, radiomics, and deep learning data, and evaluated with ROC, calibration, and decision curve analysis. The 2.5D deep learning model outperformed the 2D model (AUCs of 0.784/0.842, 0.799/0.843, and 0.824/0.827 versus 0.736/0.819, 0.751/0.818, and 0.724/0.803 in training/testing cohorts), and the combined model achieved C-index values of 0.789/0.826 with 1-, 2-, and 3-year AUCs of 0.842/0.931, 0.865/0.891, and 0.853/0.885, plus the best calibration consistency and clinical net benefit. The work indicates that combining multichannel 2.5D deep learning with radiomics and clinical features can support proactive, individualized intervention for high-risk post-transplant HCC patients.

Development of a Multimodal Model Combining Imaging and Clinical Data to Predict Bone Cement Leakage During Vertebroplasty for Fresh Thoracolumbar Compression Fractures.

Bone and skeletalscore 9 JOR Spine · IF≈4.12026-11-08W Choi, J Chen, J Qiu et al.

Bone cement leakage is a common complication of percutaneous vertebroplasty, but perioperative risk stratification using single-source clinical or imaging models remains limited. The multicenter study analyzed 486 treated vertebrae from 378 patients across three hospital campuses, using conventional CT imaging features, CT-based radiomics, and clinical variables, with the treated vertebra as the prediction unit and patient-grouped data partitioning. Logistic regression identified independent risk factors, and six machine-learning algorithms were used to build clinical, imaging, radiomics, and multimodal models evaluated by AUC, accuracy, F1 score, and decision curve analysis, with external and prospective validation and SHAP interpretation. Leakage occurred in 215 of 486 vertebrae (44.2%), and multimodal models achieved AUCs of 0.902-0.939 internally, 0.722-0.929 in external validation, and 0.834-0.925 in prospective validation, generally exceeding single-modality models, with cortical destruction, cement injection volume, and posterior wall involvement as top SHAP predictors. The work is notable for combining radiomics, imaging, and clinical data in a multimodal framework with both external and prospective validation to support individualized cement injection planning.

This study addresses perioperative risk stratification for bone cement leakage during percutaneous vertebroplasty in fresh thoracolumbar osteoporotic compression fractures, a complication with reported incidence of 5-80% and potential for neurological injury or pulmonary embolism. The authors retrospectively assembled a multicenter development cohort (486 treated vertebrae/378 patients from three campuses), split at the patient level into training (385 vertebrae) and internal test (101 vertebrae), plus an independent external cohort (50 vertebrae/45 patients) and a prospective cohort (60 vertebrae/50 patients). They extracted >1700 PyRadiomics features from semi-automatically segmented vertebral bodies on preoperative CT, reduced them via variance filtering, correlation analysis and LASSO (15 features), and combined them with conventional imaging signs (intravertebral cleft, cortical destruction, fracture type/severity, basivertebral foramen sign, posterior wall involvement) and clinical/surgical variables (BMI, BMD, puncture approach, cement volume). Six ML algorithms (XGBoost, RF, DT, GNB, KNN, SVM) were trained for clinical, imaging, radiomics and multimodal feature sets, with patient-grouped nested cross-validation (10 outer/5 inner folds), locked thresholds, patient-cluster bootstrap CIs, DeLong tests with Bonferroni correction, DCA, SHAP interpretation, a 12-feature site-sensitivity analysis, and a 108-configuration robustness analysis. Headline results: multimodal models outperformed single-modality models (internal test AUC 0.902-0.939; XGBoost 0.939), with external AUC 0.722-0.929 (XGBoost 0.929) and prospective AUC 0.834-0.925 (RF 0.925). Genuinely new elements are the combination of independent external and prospective validation with nested cross-validation, explicit quantification of center-related radiomics variability with a stable-feature sensitivity model, and a large locked-configuration robustness analysis; the modeling itself (tree ensembles on radiomics plus clinical variables) is incremental rather than methodologically novel.

Pre-treatment T2WI-based automated artificial intelligence system for predicting neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy response and prognosis in locally advanced rectal cancer: a multicenter study.

Radiomicsscore 9late · published 2026-08-11 BMC Medical Imaging · IF≈3.82026-08-11K Sun, S Lu, X Li et al.

Preoperative, noninvasive prediction of pathological complete response to neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy in locally advanced rectal cancer is clinically challenging, and the authors aimed to build an automated AI system from pre-treatment T2WI for response and prognosis. The multicenter retrospective study used 227 patients from Center 1 as the development cohort and 72 and 55 patients from two other centers as external validation cohorts 1 and 2. A 3D Swin-UNETR automatically segmented tumors, after which 1,132 PyRadiomics radiomic features and 2,048 deep learning features from a 3D ResNet-152 were extracted and combined with an end-to-end deep learning score in a multi-perspective fusion model. The fusion model reached AUROCs of 0.756 and 0.751 in the two external cohorts and stratified overall survival with p < 0.05 in all cohorts, whereas disease-free survival significance was inconsistent (p = 0.0084 development, p = 0.044 external cohort 2, p = 0.068 external cohort 1). The work is presented as exploratory, offering externally validated, moderate performance for a fully automated segmentation-to-prediction pipeline that could support clinical decision-making pending prospective confirmation.

Three-dimensional deep-learning age estimation from the fourth rib on high-resolution CT: An evaluative likelihood-ratio approach.

Bone and skeletalscore 9 Forensic Science International · IF≈3.52026-11-08Y Etli, A Özdil

Age estimation from the fourth rib has relied on İşcan's visual phase system, which requires physical bone and yields an ordinal category rather than a calibrated probability. The study used right fourth ribs from thin-section (1 mm) chest CT of 453 living adults (225 men, 228 women, 18.0–99.8 years), segmented, canonically posed, and encoded as multi-view RGB-D renderings, point clouds of the sternal end and complete rib, and twenty morphometric scalars. Sex-specific networks trained by label-distribution learning were evaluated on a frozen hold-out set with three seeds, reaching mean absolute error of 10.6 years in men and 10.4 in women (45–46% below a mean-age predictor; 9.0 and 8.4 years between ages 18 and 70), with informative likelihood ratios at every threshold from 30 to 70 years (Cllr < 1) and sex classification AUC of 0.966. The age signal was not confined to the sternal extremity used in phase scoring: in women the complete rib outperformed the sternal end (10.4 versus 14.6 years), while in men the two were equivalent. The work is notable for producing calibrated likelihood ratios from whole-rib 3D CT rather than ordinal phases, and it notes that 1 mm sections cannot resolve İşcan's fine relief, making higher-resolution surface capture a priority.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via elsevier)

This retrospective single-centre study asks whether the right fourth rib, the classical İşcan phase-scoring element, can yield automated adult age estimation from clinical thin-section chest CT, and whether the output can be expressed as calibrated forensic evidence rather than a point estimate. The authors segmented the right fourth rib from 453 adults (225 men, 228 women; 18.0–99.8 years) using AI-assisted 3D segmentation in ITK-SNAP with operator review, exported metric STL surfaces, and placed each in a canonical pose resolved from scanner patient coordinates. Four sex-specific learners were trained with label-distribution learning (Gaussian σ=3 y over 83 one-year bins, KL plus expectation-regression loss): a 20-scalar MLP baseline, a multi-view RGB-D ResNet-18 on the terminal 22 mm, a PointNet++ on the sternal-end cloud, and a PointNet++ on the whole-rib cloud. On a frozen 91-case hold-out, selected estimators reached MAE 10.6±0.5 y (men) and 10.4±0.7 y (women), 45–46% below a mean-age predictor and 4–5 y better than the scalar baseline; accuracy was 9.0/8.4 y within 18–70 y but collapsed beyond 70. Likelihood ratios were informative (Cllr 0.45–0.92) at thresholds 30–70 y. Genuinely new: the demonstration that the age signal is not confined to the sternal end (decisively so in women), the LR-based evaluative framing for rib age, and the identification of pose canonicalisation, not the network, as the dominant reproducibility bottleneck (ICC 0.872; 0.963 when poses agreed).

  • Repeatability subsample: 30 ribs re-segmented ~1 month later by the same operator (29 analysable); drawn from whole series, 20/29 seen in training

  • Metrics: MAE with 2000-sample bootstrap 95% CI, RMSE, R², Pearson r, bias, regression slope; decade and <70/≥70 subgroup breakdowns; results as mean±SD over three seeds

  • Deep learning vs scalar baseline: men 10.9–12.1 vs 14.5 y; women 10.4 vs 15.3 y (whole-rib vs scalars)
  • Sternal end vs whole rib: men equivalent (11.0±0.9 vs 10.9±0.5 y); women whole rib 10.4±0.7 vs 14.6±0.1 y (R² 0.63 vs 0.32)
