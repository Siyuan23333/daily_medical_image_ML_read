AI for reducing claims and compensation payments from missed fractures on radiographs.

Bone and skeletalscore 9late · published 2026-11-06 European Radiology · IF 4.7journal list2026-11-06M Tordjman, L Gracia, E Guillo et al.

Missed fractures on radiographs generate medicolegal claims and compensation payments, and the study asks whether an AI fracture-detection tool (BoneView) could have caught the fractures that were missed. The retrospective multicentre study drew on all compensated missed-fracture claims from the 38 university hospitals of the Greater Paris area (January 2013–December 2019), yielding 26 claimants with missed fractures plus 130 controls (5 per claimant) selected from a contemporary single-centre emergency department cohort, with 27/130 (20.8%) controls having fractures. Three radiologists of differing experience independently read the radiographs blinded, unassisted and AI-assisted after a 3-month washout, and sensitivity, specificity and accuracy were computed for readers with and without AI and for standalone AI, with compensation amounts used to estimate avoidable payouts. Standalone AI sensitivity was 80.8% (21/26) for claimants versus 96.3% (26/27) for controls (p = 0.10), and AI assistance raised expert reader sensitivity from 81.1% to 92.5% without reaching significance (p = 0.10). Total compensation was €282,850 (€10,878 per case), of which AI-assisted detection could theoretically have mitigated €265,314, a 93.8% reduction in institutional financial liability. The work links AI detection performance directly to real compensation data, framing missed-fracture AI as a medicolegal and cost-mitigation question rather than only a diagnostic-accuracy one.

Clinical Context-Dependent Value of bpMRI-Derived Imaging Representations for Prostate Cancer Risk Stratification.

Medical imaging MLscore 9late · published 2026-11-05 Journal of Imaging Informatics in Medicine · IF≈3.92026-11-05F Zou, H Xiong, J Chen et al.

The study asks whether bpMRI-derived imaging representations add value for prostate cancer risk stratification beyond clinical variables, particularly for patients in the PSA gray zone where PSA is uninformative. It used a dual-campus retrospective cohort of 536 patients with pathologically confirmed PCa who underwent pretreatment bpMRI, with Campus 1 split into training and internal test sets and Campus 2 as an independent external test cohort. Radiomics, vision transformer, and CLIP features were extracted from T2-weighted imaging, diffusion-weighted imaging, and ADC maps, and clinical and combined clinical-imaging models were evaluated with ROC, calibration, decision curve analysis, and SHAP. In the independent test cohort the combined model reached the highest AUC (0.921), close to the clinical baseline (0.907), while the radiomics model led imaging-only models (AUC 0.844); in the PSA gray zone clinical and combined performance dropped (AUCs 0.381 and 0.600) but CLIP achieved the highest AUC (0.785) with favorable net benefit. The results indicate that the value of imaging representations is context-dependent, with CLIP features offering complementary discrimination specifically when PSA-based information is weak.

Feasibility of a lumbar CT-to-MRI image translation framework using a conditional CycleGANs for fracture stage determination in osteoporotic compression fractures.

Bone and skeletalscore 9 European Spine Journal · IF≈2.12026-11-06J Hong, S Jeong, SH Hwang et al.

The problem is that MRI, which reliably indicates osteoporotic compression fracture stage, is expensive, slow, and not universally accessible, so the authors tested whether lumbar CT could be translated into MRI-like images to determine fracture stage. The data were retrospectively collected CT and MRI volumes from 652 patients with osteoporotic compression fractures who underwent both modalities within 2 weeks, split into 552 training and 100 independent test patients, with 800 vertebral bodies analyzed; the abstract does not state whether the cohort was single- or multi-centre. The method used segmentation-based rigid registration to align CT and MRI, then a two-stage framework: a 3D ResNet-based model generating vertebra-specific acute fracture maps from CT, followed by 3D conditional CycleGAN translation to T1-weighted contrast-enhanced MRI conditioned on acute stage. Acute fracture classification reached precision 0.906, recall 0.869, and F1-score 0.887 (667/678 non-acute and 106/122 acute correctly classified), while synthetic MRI showed modest similarity to real MRI (Normalized MAE 0.094 ± 0.024, PSNR 18.7 ± 1.7 dB, SSIM 0.38 ± 0.04) and reader similarity scores of 87.2% for the conditional versus 85.3% for the conventional CycleGAN. The work matters as a feasibility demonstration that CT-only data can support fracture-stage determination via conditional generative translation, though the modest image-similarity metrics indicate the synthetic MRI is not yet a substitute for real MRI.

This study addresses a practical clinical bottleneck in osteoporotic compression fracture (OCF) management: MRI is the reference standard for determining fracture acuity/stage, but it is costly, slow, and not universally accessible, whereas CT is widely available. The authors propose a two-stage pipeline that first detects vertebra-specific acute fracture maps from lumbar CT using a 3D ResNet-based model, then translates CT into T1-weighted contrast-enhanced MRI using a 3D conditional CycleGAN in which the acute-stage label conditions the generator. The rationale is that conditioning on acuity should preserve fracture-relevant morphology rather than producing generic synthetic MRI. In a retrospective cohort of 652 patients (552 train, 100 independent test; 800 vertebral bodies), acute fracture classification reached precision 0.906, recall 0.869, and F1 0.887 (106/122 acute and 667/678 non-acute correct). What is genuinely new is modest: the combination of stage-conditioned CT-to-MRI translation with downstream acuity classification and expert reading, rather than a methodological advance in GANs. The low SSIM and absence of a direct MRI-based classification comparator temper claims of clinical readiness; this is a feasibility demonstration, not validation.

Panognostics-Plus addresses the clinical need for automated dental charting and structured reporting from panoramic radiographs, where manual interpretation is time-consuming and requires dentomaxillofacial radiology expertise. The authors extend their prior anatomy-only Panognostics framework into a modular, hierarchical multi-task pipeline: YOLO detection models localize teeth, alveolar bone crest, maxillary sinus floor, dental treatments (implants, bridges, root canal treatments, crowns, fillings), and periapical lesions (radicular cysts, periapical granulomas) on full radiographs; ROI crops are then passed to YOLO segmentation models for pixel-level delineation. Preoperative Prediction of Lymph Node Metastasis in Rectal Cancer Using Multimodal Magnetic Resonance Imaging Radiomics Combined with Clinical Risk Factors.

Radiomicsscore 9 Academic Radiology · IF≈3.32026-11-06Y Hu, Y Chen

The study addresses preoperative prediction of lymph node metastasis in rectal cancer, a decision that guides treatment but is difficult to make from imaging alone. It retrospectively analyzed 129 pathologically confirmed rectal cancer patients who underwent surgery at a single hospital between 2017 and 2023, using preoperative pelvic T2-weighted and contrast-enhanced T1-weighted MRI plus clinical data, split 7:3 into training (n=90) and internal validation (n=39) cohorts. Tumors were manually segmented in 3D with ITK-SNAP, features were selected by variance filtering and LASSO, and five logistic regression models were compared (single-sequence radiomics, multimodal radiomics, multimodal plus clinical factors, and clinical factors alone). The combined multimodal radiomics and clinical model performed best in internal validation, with AUC 0.885 (95% CI 0.819-0.937), sensitivity 85.3%, specificity 82.1%, and accuracy 83.6%, significantly outperforming the other models (all P < 0.05), with T2WI entropy, T1CE peak enhancement, tumor volume, tumor size, CEA, and NLR as independent predictors. The work indicates that adding CEA and NLR to multimodal MRI radiomics improves prediction over imaging or clinical factors alone, though the single-center retrospective design means multicenter external validation is still needed.
