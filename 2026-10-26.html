A deep learning model for detection and staging of osteonecrosis of the femoral head on x-ray images: a retrospective, large-scale, multicentre study in China.

Bone and skeletalscore 9 The Lancet Digital Health · IF 24.1journal list2026-10-26Z Wei, S Hu, H Sun et al.

Accurate detection and Ficat-Arlet staging of osteonecrosis of the femoral head (ONFH) on x-rays typically requires specialist expertise, motivating an automated deep learning approach. The retrospective multicentre study used 20,722 anteroposterior hip x-rays (28,785 hips) from 16,399 patients across six Chinese tertiary hospitals (2015-2023), including ONFH, healthy, and radiographically similar confounding hips, with expert orthopaedist annotations as ground truth. The OsteoAID model, built on the YOLOv8 detection network, was compared against original YOLOv8, radiologists and orthopaedists on external test sets, and against MRI as the reference standard. On internal testing, staging accuracy rose from 87.42% to 96.44% and AUC from 88.54% to 96.94% versus YOLOv8, with detection mAP of 92.97% at IoU 50%; on external set I, staging accuracy was 96.60%, exceeding radiologists (74.96%) and junior orthopaedists (83.98%) and matching expert orthopaedists (96.54%, p=0.20), while model assistance improved junior orthopaedist and radiologist accuracy by 12.70 and 21.47 absolute points and reduced diagnostic time across all groups. Robustness held on degraded images and smartphone photographs (93.76% accuracy), and x-ray plus model approached MRI-level performance (96.63% accuracy, 97.33% AUC), supporting use as an accessible decision-support tool for triage and treatment planning.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via elsevier)

OsteoAID addresses a real clinical gap: x-ray is the universal first-line hip imaging modality, yet ONFH detection and Ficat-Arlet staging on plain films are subtle, expertise-dependent tasks, and prior AI work mostly separated ONFH from normal hips only, without differential diagnosis against confounding hip pathologies or staging. The authors built a YOLOv8-based detection network with three modifications (AKC2f geometric deformation adaptation, ACmix collaborative local/global feature fusion, Focaler-IoU regression loss) that jointly localises femoral head lesions and assigns a Ficat-Arlet stage from a single anteroposterior view. Training used 28,785 hips from 16,399 patients across six Chinese tertiary centres, deliberately retaining poor-quality images (improper exposure/positioning, photographs of films) and adding 4,635 hips with mimicking conditions plus 2,465 healthy hips. On the internal test set, staging accuracy rose from 87.42% (YOLOv8) to 96.44% and AUC from 88.54% to 96.94%; detection mAP@0.5 was 92.97%. On external set I, OsteoAID reached 96.60% staging accuracy, matching expert orthopaedists (96.54%, p=0.20) and exceeding junior orthopaedists (83.98%) and radiologists (74.96%). Genuinely new elements are the multidisease differential-diagnosis framing, simultaneous staging, validation on a deliberately degraded image set, and an MRI-referenced external set; the architecture itself is an incremental YOLO variant.

Datasets

  • Development/internal: 28,785 hips, 16,399 patients, six Chinese tertiary hospitals (PUMCH, Jishuitan, Shantou, Foshan, FAH-SYSU, GPPH); AP x-ray; private; 70% train/20% validation/10% internal test, patient-level split
  • External test I: 1,841 images, 3,185 hips, 1,233 ONFH patients (FAH-SYSU, Yuebei People's Hospital); AP x-ray; private; external test; expanded with 1,665 other-disease hips and 771 healthy hips
  • External test II: 248 challenging images, 444 hips, 209 patients, acquired before 2010 (earliest 2001), PUMCH; AP x-ray incl. photographs of films; private; external stress test
  • External test III: 103 images, 206 hips, 99 patients, FAH-SYSU, with paired MRI as reference standard; AP x-ray + MRI; private; external test
  • Reader study: 24 junior and 24 expert orthopaedists plus 3 radiologists reading external sets I and II, with and without AI assistance

Method

  • YOLOv8 object-detection backbone with feature fusion and detection head, repurposed for joint lesion localisation and Ficat-Arlet stage classification
  • AKC2f geometric deformation adaptation module for flexible spatial sampling across stages; ACmix local/global collaborative feature fusion; Focaler-IoU bounding-box regression loss for hard/easy sample balance
  • Trained with SGD using Ultralytics default loss coefficients; validation set used for convergence monitoring and architecture selection
  • Reference labels: consensus of two >15-year orthopaedists with third-senior adjudication (97.5% confident on AP x-ray alone); external set III labelled from MRI
  • Baselines: unmodified YOLOv8, junior/expert orthopaedists, radiologists, and AI-assisted clinicians

Validation

  • Retrospective internal test plus three independent external test sets (geographic, temporal/challenging-image, and MRI-referenced), patient-level splitting with no overlap
  • Metrics: accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, F1, AUC with 95% CIs; mAP@0.5 for detection; per-stage one-vs-rest analyses
  • Clinician comparison via t tests and McNemar's tests, p<0.05; misclassification review for systematic stage over/underestimation

Results

  • Internal test staging accuracy 96.44% (95% CI 94.73-97.17) vs 87.42% (85.88-89.20) for YOLOv8; AUC 96.94% vs 88.54%
  • Detection mAP@0.5 92.97% (91.73-94.43)
  • External set I staging accuracy 96.60% (94.27-97.81), vs experts 96.54% (p=0.20), juniors 83.98%, radiologists 74.96%
  • AI assistance raised junior orthopaedist accuracy by 12.70% absolute and radiologist accuracy by 21.47% (both p<0.0001); expert gain 0.74% (p=0.080) but reduced reading time
  • External set II (challenging images) accuracy 93.76% (93.09-95.82); external set III (MRI reference) accuracy 96.63% (95.68-97.86), AUC 97.33% (96.65-98.75)

Limitations

  • Retrospective, China-only cohort; generalisability to other populations and ethnic groups untested
  • Reference standard is expert x-ray consensus (with MRI only in external set III), risking label noise for early-stage disease
  • No explicit cross-site harmonisation; residual scanner/site bias possible; within-patient hip correlation not modelled hierarchically, so precision estimates may be optimistic
  • No performance disaggregation by sex or age; excludes ONFH coexisting with other hip conditions and post-surgical hips; no prospective evaluation

Availability

Model development code is publicly available; de-identified x-ray data available on reasonable request to the corresponding author, not publicly released.

Multicentre benchmark of PSMA PET/CT and mpMRI fusion for non-invasive clinically significant prostate cancer diagnosis

Medical imaging MLscore 9 npj Digital Medicine · IF 15.1journal list2026-10-26Chen, Jian, Liao, Zhangcheng, Wang, Yuhang et al.

Non-invasive diagnosis of clinically significant prostate cancer is difficult, and it is unclear how much PSMA PET/CT adds beyond multiparametric MRI or how best to fuse the modalities. The study used a real-world multicentre cohort of 527 patients from three hospitals with paired PSMA PET, CT, T2-weighted MRI and DWI plus pathology-confirmed biopsy labels, developing models in one centre (n=363) and testing them in two external centres (n=164). It compared frozen pretrained encoders, fine-tuned 2D and 3D CNNs and Vision Transformers, three fusion strategies, and an interpretable cross-modal Transformer. PSMA PET gave the largest incremental value, with its removal reducing AUC by 0.077 internally and 0.055 externally and receiving the highest attention attribution (alpha_PET = 0.45); PSMA PET/CT also outperformed mpMRI externally (AUC 0.753 vs 0.653), while excluding DWI improved external AUC by 0.079 amid inter-centre b-value heterogeneity. Intermediate fusion was most consistent internally, whereas late fusion and pretrained 2D models generalised better externally, making the benchmark useful for choosing modalities and fusion strategies that transfer across institutions.

MRI-based radiomics models for the preoperative prediction of massive intraoperative blood loss in spinal metastases patients.

Radiomicsscore 9 BMC Medical Imaging · IF≈3.82026-07-28H Deng, J Yan, J Zhang et al.

Preoperative prediction of massive intraoperative blood loss (MBL, defined as 2500 ml or more) in spinal metastasis surgery is needed to guide perioperative planning, but no reliable preoperative tool is described. The study enrolled 507 patients with spinal metastases, using T2WI and contrast-enhanced T1 MRI sequences for radiomic feature extraction; the abstract does not state whether the cohort came from one or multiple centres. Features were reduced with LASSO regression, and 11 machine learning models were compared, with AdaBoost selected as the radiomics model (AUC 0.775); a clinical model was built from independent risk factors (tumour vascularity, preoperative embolization, tumour location, surgical segments), and a combined radiomics-clinical model was evaluated with AUC, accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, F1, calibration curves and decision curve analysis, and visualized as a nomogram. The combined model achieved the best discrimination (AUC 0.818; 95% CI 0.7191-0.9165), exceeding the radiomics model (AUC 0.768) and the clinical model (AUC 0.583), with good calibration and the greatest net clinical benefit on decision curve analysis. The work indicates that adding MRI radiomic features to routinely available clinical variables improves preoperative MBL risk stratification over clinical factors alone, supporting individualized perioperative management.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via unpaywall-pdf)

This single-center retrospective study asks whether preoperative MRI radiomics can predict massive intraoperative blood loss (MBL, defined as >2500 mL) in patients undergoing surgery for spinal metastases, a setting where blood loss is highly variable (reported means 870–2180 mL) and currently estimated by surgeon experience. The authors enrolled 507 pathologically confirmed spinal metastasis patients (2020–2023) who had T2WI and contrast-enhanced T1WI; 2394 radiomic features were reduced via t-test/Mann-Whitney, Pearson correlation (>0.9 removal), greedy recursive deletion, and LASSO (10-fold CV) to 31 features (12 T2WI, 19 CET1). Eleven classifiers were benchmarked; the MLP (internal validation AUC 0.801) was selected as the radiomics signature. Four independent clinical predictors (tumor hypervascularity, preoperative embolization [protective, OR 0.24], lumbosacral location, surgical segment count) were combined with the signature. The combined model reached AUC 0.882 (training), 0.846 (internal validation), and 0.810 (independent test), modestly exceeding radiomics-only (0.862/0.801/0.793) and clinical-only (0.837/0.793/0.737) models, with a nomogram and favorable DCA. Genuinely new elements are limited: the dual-sequence (T2WI+CET1) radiomic characterization of tumor vascularity/heterogeneity for a blood-loss endpoint and the explicit fusion with clinical risk factors. The incremental gain over clinical variables is small (~0.07 AUC on test), and the heavy train-to-validation performance drop across classifiers signals overfitting; this is an incremental, single-center contribution rather than a methodological advance.

  • Training set: n=294 (cases Jan 2021–Jun 2023, 7:3 split), private
  • Internal validation set: n=126 (same period, 7:3 split), private
  • Independent test set: n=87 (remaining cases outside Jan 2021–Jun 2023 window), private, held-out test

  • Feature selection: normality-based t-test/Mann-Whitney (p<0.05), Pearson r>0.9 removal, greedy recursive deletion, LASSO with 10-fold CV yielding 31 features
  • Eleven classifiers benchmarked (LR, Naive Bayes, SVM, KNN, RF, Extra Trees, XGBoost, LightGBM, Gradient Boosting, AdaBoost, MLP) with 5-fold CV; MLP chosen as Rad-Signature
  • Clinical model via univariate (p<0.2) then multivariate stepwise logistic regression; combined model = Rad-Signature + 4 clinical predictors; nomogram visualization
  • Baselines: clinical-only model and radiomics-only model compared against combined fusion model

Validation

  • Internal random split (training vs internal validation) plus a temporally held-out independent test set from the same single center; no true external cohort
  • Metrics: AUC with 95% CI, accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, F1; calibration curves and decision curve analysis
  • DeLong test and net reclassification improvement (NRI) for pairwise model comparisons; two-sided p<0.05

Results

  • Combined model AUC: 0.882 (95% CI 0.826–0.939) training, 0.846 (0.733–0.959) validation, 0.810 (0.693–0.926) test
  • Radiomics-only (MLP) AUC: 0.862 training, 0.801 validation, 0.793 test; clinical-only AUC: 0.837, 0.793, 0.737
  • Test-set combined model: accuracy 0.816, sensitivity 0.625, specificity 0.859, PPV 0.500, NPV 0.910, F1 0.556
  • Clinical predictors: hypervascular tumor OR 2.43 (1.18–5.00, p=0.016); preoperative embolization OR 0.227 (0.074–0.699, p=0.010); lumbosacral location OR 7.51 (2.14–26.31); surgical segments OR 1.37 (1.03–1.83)
  • Severe overfitting in several classifiers: XGBoost training AUC 0.991 vs validation 0.665; SVM 0.944 vs 0.531; Naive Bayes best on validation (0.844)

  • Authors state: manual segmentation is time-consuming and subject to inter-observer variability; automated segmentation proposed for future work
  • Omitted: no true external cohort despite the 'independent test' label; test set drawn from the same institution and overlapping era
  • Omitted: low PPV (~0.27–0.59) and modest sensitivity (0.625 on test) limit clinical actionability; MBL prevalence only ~16%; no inter-/intra-observer reproducibility (ICC) analysis of features or segmentation; no reporting of calibration metrics (e.g., Brier score) or NRI/DeLong p-values in text

Multiparametric MRI radiomics model for preoperative prediction of IDH1 R132H immunopositivity status in adult diffuse Glioma.

Radiomicsscore 9 The Neuroradiology Journal · IF≈2.02026-10-26HD Nguyen, MHT Le, HT Tran et al.

Predicting IDH1 R132H immunopositivity status in adult diffuse gliomas preoperatively is clinically important for prognosis and treatment planning, and this study tests whether multiparametric MRI radiomics can serve as a non-invasive marker. The retrospective cohort comprised 94 adult glioma patients from a single centre, Viet Duc University Hospital, who underwent preoperative conventional MRI, DWI and DSC-MRI. Radiomics features were extracted from ADC and rCBV maps within the tumour region, filtered by correlation, and selected via LASSO (5 ADC and 10 rCBV features) to build logistic regression models. The ADC model reached an AUC of 0.875, the rCBV model 0.943, and the combined model 0.955 with 89.5% accuracy, 87.5% sensitivity and 90.9% specificity, though differences between models were not statistically significant. The work indicates that perfusion- and diffusion-derived radiomics may discriminate IDH1 R132H status, but the single-centre design and small held-out test set mean the estimates need external validation in larger prospective cohorts.

Prediction of Double-Expressor Lymphoma in Diffuse Large B-Cell Lymphoma Using a Non-invasive Model Based on [18F] FDG PET/CT and Stacking.

Radiomicsscore 9 Academic Radiology · IF≈3.32026-10-26J Liang, G Lu, Q Zhong et al.

The study addresses non-invasive identification of double-expressor lymphoma (DEL) in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), which otherwise requires immunohistochemistry. It retrospectively enrolled 143 patients (82 male, 61 female, median age 61.5 years) from a single period (January 2019 to July 2025) with baseline [18F] FDG PET/CT, split 7:3 into training and test sets at the patient level; the abstract does not state whether the cohort was single- or multi-centre. Clinical, radiomics and deep learning features were extracted, selected via LASSO, and combined into individual and fusion models, with a ViT_KNN deep learning model, late fusion via Stacking_LR, and interpretation through SHAP and a nomogram. On the test set, ViT_KNN reached an AUC of 0.936 (95% CI 0.856-1.000), above clinical (0.857) and radiomics (0.706) models, while Stacking_LR achieved the best performance (AUC 0.948, 95% CI 0.865-1.000, accuracy 95.3%, specificity 100%, sensitivity 88.2%), with calibration and decision curve analysis supporting clinical net benefit. The work is notable for combining deep learning radiomics with clinical data through a stacking ensemble and for providing an interpretable nomogram for DEL risk stratification.
