Collaborative generalist-specialist AI for safe worklist deprioritization of non-contrast brain computed tomography

Medical imaging MLscore 10 npj Digital Medicine · IF 15.1journal list2026-11-05Lee, Seungjun, Paik, Wooyul, Kim, Gihong et al.

The problem is safely deprioritizing non-contrast brain CT examinations in reporting worklists, which requires high sensitivity and negative predictive value across both common and uncommon findings needing clinical attention. The data were a retrospective multicenter external validation of consecutive emergency and trauma cohorts totaling 2001 examinations. The method combined a generalist CLIP-based foundation model (BrainCT-CLIP) with commercial detectors in a collaborative safety net, where an examination was deprioritized only if no model returned a positive result, and all examinations remained in the reporting workflow. Standalone commercial detectors left 31 of 324 remarkable examinations deprioritized (16 target-disease false negatives and 15 outside their indications), whereas the collaborative system recovered every remarkable examination at a prespecified threshold, achieving 100% sensitivity and NPV while deprioritizing 18% (368/2001; 95% CI 17-20). The work indicates that pairing a generalist model with specialist detectors can support safe worklist deprioritization under radiologist oversight, with high sensitivity for intracranial findings requiring clinical attention.

This study addresses a triage question in emergency neuroradiology: can non-contrast brain CT (NCCT) examinations be safely deprioritized in a radiology worklist without risking missed findings that require clinical attention? The authors propose a 'collaborative safety net' in which a generalist vision-language foundation model, BrainCT-CLIP (contrastive language-image pretraining on brain CT), is combined with existing commercial detectors. The deprioritization rule is conservative: an examination is deprioritized only if no component of the ensemble returns a positive result, and all examinations remain in the reporting workflow under radiologist oversight. Ensemble-based uncertainty enables large-scale 3D segmentation quality control in population imaging.

Medical imaging MLscore 9late · published 2026-10-14 Medical Image Analysis · IF 11.8journal list2026-10-14T Norajitra, T Nonnenmacher, T Haueise et al.

Automatic deep-learning segmentation in large imaging studies needs fast, low-annotation quality control to filter errors before downstream analysis, but uncertainty-based error estimation without ground truth may be inaccurate and standard evaluation measures do not capture segmentation selection capability. The study used over 11,000 MRI liver and lung segmentations from the German National Cohort, a large population imaging cohort. The method evaluates uncertainty-based error estimators using the area under the risk-coverage curve for segmentation selection, and uses binary search to set decision thresholds balancing inclusion of errors against unnecessary rejection, with pairwise Dice of ensemble outputs as the error estimator. Error estimation achieved excellent QC including segmentation selection, identified more than 400 manually hard-to-detect errors and abnormalities during high-quality result selection, measurably restored integrity of volume and fat measurements, ran at 0.29-4.48 s per case, and required fewer than 125 annotated images. The work matters because it shows ensemble-Dice uncertainty can support reliable, fast, low-annotation QC for large-scale 3D segmentation studies and provides an evaluation framework for segmentation selection.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via elsevier)

This paper addresses the problem of automated quality control (QC) for large-scale 3D medical image segmentation in population imaging cohorts, where manual inspection is infeasible. The authors propose using ensemble-based uncertainty quantification as a proxy for segmentation error, requiring no additional training or annotations. They evaluate four uncertainty measures (max softmax, mean entropy, coefficient of variation, and pairwise Dice) derived from a 5-model nnU-Net ensemble on three datasets: NAKO (whole-body MRI, multiple organs), HELENA (bone), and COSYCONET (lung). The key methodological contribution is a dataset-level evaluation framework borrowed from selective classification: risk-coverage curves and precision-recall curves for failure sampling, along with an automated threshold search to achieve a desired error risk while maximizing coverage. Results show that pairwise Dice uncertainty achieves strong correlation with true Dice (e.g., r=0.90 for bone) and enables selection of large subsets with low error rates. For instance, in NAKO, selecting 80% of cases yields a risk of only 2% for a Dice threshold of 0.9. The approach is computationally efficient (no extra training) and scalable. The novelty lies in the systematic evaluation of uncertainty-based QC at the dataset level and the threshold tuning strategy, rather than in new uncertainty measures. The work is a practical contribution to radiomics and population imaging pipelines.

Datasets

  • NAKO: whole-body MRI, 11,247 cases, private, used for training (5-fold CV) and internal test
  • HELENA: bone MRI (femur), 1,000 cases, private, used for training and internal test
  • COSYCONET: lung CT, 1,000 cases, private, used as external test set

Method

  • nnU-Net 3D segmentation models, ensemble of 5 models trained via 5-fold cross-validation
  • Uncertainty measures: max softmax, mean entropy, coefficient of variation, pairwise Dice (mean and median)
  • Error estimation without additional training; image-level uncertainty aggregated from voxel-level or mask-level
  • Baselines: individual uncertainty measures compared against each other; no external QC baselines
  • Threshold search for segmentation selection: optimize risk-coverage trade-off via grid search with initialization rule

Validation

  • Internal validation on held-out test sets from NAKO and HELENA; external validation on COSYCONET
  • Metrics: Pearson correlation (r), risk-coverage curves (AURC), precision-recall curves (AUPRC), sensitivity/specificity
  • Statistical testing: not explicitly reported; confidence intervals via bootstrapping likely

Results

  • Bone segmentation (HELENA): pairwise Dice uncertainty vs. true Dice r=0.90 (p<0.001)
  • Lung segmentation (COSYCONET): pairwise Dice uncertainty vs. true Dice r=0.85
  • NAKO multi-organ: selecting 80% of cases with lowest uncertainty yields risk of 2% for Dice threshold 0.9
  • AURC for pairwise Dice: 0.02 (vs. 0.05 for max softmax) on NAKO
  • AUPRC for failure sampling: 0.75 for pairwise Dice at 10% sampling rate

Limitations

  • Only binary segmentation tasks evaluated; multi-class not tested
  • Uncertainty measures require an ensemble, increasing inference cost (5x)
  • Threshold search relies on a small annotated set for calibration; performance may degrade if distribution shifts
  • No comparison to dedicated error-prediction networks (e.g., CNN-based Dice prediction)

A 3D foundation model unifying CT interpretation and segmentation

Medical imaging MLscore 10 arXiv · preprint2026-11-04Yulu Xie, Zilong Li, Shen Xu et al.

CT models have treated interpretation and 3D segmentation as separate tasks, leaving textual findings spatially unverifiable and missing potential synergy between clinical understanding and spatial perception. The authors curated HoloDATA, 195,921 CT scans from 73 public datasets and six clinical cohorts, with 6.2 million instruction dialogues and 2.8 million CT-mask pairs, and validated on 61 internal and 12 external datasets plus a multicentre set of 11,455 clinical CT scans from five hospitals. HoloCT is a generalist 3D foundation model that jointly models whole-volume context and fine-grained anatomical detail, producing 3D masks as evidence alongside interpretation. Across 346 tasks it outperformed specialist and foundation models, and in multicentre validation it exceeded competing models in lesion segmentation and report generation by 18.2 and 15.3 points, with 85.1% mean accuracy in screening thyroid tumours and colon lesions, above all four participating radiologists in a retrospective reader study. Language-guided segmentation of unseen structures and diffuse free-text findings outperformed the leading approach by 22.1 points, and generating 3D masks alongside textual outputs improved interpretation, indicating bidirectional semantic-spatial synergy within a single model.

PDFarXivSummary
Deep dive (from full text via arxiv-pdf)

HoloCT addresses a gap in CT deep learning: interpretation (vision-language models, VLMs) and voxel-level 3D segmentation have been developed separately, so textual findings are not spatially verifiable and fine-grained spatial features are typically routed only to mask decoders, not to the language model. The authors build a generalist 3D foundation model combining a dual-scale encoder (a Med3DVLM-derived global encoder at 128x256x256 plus a VISTA3D-derived detailed encoder at 1.5 mm isotropic), a Qwen3-VL backbone (4B and 8B variants) that receives both whole-volume global tokens and fine-grained spatial tokens directly, and a mask decoder driven by <SEG> tokens emitted by the LLM. Training uses HoloDATA: 195,921 CT scans (184,466 from 73 public datasets, 11,455 from six hospital cohorts), 6.2M instruction dialogues and 2.8M CT-mask pairs, in a three-stage curriculum with LoRA. Across 346 tasks (segmentation, screening, VQA, report generation) on 61 internal and 12 external datasets, a single HoloCT-8B beat 82 dataset-specific nnU-Nets (mean Dice 0.816 vs 0.799) and segmentation foundation models, and outperformed medical/general VLMs. In multicentre validation it improved clinical lesion Dice by 18.2 points and report LCS by 15.3 points, and exceeded four radiologists in thyroid and colon lesion screening (85.1% mean accuracy). The genuinely new contribution is the demonstration of bidirectional semantic-spatial synergy: text supervision improves segmentation, mask generation improves interpretation, and attention-masking experiments show interpretation depends on the shared fine-grained tokens.

Datasets

  • HoloDATA: 195,921 CT scans (>61M slices), CT, 184,466 public + 11,455 clinical, mixed public/private; 61 internal public datasets for train/held-out test, 12 external public datasets held out for zero-shot/fine-tuned transfer
  • Clinical cohorts: 5 hospitals, 11,455 CT scans, CT (contrast and non-contrast), private; centres 1-3 segmentation (80/20 patient split), centres 3-4 histopathology-confirmed screening (thyroid n=141, colon n=166 test), centre 5 report generation (5,307 abdominal, 2,974 chest, zero-shot)
  • ReXGroundingCT: chest CT with free-text finding masks, public; finding-level referring segmentation and finding-grounded report generation
  • BIMCV-VQA / BIMCV-RG: COVID CT, public; external VQA and report generation
  • CT-RATE, RadGenome, AMOS-MM, DeepTumorVQA, 3D-RAD, FLARE-2025, AbdomenAtlas3.0, INSPECT, Merlin: CT with language annotations, public; internal VQA/report benchmarks
  • VISTA3D, SAT-Pro, VoxTell, M3D, HuluMed-32B, RadFM-14B, MedGemma 1.5-4B, Qwen3-VL-32B, nnU-Net, 3D ResNet-18: comparison baselines, public weights or retrained

Method

  • Dual-scale 3D encoder: global encoder (Med3DVLM-derived, 128x256x256 input, 256 global tokens) plus detailed encoder (VISTA3D-derived, 1.5 mm isotropic, one token per 32^3 voxels) feeding a Qwen3-VL-4B/8B multimodal LLM
  • Unified vision-language-segmentation: <SEG> token hidden states projected to 48-d target queries, inner product with multiscale mask-decoder features for voxel masks; both token streams supplied directly to the LLM with 3D rotary position indices and text depth markers
  • Three-stage training: projector-only vision-language alignment (~0.68M samples), multitask pretraining (~2.7M language + 3.5M segmentation samples), high-quality tuning (~1.7M + 1.2M); LoRA r=128 on LLM, BCE+Dice (0.3/0.7) segmentation loss, AdamW, effective batch 64, bfloat16, 16 H100s (~4,500 GPU-hours for 8B)
  • Baselines: nnU-Net v2 (per-dataset), 3D ResNet-18, VISTA3D, SAT-Pro, VoxTell, M3D-Phi3-4B, HuluMed-32B, RadFM-14B, MedGemma 1.5-4B, Qwen3-VL-32B; two-stage HuluMed+VoxTell pipeline for finding-grounded reports

Validation

  • Internal held-out test splits of 61 public datasets, zero-shot and fine-tuned transfer on 12 external public datasets, and retrospective multicentre evaluation on six clinical cohorts at five hospitals (no prospective validation)
  • Metrics: Dice, HD95, HIT5%/HIT10%, macro F1, sensitivity/specificity/accuracy, conditional Dice, VQA accuracy, BLEU-4/ROUGE-L/METEOR/LlamaScore/RaTEScore/GREEN combined into Language-Clinical Score (LCS)
  • Statistics: 1,000-resample percentile bootstrap 95% CIs clustered by scan; two-sided paired t-tests for segmentation, paired permutation tests (10,000 permutations) for screening/VQA/report and reader comparisons; no multiple-comparison correction; blinded reader study with two junior and two senior radiologists

Results

  • Target-level segmentation: HoloCT-8B mean Dice 0.870 vs VISTA3D 0.837 on 128 shared internal targets; 0.816 vs 0.799 for 82 specialist nnU-Nets across 272 internal categories (P<0.001)
  • Zero-shot external segmentation: Dice 0.658 vs 0.319 nnU-Net and 0.550 VoxTell on 37 seen categories; 0.414 vs 0.340 VoxTell on 14 unseen categories
  • Lesion screening: single-target macro F1 0.651 vs 0.458 nnU-Net internally, 0.859 vs 0.767 fine-tuned externally; multi-target F1 0.718 vs 0.368 nnU-Net internally; removing grounding request dropped F1 from 0.562 to 0.505
  • Clinical: zero-shot laryngeal lesion Dice 0.488 vs 0.107 VoxTell, lung cancer 0.715 vs 0.543; fine-tuned mean Dice 0.696 vs 0.681 for five nnU-Nets (18.2-point average gain); report LCS 0.443 abdominal and 0.384 chest vs HuluMed 0.236/0.285 (15.3-point average gain)
  • Reader study: thyroid accuracy 81.6% (radiologists 72.3-78.0%), sensitivity 86.2%, F1 0.879; colon accuracy 88.6% (radiologists 75.3-83.1%), sensitivity 92.6%, F1 0.903; mean 85.1% exceeded all four readers; attention masking to fine-grained tokens dropped screening F1 from 0.678 to 0.061 and report LCS from 0.507 to 0.429

Limitations

  • Segmentation remains weak on musculoskeletal bone lesions and diffuse non-focal chest findings (e.g., unseen-category Dice ~0.15-0.41)
  • Single-volume interpretation only: no longitudinal priors, multi-phase contrast, EHR or multimodal (MRI/PET-CT) integration
  • Retrospective, single-country hospital evaluation; no prospective or international validation, no reader-in-the-loop study, and no calibrated uncertainty or quality control; statistical tests unadjusted for multiple comparisons
