A Robust Physics-Informed Implicit Neural Representation for Speed-of-Sound Image Reconstruction in USCT.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging · IF 10.0journal list2026-11-17Z Wang, W Yan, Z Li et al.

Full-waveform inversion for speed-of-sound reconstruction in ultrasound computed tomography is severely non-convex, so traditional discrete grid-based methods often fall into local minima and produce anatomical artifacts that limit clinical use. The work is validated on numerical phantoms and in vivo breast and musculoskeletal data, with comparisons against MRI; the abstract does not state cohort sizes or whether the data are single- or multi-centre. The proposed POLARIS framework is an unsupervised continuous neural field inversion method that uses spatial continuity as implicit regularization, with a scale-aware radially-modulated polar encoding matched to the circular acquisition geometry, anatomical boundary priors extracted from acoustic signals, and stochastic shot-batched optimization. DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from abstract only)

This paper addresses the clinical need for quantitative speed-of-sound (SOS) maps in ultrasound computed tomography (USCT), where full-waveform inversion (FWI) is the reference method but suffers from severe non-convexity: discrete grid-based solvers frequently converge to local minima, producing anatomical artifacts that block clinical translation. The authors propose POLARIS, an unsupervised continuous neural-field inversion framework that uses spatial continuity as an implicit regularizer to stabilize FWI. Three design elements are central: (1) a scale-aware, radially-modulated polar encoding matched to the circular USCT acquisition geometry, intended to correct radial-angular sampling non-uniformity; (2) extraction of anatomical boundaries from acoustic signals via the deterministic coupling background velocity, used as a structural prior to preserve tissue integrity; and (3) stochastic shot-batched optimization that exploits global parameter coupling to update the SOS field from subsets of emissions and accelerate convergence. The headline claim is higher accuracy and structural fidelity than competing methods on numerical and in vivo breast and musculoskeletal data, with MRI-confirmed anatomical consistency and robust performance under sparse-view acquisition, suggesting reduced measurement requirements. Because only the abstract is available, quantitative details (error metrics, ablation magnitudes, statistical tests) cannot be verified. The genuinely new elements appear to be the polar, scale-aware encoding tailored to circular-array USCT and the use of the coupling background velocity as a data-derived structural prior within an unsupervised neural-field FWI; the shot-batching scheme is a practical acceleration rather than a conceptual advance. Claims of clinical readiness remain unsubstantiated without full-text numbers and prospective validation.

Deep learning retinal vascular biomarker predicts preeclampsia in a multicenter prospective cohort

Medical imaging MLscore 9 npj Digital Medicine · IF 15.1journal list2026-11-17Zhou, Tianfan, Feng, Wei, Liu, Huanrong et al.

Preeclampsia causes maternal and perinatal morbidity and mortality, but noninvasive screening for it remains limited. The study used prospectively collected multicenter data from 8,616 pregnant individuals, combining retinal photographs with maternal clinical factors and mean arterial pressure for retrospective model development and evaluation. The Retina-PE model fuses these modalities via cross-attention to predict subsequent preeclampsia occurrence and early-onset or preterm preeclampsia within one or four weeks. Development-dataset image-level AUCs were 0.853 and 0.708 for preeclampsia at 24–28 and 28–32 weeks, 0.871 and 0.791 for 1- and 4-week preterm preeclampsia, and 0.979 and 0.862 for 1- and 4-week early-onset preeclampsia, with external-validation AUCs of 0.728 and 0.815, 0.801 and 0.801, and 0.776 and 0.766 respectively, though incremental performance was task-specific and patient-level analyses had few events in some tasks. The work supports prospective evaluation of retinal imaging as a noninvasive tool for maternal risk stratification.

One-Step Rectified Flow Reconstruction with Spectral Weighting for Limited-angle Cardiac CT.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging · IF 10.0journal list2026-11-17S Han, C Fang, B Morovati et al.

Limited-angle cardiac CT reconstruction suffers from severe directional artifacts and resolution loss caused by missing frequency wedges, and existing diffusion-based methods suppress these artifacts well but need costly iterative sampling. The study uses XCAT simulations and clinical cardiac CT data, though the abstract does not state cohort sizes or whether the clinical data are single- or multi-centre. The proposed method is a rectified-flow framework trained with paired supervision that learns a deterministic transport from artifact-contaminated FDK reconstructions to full-view references, combined with 2.5D slice-context modeling and a reconstruction-aware spectral loss applied to an approximate endpoint reconstruction. One-step inference (a single network evaluation) is used, and experiments report better quantitative performance and substantially reduced inference time than the compared baselines, with a radiologist's qualitative review suggesting improved visualization of motion-sensitive coronary structures with less motion blur. The contribution is fast, single-evaluation limited-angle reconstruction that embeds frequency-domain priors in the training objective rather than relying on iterative sampling.

Quantitative CT Phenotypes and Prognosis in Combined Pulmonary Fibrosis and Emphysema.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Radiology · IF 17.6journal list2026-11-01H Wang, Y Ni, Y Deng et al.

Combined pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema (CPFE) has heterogeneous outcomes, and visual assessment or separate measurement of emphysema and fibrosis does not directly identify which structural component predominates in an individual patient. This secondary analysis of a prospective multicenter observational study included 1583 patients with CPFE (median age 65 years; 1186 men) enrolled between January 2017 and December 2024, using high-resolution CT. A fixed commercial artificial intelligence workstation automatically segmented the lungs and quantified emphysema and fibrosis, classifying patients as fibrosis-dominant when fibrosis percentage was at least the emphysema percentage and as emphysema-dominant otherwise. Of the cohort, 823 (52%) were fibrosis-dominant and 760 (48%) emphysema-dominant; the fibrosis-dominant group had higher fibrosis percentage (median 18% vs 10%; P < .001) and the emphysema-dominant group higher emphysema percentage (median 7% vs 6%; P < .001). During a median follow-up of 2.59 years with 513 deaths, the fibrosis-dominant phenotype was associated with higher all-cause mortality after adjustment (hazard ratio 1.36 [95% CI: 1.13, 1.62]; P < .001).

This is an abstract-only summary; the full text was not available, so detail is limited to the abstract. The clinical question is whether quantitative CT can objectively split combined pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema (CPFE) into fibrosis-dominant versus emphysema-dominant phenotypes, and whether these phenotypes carry distinct prognosis. DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from abstract only)

This paper addresses a central practical obstacle in computational virtual staining: models trained on one staining/acquisition domain often fail to generalize to heterogeneous staining protocols and scanner conditions, and are sensitive to nuisance variation. The clinical question is whether virtual staining can be made reliable enough for workflow use across sites and preparation conditions. The authors propose a causal-intervention-based training framework that, from a causal standpoint, separates pathology-relevant features from pathology-irrelevant nuisance variations. Rather than enumerating specific nuisance factors (stain intensity, color shifts, acquisition artifacts), they introduce a unified strategy to capture nuisance-related variation without specifying its form, then apply causal intervention during training to suppress those nuisance features and force reliance on stable pathology-relevant signals. The framework is model-agnostic and plug-and-play with existing virtual staining architectures. The abstract reports extensive experiments across multiple datasets, staining tasks, evaluation protocols, and settings showing effectiveness, but provides no quantitative results, so the headline numbers cannot be assessed. Genuinely new: framing virtual staining generalization/robustness as a causal nuisance-suppression problem with a model-agnostic intervention module, rather than domain adaptation or stain normalization. The claim of being among the first causal treatments of virtual staining is plausible but unverified from the abstract alone.

A Registration-Free and Slope-aware Framework for CBCT-based Dental Implant Planning.

Medical imaging MLscore 8 IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging · IF 10.0journal list2026-11-17X Yang, J Ren, X Li et al.

Automated dental implant planning from CBCT currently depends on training labels obtained by registering pre- and post-implantation scans, which suffers from low registration accuracy and scarce paired data. The authors introduce ImplantFairy, described as the first large-scale public dataset for dental implant planning, containing 1,622 implant-specific CBCT samples; the abstract does not state the number of centres or scan sources.
