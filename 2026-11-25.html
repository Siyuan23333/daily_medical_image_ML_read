The autoPET3 challenge: Automated lesion segmentation in whole-body PET/CT - Multitracer multicenter generalization.

Medical imaging MLscore 10late · published 2026-11-19 Medical Image Analysis · IF 11.8journal list2026-11-19J Dexl, K Jeblick, A Mittermeier et al.

The challenge addresses automated lesion segmentation in whole-body PET/CT under compositional generalization, where models must handle tracer-center combinations not seen during training. Training data comprised 1014 [18F]-FDG PET/CT studies from University Hospital Tübingen and 597 [18F]/[68Ga]-PSMA PET/CT studies from LMU Munich (described as the largest public annotated PSMA PET/CT dataset), with a held-out test set of 200 studies spanning four tracer-center combinations, two of them unseen pairings, making the benchmark multi-centre. Seventeen teams submitted 27 algorithms, mostly nnU-Net-based 3D networks with PET/CT channel concatenation, and a separate data-centric award fixed the baseline model to isolate data handling strategies. The top algorithm reached a mean DSC of 0.66, FNV of 3.18 mL, and FPV of 2.78 mL across all four test conditions, improving DSC by 8% and reducing false-negative volume by 5 mL versus the provided baseline, with stable top-tier ranking under bootstrap resampling and alternative ranking schemes. The authors conclude that in-domain multitracer segmentation is sufficient and likely near reader agreement, that generalization to unseen tracer-center combinations remains open and is driven mainly by systematic volume overestimation, and that case heterogeneity and difficulty explain performance variation more than algorithm choice among top teams.

Deep dive (from full text via arxiv)

autoPET3 (MICCAI 2024) benchmarks whole-body PET/CT lesion segmentation under a compositional generalization design: training data pair tracers with centers (FDG from Tübingen, PSMA from Munich), while the 200-study test set includes both seen pairings and unseen cross-center composites (FDG-Munich, PSMA-Tübingen). Training comprised 1,014 FDG studies (UKT) plus 597 PSMA studies (LMU; the largest public annotated PSMA PET/CT set to date). A second, data-centric award category fixed the model (MONAI nnU-Net replica) and allowed only pipeline changes. Seventeen teams submitted 27 algorithms, almost all 3D nnU-Net variants with PET/CT channel concatenation; 11 used a single multitracer model and 4 used tracer-routing with expert networks. The winner (LesionTracer A) reached mean DSC 0.66, FNV 3.18 mL, FPV 2.78 mL across all four conditions, +8% DSC and ~5 mL less FNV than the provided baseline. Genuinely new contributions are the public PSMA dataset, the explicit compositional (tracer × center) evaluation, the data-centric track, and unusually thorough post-hoc analyses: bootstrap and multi-scheme ranking stability, mixed-effects variance partitioning (patient 61% vs team 1.3% of DSC variance), lesion-level detection/merge/split decomposition, volume-bias analysis, and a lacrimal-gland cropping ablation. The main scientific message is that in-domain performance is near reader agreement, whereas unseen composites fail mainly through systematic volume overestimation and unseen physiological uptake.

Datasets

  • FDGtrain (autoPET/TCIA, UKT): [18F]FDG PET/CT, 1,014 studies/900 patients, 1 scanner, public, training
  • PSMAtrain (LMU, TCIA + separate challenge repository): [18F]PSMA-1007 and [68Ga]PSMA-11 PET/CT, 597 studies/378 patients, 3 scanners, public, training
  • FDGUKT test: 50 studies, in-domain (seen tracer-center), private held-out test
  • PSMALMU test: 50 studies, in-domain, private held-out test
  • FDGLMU test: 50 studies, unseen composite (FDG at LMU), private held-out test
  • PSMAUKT test: 50 studies, unseen composite (PSMA at UKT), private held-out test
  • Reader subset: 25 re-read FDGUKT and 25 FDGLMU (autoPET1 second reads), 25 PSMALMU junior-reader annotations; none for PSMAUKT

Method

  • Predominantly nnU-Net (12/15 AC1 teams), 3D networks, PET+CT channel concatenation; one 2D U-Net
  • Two multitracer paradigms: single global model (11 teams) vs tracer-classifier routing to tracer-specific experts (4 teams, e.g., ResNet18 on PET MIPs)
  • Winner LesionTracer: nnU-Net ResEnc L, dual lesion+organ heads, 192^3 patches, misalignment augmentation, multimodal pretraining, 5-fold ensemble, dynamic TTA
  • Other design variations: TotalSegmentator organ priors as auxiliary labels, ResEnc M/L/XL backbones, per-image vs global PET normalization, SUV post-thresholding
  • Baselines: out-of-the-box nnU-Net, MONAI nnU-Net replica (fixed AC2 model), and post-hoc single-tracer specialist models; AC2 restricted to data-pipeline changes only

Validation

  • Held-out 200-study test set split into four tracer-center conditions; no prospective evaluation
  • Metrics: DSC (lesion-positive cases only), FNV and FPV (volume-weighted, one-voxel detection criterion), plus NSD, volumetric similarity, CC-DSC, PQ/RQ/SQ, patient-level sensitivity/specificity
  • Ranking robustness: bootstrap (n=2,000) and five alternative ranking schemes; linear mixed-effects models for variance partitioning; Holm-corrected Wilcoxon tests in one scheme

Results

  • Top algorithm: mean DSC 0.66, FNV 3.18 mL, FPV 2.78 mL; +8% DSC and ~5 mL lower FNV vs baseline
  • Best DSC on FDGUKT, worst on PSMAUKT; out-of-domain composites show ~1.7× systematic volume overestimation
  • Patient-level variance 61% of unexplained DSC variance vs 1.3% for team (σ 0.171 vs 0.025); each volume doubling gives +0.039 DSC
  • Lesion sensitivity ~0.83 at one-voxel criterion but ~0.48 at IoU 0.5; <40% for SUVmax <4.1 vs >95% for SUVmax >15
  • Majority-vote ensemble of top 5 would rank 1st (weighted rank 4.56), mainly by reducing FPV; lacrimal-gland cropping raised LesionTracer DSC by ~5% and ZeroSugar by 11.5% on PSMAUKT

Limitations

  • Tracer, center, scanner, reconstruction, annotation protocol and demographics are confounded within each condition; fixed effects cannot isolate causes
  • Five-minute per-case runtime cap penalized heavy ensembling, full TTA and larger models, possibly distorting ranking
  • Reader comparison is approximate (n=25, mixed inter/intra-reader protocols, no PSMAUKT second reader); single-annotator labels and annotation shift (slice-by-slice vs threshold-based) may bias ground truth
  • Authors omit: no prospective clinical validation or downstream TMTV/prognosis utility; AC2 had only 4 teams and a constrained MONAI baseline, limiting conclusions about data-centric methods

A plaque heterogeneity score derived from high-resolution vessel wall imaging for identifying stroke-associated vulnerable carotid plaques

Medical imaging MLscore 9 European Radiology · IF 4.7journal list2026-11-25Wang, Yaru, Liu, Dong, Chi, Xiuyu et al.

Identifying stroke-associated vulnerable carotid plaques currently relies on qualitative, operator-dependent assessment that lacks quantitative measures of plaque heterogeneity. The study analyzed 423 carotid plaques in 341 patients from three medical centers (June 2018 to June 2024) using contrast-enhanced T1-weighted high-resolution vessel wall imaging, with Center 1 split into training (n=168) and internal validation (n=72) sets and Centers 2 (n=73) and 3 (n=110) serving as external validation cohorts. Plaque voxels were clustered with a Gaussian mixture model, and the spatial distribution of subregions was integrated into a plaque heterogeneity score (PHS), which was combined with conventional metrics in a multivariable model presented as a nomogram. The integrated model achieved AUCs of 0.849-0.865 across all datasets, with a maximum intergroup AUC difference of 0.041 across age subgroups, and PHS added incremental discrimination beyond conventional vulnerability and burden assessments (ΔAUC 0.080-0.112; IDI 0.089-0.138; all p<0.05), including a ΔAUC of 0.134 and IDI of 0.143 when added to Plaque-RADS in external validation set 1. The contribution is a quantitative, interpretable heterogeneity marker that improves identification of stroke-associated plaques over conventional assessment across multicenter cohorts.

Independent evaluation of eight deep learning breast cancer detection algorithms as a second reader and for triage in a UK breast screening cohort

Medical imaging MLscore 9 European Radiology · IF 4.7journal list2026-11-25Rothwell, Joshua, Hickman, Sarah E., Payne, Nicholas R. et al.

The study addresses how eight commercial deep learning breast cancer detection algorithms from five vendors compare as standalone readers, paired with a single human reader, and for triage in a UK screening setting. It used 22,778 retrospectively collected digital mammograms from two UK Breast Screening Programme sites (Cambridge and Norwich, 2017), a multi-centre cohort containing 294 screen-detected, 151 interval, and 209 next-round cancers, with histopathologically confirmed cancer as the reference standard. Algorithms were assessed at single-reader specificity as standalone readers or paired with the first human reader, and in low- and high-suspicion triage scenarios, using AUC, sensitivity, specificity, recall rate, and arbitration rate, with noninferiority tested at a 5% margin. AUCs ranged 0.82-0.91, with one algorithm inferior to all others (p < 0.001); as standalone readers four algorithms had noninferior sensitivity (47.2-64.5%) versus single reading (62.9%), paired reading gave sensitivities of 64.7-67.4% (all but one noninferior to double reading at 67.9%) and specificity of 97.5% (all superior to double reading at 97.1%, p < 0.001), and combined triage reached sensitivity of 68.8-71.2%, superior to double reading for five algorithms and noninferior for the rest. The work matters because it provides an independent head-to-head benchmark showing that commercial algorithms vary in performance and that newer versions are not necessarily better, supporting independent evaluation before prospective deployment.

This retrospective head-to-head benchmark asks which commercial deep learning (DL) mammography algorithms can substitute for or triage within the UK NHS Breast Screening Programme's triennial double-reading workflow, ahead of the prospective EDITH trial. Eight algorithms from five vendors (Google/iCAD, iCAD ProFound, Lunit, Therapixel, ScreenPoint Transpara, Vara; aliased DL-1a to DL-5b) were run on 22,778 two-view screening mammograms (654 cancers: 294 screen-detected, 151 interval, 209 next-round) from two sites using Philips (Cambridge) and GE (Norwich) systems, with no priors. Algorithms were assessed standalone, paired with the first reader, and in rule-out (99% sensitivity), rule-in (99% specificity) and combined triage simulations, with thresholds calibrated on a different screening year. Discrimination was broadly comparable (AUC 0.82-0.91), with DL-5b (0.91) and DL-1b (0.90) best and DL-5a (0.82) clearly worst. Standalone, four algorithms were noninferior to single reading (sensitivity 62.9%); paired, all but DL-5a were noninferior to double reading (67.9%) with marginally higher specificity (97.5% vs 97.1%) but more arbitrations. Combined triage was noninferior for all and superior for five (sensitivity 68.8-71.2%), at the cost of higher recall. Genuinely new: multi-vendor, multi-algorithm comparison within UK triennial screening, explicit inclusion of interval/next-round cancers as targets, and demonstration that newer versions do not always improve performance.

Performance of a mammography-based deep learning risk model across breast cancer tumor phenotypes

Medical imaging MLscore 9 European Radiology · IF 4.7journal list2026-11-25Albasha, Heba, Mercaldo, Sarah F., Carney, Madeline et al.

Breast cancers vary in histologic subtype, grade, molecular profile, and stage, and it is unclear whether an image-only deep learning risk model discriminates equally well across these phenotypes. The study used a retrospective, six-site cohort of 341,921 screening mammograms from 104,472 women aged 30 and older (2009-2022), with cancers identified via tumor registry linkage, yielding 1991 cancers (77.5% invasive, 22.5% DCIS). The previously validated Mirai image-only DL model generated risk scores from screening mammograms, and discrimination was assessed with AUCs and DeLong comparisons across subtypes, grades, molecular profiles, and stages. Discrimination was similar for invasive cancer (AUC 0.81, 95% CI 0.80-0.82) and DCIS (AUC 0.81, 95% CI 0.79-0.83; p=0.88), did not differ significantly between invasive ductal and lobular carcinomas (0.81 vs 0.78; p=0.08), and remained robust across grades (0.79-0.83), molecular profiles (0.77-0.88), and stages (0.71-0.82). The findings indicate that screening mammograms carry imaging biomarkers associated with diverse tumor phenotypes and support the model's use in risk-stratified screening and prevention.

Can frontier vision-language models support first-pass orthopaedic landmark annotation? A zero-shot technical feasibility study of 553 lateral knee radiographs.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Journal of Experimental Orthopaedics · IF≈3.82026-11-24P Vivekanantha, S Kotipalli, MD Bouchard et al.

Accurate localization of anatomical landmarks is needed to automate sagittal knee measurements such as posterior tibial slope and patellar height, and the study asks whether a frontier vision-language model can do this without task-specific training. The data were 553 lateral knee radiographs manually annotated for four structures (patella, tibial plateau, tibial shaft, tibial tubercle); the abstract does not state the source or whether the cohort was single- or multi-centre. GPT-5.5 was prompted via the OpenAI API to output annotations directly in YOLOv8 pose format, with schema validation before comparison to human annotations using Euclidean distance in normalized and pixel space, plus token, runtime and cost tracking. It produced valid annotations for 552 of 553 radiographs (99.8% schema success), with mean (SD) pixel error of 62.9 (69.3) pixels and median (IQR) 41.2 (23.1-77.3) pixels across 4416 landmark predictions; mean normalized error was 0.0376 (0.0325), lowest for the superior patellar pole (0.0240) and highest for the distal tibial shaft (0.0561) and tibial tubercle (0.0515), at a total cost of $64.64 and 11.9 h runtime. The authors conclude the accuracy is insufficient for independent deployment but that definition-only prompting may serve as a first-pass annotation aid to reduce manual labelling burden for orthopaedic pose-estimation datasets.
