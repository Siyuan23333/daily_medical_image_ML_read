CPCLNet: Advancing Cross-Modal Few-Shot Medical Image Segmentation with Clustering-Inspired Prototype Consistency Learning.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging · IF 10.0journal list2026-11-12P Zhang, C Liu, Y Wu et al.

Cross-modal few-shot medical image segmentation is hampered by modality discrepancy that disrupts support-query matching, contaminates support-derived foreground and background prototypes, and yields noisy affinity maps. The study evaluates the method on three imaging modalities across CT→MRI and CT→PET tasks under 1-shot and 5-shot settings, though the abstract does not state cohort size or centre composition. CPCLNet combines a parameter-sharing feature encoder, an episode-specific cross-modal distribution alignment module, a clustering-inspired representative prototype descriptor to suppress unstable support responses, and an RWKV-based affinity map prediction module with linear complexity for long-range propagation. It achieves an average Dice improvement of 3.34 percentage points over the second-best competitor across the cross-modal tasks, and integration with MedSAM yields consistent further gains. The contribution is a prototype consistency learning formulation that jointly addresses feature-space alignment, prototype reliability, and affinity-map coherence for cross-modal few-shot segmentation.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from abstract only)

This paper addresses cross-modal few-shot medical image segmentation (FSMIS), where a model trained on a labeled support set from one imaging modality (e.g., CT) must segment a query image from a different modality (e.g., MRI or PET) given only 1 or 5 annotated examples. The clinical motivation is annotation scarcity and poor generalization across scanners/modalities. The authors argue that severe modality discrepancy corrupts support-query matching, contaminates foreground/background prototypes, and yields noisy affinity maps. CPCLNet reframes cross-modal FSMIS as prototype consistency learning with three components: a parameter-sharing encoder giving a common representation basis, a cross-modal distribution alignment module performing episode-specific support-query alignment, and a clustering-inspired representative prototype descriptor that suppresses unstable support responses to build compact, modality-robust prototypes. An RWKV-based affinity map prediction module then propagates prototype-guided correspondences over long-range spatial context at linear complexity. Headline result: an average Dice gain of 3.34 percentage points over the second-best competitor across CT→MRI and CT→PET tasks in both 1-shot and 5-shot settings, plus consistent gains when integrated with MedSAM. Genuinely new elements are the clustering-inspired prototype descriptor and the RWKV affinity module for cross-modal FSMIS; the distribution-alignment and parameter-sharing ideas are established. Only the abstract was available, so architectural and training specifics cannot be verified.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging · IF 10.0journal list2026-11-12N Bayasi, J Fayyad, F Yousefirizi et al.

Interactive foundation models such as SAM generalize poorly across clinical deployment sites, and existing test-time adaptation methods either drop the physician (losing clinical grounding) or keep the physician (reintroducing annotation burden). The evaluation is a pre-clinical study on six oncological tumor segmentation benchmarks covering CT (colon, pancreas, liver, kidney) and PET (head and neck, lung), using agreement with reference-standard segmentations as a proxy for physician acceptance; the abstract does not state cohort sizes or whether the data are single- or multi-centre. Clinical-SAM is a two-phase agentic physician-in-the-loop TTA framework for a frozen SAM: in Phase I a self-prompting agent trained with reinforcement learning generates prompts autonomously, and in Phase II the agent policy is refined online using only binary accept/reject feedback from physicians, converging toward a reference acceptance standard with progressively fewer interactions. Clinical-SAM addresses the deployment-time generalization gap of interactive foundation models like SAM for clinical tumor segmentation. The clinical question is how to adapt a frozen SAM to new sites without per-case expert spatial prompts, while still keeping physician judgment in the loop. A Multi-Cohort nnU-Net for White Matter Hyperintensity Segmentation Across 2D and 3D FLAIR Acquisitions

Medical imaging MLscore 10 medRxiv · preprint2026-11-12Chen, Y., Khatri, U., Xu, B. et al.

Automated white matter hyperintensity (WMH) segmentation tools are typically developed within narrow population and protocol ranges, leaving unclear whether accuracy and error stay consistent across 2D versus 3D FLAIR and whether algorithm choice affects downstream clinical associations. The authors trained a single nnU-Net taking FLAIR and T1-weighted inputs on 460 2D and 3D FLAIR scans from ADNI, the CSVD-enriched WWISH cohort (with same-visit paired 2D and 3D FLAIR), and UK Biobank, holding out 92 scans from 66 participants at the participant level, and tested on an independent ADNI3 3D FLAIR set (n = 25) against SHIVA-WMH, segcsvdWMH, and FreeSurfer WMH-SynthSeg. On the internal test set the model reached mean Dice 0.73 (SD 0.10) versus 0.50, 0.57, and 0.29 for the comparators (all FDR-adjusted p < 0.001), with similar Dice on 2D and 3D FLAIR (0.75 vs. 0.71), and in paired WWISH scans the 2D-versus-3D volume bias was 1.8 mL for the model versus 4.7-9.9 mL for comparators, with the largest gains in low-burden scans (<5 mL; Dice 0.66 vs. 0.19-0.54) and for lesions of 100 voxels or fewer (detection 77-87% vs. 14-63%). In ADNI3 the model retained mean Dice 0.70 (SD 0.14) without retraining and exceeded all comparators (FDR-adjusted p <= 0.002), and model-derived WMH volume predicted incident dementia beyond clinical covariates (OR per SD 1.40, 95% CI 1.11-1.77; likelihood-ratio p = 0.009; cross-validated AUC 0.71 vs. 0.68) whereas comparator-derived volumes did not significantly improve model fit. The work indicates that one model trained across disease contexts and FLAIR acquisitions can give acquisition-consistent, low-burden-sensitive segmentation that transfers to an unseen protocol and carries clinical signal, supporting use in multi-cohort, mixed-etiology studies.

Deep dive (from full text via medrxiv-pdf)

This study asks whether a single deep-learning model can segment white matter hyperintensities (WMH) consistently across heterogeneous disease contexts and across conventional 2D versus isotropic 3D FLAIR, and whether algorithm choice changes downstream clinical associations. The authors trained one 3D nnU-Net (FLAIR + T1 inputs) on 460 scans from 330 participants pooled from ADNI (neurodegenerative), WWISH (CSVD-enriched, with same-visit paired 2D/3D FLAIR), and UK Biobank (population-based), holding out 92 scans from 66 participants at the participant level. They benchmarked against SHIVA-WMH, segcsvdWMH, and FreeSurfer WMH-SynthSeg at voxel, volume, and lesion levels, tested transfer on an independent ADNI3 3D set (n=25), and compared incident-dementia prediction in 1,174 cognitively unimpaired ADNI/Knight ADRC participants (42 events). The model reached mean Dice 0.73 versus 0.50/0.57/0.29, with similar accuracy on 2D (0.75) and 3D (0.71) FLAIR, smaller between-acquisition volume bias (1.8 mL vs 4.7–9.9 mL), and large gains in low-burden scans and small lesions. Only model-derived volume improved dementia model fit (OR 1.40/SD; AUC 0.71 vs 0.68). Genuinely new: a single cross-cohort, cross-acquisition model with paired-acquisition bias analysis and label-independent clinical validation; the architecture itself is not novel, and reference masks share the authors' labeling convention.

Method

  • 2D and 3D FLAIR both resampled to 1-mm T1 grid; one network for both acquisitions, no acquisition-specific models
  • Preprocessing: SynthStrip skull stripping, N4 bias correction, FSL-FLIRT affine registration, intensity normalization clipped to [-1,1]
  • Composite Dice + cross-entropy loss; nnU-Net on-the-fly augmentation; 0.5 probability threshold; checkpoint selected on validation set
  • Baselines: SHIVA-WMH (FLAIR+T1, threshold 0.5), segcsvdWMH (FLAIR, 0.35), FreeSurfer WMH-SynthSeg (FLAIR, discrete label)

Validation

  • Internal held-out test (participant-level split) plus external ADNI3 3D test without retraining or threshold tuning
  • Metrics: Dice, signed/absolute volume difference, Bland-Altman, ICC, lesion-level detection by size strata (15–30, 31–100, 101–250, >250 voxels), false-positive lesions
  • Friedman test with paired Wilcoxon and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR across strata; logistic regression with likelihood-ratio tests, 10-fold cross-validated AUC, bootstrap CIs, permutation testing

Results

  • Internal test mean Dice 0.73 (SD 0.10) vs 0.50 SHIVA-WMH, 0.57 segcsvdWMH, 0.29 WMH-SynthSeg (FDR-adjusted p ≤ 5.3e-16)
  • Dice 0.75 on 2D vs 0.71 on 3D FLAIR; between-acquisition mean bias difference 1.77 mL vs 4.67–9.92 mL for comparators in 26 paired WWISH participants
  • Low-burden (<5 mL) Dice 0.66 vs 0.19–0.54; very small lesion (15–30 voxels) detection 77% vs 14–63%; small lesion (31–100) detection 87% vs 14–62%
  • External ADNI3 3D Dice 0.70 (SD 0.14) vs 0.60 SHIVA-WMH, 0.68 segcsvdWMH, 0.42 WMH-SynthSeg (FDR-adjusted p ≤ 0.002)
  • Incident dementia: OR 1.40 per SD (95% CI 1.11–1.77), LR p = 0.009, cross-validated AUC 0.712 vs 0.683 clinical-only; comparator volumes did not significantly improve fit (LR p = 0.052–0.65)

Limitations

  • Cohorts do not cover the full range of scanners, field strengths, and protocols; external ADNI3 set is small (n=25) and from the same consortium as development data
  • Reference masks were initialized by an automated method and refined by a single rater with neurologist adjudication; shared labeling convention may favor the proposed model in voxel-level benchmarking
  • Requires coregistered T1; cross-sectional only, so longitudinal change and regional performance are untested
  • Paired 2D/3D agreement assessed in only 26 participants from one cohort; pooled analyses ignore within-participant correlation; dementia validation limited to global CDR in a modest sample

Explainable radiomics and clinical models for prediction of total knee arthroplasty in knee osteoarthritis: data from Osteoarthritis Initiative.

Bone and skeletalscore 9late · published 2026-11-11 Skeletal Radiology · IF≈2.52026-11-11A Pooyan, E Alipour, N Eshraghi et al.

Predicting which knee osteoarthritis patients will progress to total knee arthroplasty (TKA) is clinically important, and this study asks whether MRI radiomics, with or without clinical variables, can forecast TKA within 9 years. The retrospective exploratory analysis used 507 participants from the Osteoarthritis Initiative-Zuse Institute Berlin dataset with manually segmented knee MRIs, of whom 66 underwent TKA, drawn from a single multi-site cohort. Four hundred bone and cartilage radiomics features were combined with 13 clinical variables and modeled with logistic regression, LASSO, and XGBoost across radiomics-only, clinical-only, and combined feature sets, using an 80/20 development-holdout split and stratified nested cross-validation with preprocessing, selection, and tuning confined to training folds. On the held-out test set, radiomics-only LASSO had the highest observed AUC-ROC of 0.76 (95% CI 0.61-0.88), with combined LASSO close at 0.75 (0.61-0.87) and combined logistic regression and XGBoost at 0.73 and 0.72; in nested cross-validation combined LASSO reached AUC-ROC 0.786 (0.722-0.841), AUC-PR 0.343, and F1 0.405, only modestly above radiomics-only LASSO (0.781). Consistently retained predictors were femoral bone sphericity, femoral cartilage first-order skewness, and WOMAC disability. The work is exploratory and the authors state that external validation is required before clinical application.
