DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from abstract only)

This paper addresses the clinically important problem of segmenting extremely thin and tubular anatomical structures in dental cone-beam CT (CBCT): mandibular/maxillary cortical bone and the inferior alveolar nerve (IAN). Accurate delineation of these structures is essential for implant and surgical treatment planning, yet standard deep segmentation pipelines such as nnU-Net struggle with thin, low-contrast, topologically complex targets. The authors propose an anatomy-guided coarse-to-fine framework: a first CNN-based coarse segmentation stage is followed by an implicit refinement function that operates point-wise, concentrating computation on uncertain boundary regions rather than uniformly over the volume. The refinement is conditioned on an anatomical prior derived from the signed distance field (SDF) of the teeth, motivated by the stable spatial relationship between dentition and the target thin structures. Evaluations on challenging dental CBCT datasets for both cortical bone and IAN segmentation report improvements over state-of-the-art methods across metrics, with the headline claim of over 30% improvement in distance-based error metrics (ASD and HD). The genuinely new element is the use of tooth SDF as a geometric prior to guide implicit, uncertainty-focused refinement of thin structures; the coarse-to-fine and implicit-representation ingredients are individually established, so the contribution is primarily the prior design and its empirical effect on distance metrics. Only the abstract was available, so architectural and training specifics cannot be assessed.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Medical Image Analysis · IF 11.8journal list2026-10-30Y Yuan, J Bao, X Ge et al.

Automated prostate cancer diagnosis from bpMRI with vision-language models typically treats diagnosis as generic image-to-label matching, missing the structured reasoning, anatomical context, and sequence prioritization defined by PI-RADS, and clinical reading remains operator-dependent. The study uses a multi-center cohort of two public and one private datasets plus an external private dataset, with patient-level diagnosis and lesion-level localization evaluated on bpMRI. KAMP-Net is a multi-modal prompt-tuning network that translates PI-RADS criteria into the computational graph via zone-aware modality routing that dynamically gates heterogeneous bpMRI sequences and hierarchical vision-language alignment mirroring a radiologist's bottom-up workflow. The abstract reports competitive performance in patient-level diagnosis and lesion-level localization without giving specific numbers, and in an AI-assisted human-in-the-loop workflow the strategy reduced false positives for equivocal PI-RADS 3 cases without risking missed diagnoses. The contribution is reframing VLM adaptation for prostate MRI from label-driven to knowledge-driven learning aligned with clinical guidelines, with code released.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via elsevier)

KAMP-Net addresses the clinical problem of distinguishing clinically significant prostate cancer (csPCa) from indolent disease on bi-parametric MRI (T2W, DWI, ADC), where lesions are morphologically similar, PI-RADS interpretation is reader-dependent, and unnecessary biopsies are common. The authors reframe medical prompt tuning for CLIP-style vision-language models as a 'knowledge-driven' rather than 'label-driven' adaptation: structured PI-RADS v2.1 logic is embedded into the network's routing and alignment. Three components implement this: (1) zone-conditioned visual prompting, where nnU-Net-derived PZ/TZ masks are overlaid on slices and separate frozen-CLIP pathways with deep visual prompts process each zone, refined by cross-modal attention against LLM-distilled zone-specific PI-RADS text; (2) knowledge-infused multi-granularity textual prompts at pixel, zone, and volume levels combining expert phrases with learnable tokens; (3) hierarchical vision-language alignment matching visual features to prompts at pixel-to-entity, zone-to-context, and volume-to-diagnosis levels. Evaluation spans two public and two private multi-center cohorts for patient-level diagnosis and lesion localization, with comparisons to state-of-the-art AI baselines and to radiologists, including an AI-assisted reading strategy that probabilistically fuses AI and radiologist scores. The genuinely new element is the explicit encoding of zone-dependent PI-RADS modality weighting and hierarchical diagnostic reasoning as differentiable network constraints, rather than generic global image-text alignment. Learning to fuse and reconstruct multi-view graphs for diabetic retinopathy grading.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Medical Image Analysis · IF 11.8journal list2026-10-30H Li, Y Lin, H Wang et al.

Existing multi-view fundus image methods for diabetic retinopathy grading often fuse visual features directly and overlook inter-view correlations from the same patient, limiting use of cross-view consistency. The work evaluates on MFIDDR, described as the largest multi-view fundus image dataset, though the abstract gives no cohort size, source, or centre information. The proposed MVGFDR framework uses a Multi-View Graph Fusion module with three parts: graph initialization using residual-guided connections and DCT coefficients as frequency-domain anchors, selective cross-graph node fusion based on frequency-domain relevance, and masked cross-view reconstruction to learn view-invariant shared representations. The abstract reports state-of-the-art overall performance on MFIDDR but provides no quantitative numbers. Its contribution is explicitly disentangling shared and view-specific features across views rather than fusing visual features directly.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via arxiv)

This paper addresses five-grade diabetic retinopathy (DR) grading from multi-view fundus photography, arguing that single 45–50° fields of view miss lesions visible only from other viewpoints, and that existing multi-view dual-stream methods fuse all visual features indiscriminately, ignoring the strong inter-view correlation of images from the same eye. The authors propose MVGFDR, an end-to-end framework built on a PVT backbone in which each stage's patch features are converted into visual graphs whose cluster anchors are initialized from Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) basis functions. Graph nodes are split by DCT frequency index: high-frequency nodes (lesions, vascular boundaries) are treated as view-specific and fused across views with a GCN, while low/mid-frequency nodes (contours, background, main vessels) are treated as shared and fed to a masked cross-view reconstruction module, where a decoder-only transformer with view- and frequency-positional embeddings reconstructs randomly masked nodes of one view from the others. Training combines focal classification loss with a cosine+MSE reconstruction loss. On MFIDDR (8,613 eyes, 34,452 images), MVGFDR reaches 85.87% accuracy, 93.00% specificity, 73.14% kappa, 84.49% F1 and 96.17% AUC, beating the strongest expert-guided baseline SMVDR (84.01/91.30/71.36/83.69/95.58) without lesion maps or prompts. The genuinely new element is the frequency-band-based disentanglement of shared versus view-specific graph nodes, which gives a principled, medically motivated alternative to blind feature concatenation; the masked cross-view reconstruction is a sensible but less novel extension of masked graph modeling. Gains are consistent but modest over the best expert-guided competitor, and evaluation is retrospective on a single public dataset plus small auxiliary benchmarks.

Datasets

  • MFIDDR: 4-view color fundus (Zeiss Visucam), 8,613 eyes / 34,452 images at 2124x2556, public; official train (25,848 images) / test (8,604 images) split, no separate validation set reported
  • DRTiD: two-field fundus DR grading, private, size not stated; used as an additional test benchmark
  • CheXpert: chest X-ray, frontal+lateral views, public; used as cross-modality multi-view benchmark
  • MVG-DDR: DDR single-view fundus augmented with three diffusion-generated views (DreamBooth/Stable Diffusion, 15 models, 15k iterations each), derived from public DDR; released via figshare link

Method

  • Pyramid Vision Transformer (PVT, ImageNet-pretrained) backbone with a Multi-View Graph Fusion module inserted at all four stages; concatenated [CLS] tokens from all views feed a two-layer MLP classifier
  • Multi-view Graph Initialization: residual-guided soft-assignment clustering with M=32 anchors initialized from DCT basis coefficients (learnable amplitudes, fixed frequency basis)
  • Node selection by DCT index threshold k_theta=0.75: high-frequency nodes fused across views via GCN; low/mid-frequency nodes routed to reconstruction
  • Masked Cross-View Reconstruction: decoder-only transformer with view-positional and frequency-positional embeddings, masking rate eta=0.5, hybrid cosine+MSE loss (alpha=0.3); GCN-based reconstructor as ablation baseline
  • Training: focal loss + averaged reconstruction loss, Adam, lr 1e-5, 100 epochs, batch 40, single A100; compared against 10 single-view backbones (incl. RETFound) and multi-view MVCINN, ETMC, LFMVDR, MVTSM, SMVDR

Validation

  • Retrospective internal train/test split on MFIDDR following prior protocol; no external multi-view DR test set of the same kind available
  • Auxiliary generalization tests on private DRTiD, public CheXpert, and synthetic MVG-DDR
  • Metrics: accuracy, precision, sensitivity, specificity, F1, Cohen's kappa, AUC; per-grade precision/sensitivity/F1; no confidence intervals or significance tests reported

Results

  • MFIDDR: Acc 85.87%, Spe 93.00%, Kappa 73.14, F1 84.49, AUC 96.17 vs SMVDR (expert prompts) 84.01/91.30/71.36/83.69/95.58 and MVCINN 80.10/83.32/62.45/78.86/91.07
  • MFIDDR vs best single-view (Vim): +8.84 Acc points (85.87 vs 77.03) and +16.83 kappa points (73.14 vs 56.31)
  • Grade 4 F1 68.07 vs 64.10 (MVTSM with lesion) and 30.43 (SMVDR with prompts); Grade 1 F1 72.41 vs 71.70 (SMVDR)
  • DRTiD: Acc 92.68, Kappa 83.99, F1 90.71 vs Qmix 89.29/80.80/88.31; CheXpert: Acc 76.10, Kappa 61.25 vs MVCINN 73.04/56.90
  • Ablation (MFIDDR Acc): PVT+graph fusion 79.96 -> +node selection 81.10 -> +reconstruction 82.52 -> full 85.87; GCN reconstructor variant 84.52 Acc / 95.42 AUC

Limitations

  • No external multi-view DR dataset of comparable kind exists; DRTiD is private and CheXpert is a different modality, so true cross-site generalization is unproven
  • No confidence intervals, cross-validation, or statistical significance testing; single train/test split only
  • Class imbalance persists: t-SNE shows Grade 3/4 overlap and Grade 1 embedded in Grade 0; Grade 4 sensitivity is only 55.12%
  • Several hyperparameters (M=32, k_theta=0.75, eta=0.5, alpha=0.3) tuned on the reported test benchmark; MVG-DDR gains are smaller and confounded by synthetic-image style bias toward MFIDDR

Availability

A Novel Conditional Adversarial Diffusion Model for High-Fidelity OCT-to-OCT Image Translation and Precise Anti-VEGF Therapy Response Prediction in Diabetic Macular Edema

Medical imaging MLscore 10 arXiv · preprint2026-11-02Reza Rasti, Armin Biglari, Sobhan Mohammadi Hafshejani et al.

Patients with diabetic macular edema often fail to respond to costly first-line anti-VEGF injections, and predicting non-response before treatment could avoid a trial-and-error approach. The study used optical coherence tomography (OCT) scans from a US cohort of 127 subjects for development with subject-level 5-fold cross-validation and an independent Iranian cohort of 44 subjects for external validation, making it a multi-centre, cross-population design. The method is a conditional adversarial diffusion framework that synthesizes post-treatment OCT images from baseline scans to derive each patient's predicted initial treatment response. Benchmarked against fourteen baseline and comparison translation models, it achieved the best response prediction performance, with balanced accuracies of 85.7% and 93.0% on the US cohort and 85.2% and 95.3% on the Iranian cohort at 10% and 20% central subfield thickness reduction thresholds, respectively. The work is notable for combining image-to-image translation with response prediction and for validating on an external cohort from a different country, supporting use as a pre-treatment decision-support tool.

PDFarXivSummary
Deep dive (from full text via arxiv-pdf)

This paper addresses the clinical problem of predicting which diabetic macular edema (DME) patients will respond to anti-VEGF therapy, given that 31-65% show incomplete response. Rather than a direct classifier, the authors propose SCOD-GAN, a two-stage conditional adversarial diffusion framework that synthesizes a post-treatment OCT B-scan from a baseline scan; response is then derived post hoc by measuring the CST change between baseline and synthesized post-treatment images using a UNet++ segmentation model. Stage 1 maps the pre-treatment OCT to a post-treatment total-retina thickness mask (BCE+Dice+adversarial supervision); Stage 2 conditions on both the baseline OCT and the synthesized mask to generate the post-treatment OCT (L1+adversarial). Diffusion reverse processes are parametrized by conditional GANs, allowing sampling in only 4 steps. Trained with subject-level 5-fold CV on Duke-DME (n=127) and externally validated on a new Isfahan-DME cohort (n=44), the model achieved balanced accuracies of 85.7%/93.0% (US, 10%/20% CST-reduction thresholds) and 85.2%/95.3% (external), outperforming 14 translation baselines on response prediction. The genuinely new elements are the thickness-mask bottleneck as an interpretable anatomical prior, the diffusion-GAN hybrid with 4-step sampling, and the external multi-center validation; the response-prediction gains over a strong direct classifier (CADNet) are modest (AUC 0.879 vs 0.851).

Datasets

  • Duke-DME: SD-OCT volumes (61 B-scans, 768x496), pre/post 3 anti-VEGF injections, n=127 patients, private (from prior work [4]), 5-fold subject-level CV train/test
  • Isfahan-DME: SD-OCT volumes (19/25/31 B-scans, 768x496 or 512x496), n=44 patients (bevacizumab 20, aflibercept 24; 2-4 injections), private (new, available on academic request), held-out external test
  • UNet++ segmentation model: trained/CV on Duke-DME only, Isfahan-DME as unseen test (Dice 0.958 vs 0.944)

Method

  • Two-stage cascaded conditional diffusion-GAN (SCOD-GAN): Stage 1 G_A->B generates post-treatment retina mask; Stage 2 G_B->C generates post-treatment OCT conditioned on baseline OCT + synthesized mask
  • NCSN++-style UNet generators (ResNet + attention blocks, 32-dim sinusoidal time embedding, 256-dim latent adaptive normalization); temporal-embedding discriminators
  • Diffusion reverse process parametrized by conditional GANs; 4 diffusion steps (grid-searched over [2,4,10,50,1000]); beta_min=0.1, beta_max=20
  • Losses: Stage 1 BCE+Dice+adversarial; Stage 2 L1+adversarial; lambda_mask=lambda_image=1 (grid-searched); 100 epochs, lr 1.5e-4, PyTorch 1.12, 2x Titan GPUs
  • Response derived post hoc from CST change via UNet++ segmentation; compared against 14 baselines (Pix2Pix, CycleGAN, UNIT, MUNIT, BicycleGAN, Pix2PixHD, Heng-SRC, Fast-DDPM, SGD, I2I-Mamba, selfRDB, FGDM, PatchGCL, StegoGAN) and direct classifier CADNet

Validation

  • Internal subject-level 5-fold CV on Duke-DME; external validation on independent Isfahan-DME cohort (models trained on Duke applied to Isfahan)
  • Image metrics: MAE, PSNR, SSIM, FID, SWD, IS, KID computed within retina mask; classification: balanced accuracy, precision, sensitivity, F1 at 10% and 20% CST-reduction thresholds
  • Wilcoxon signed-rank tests for case-level metric differences and Spearman correlations (Table V); segmentation-perturbation sensitivity analysis (+/-3 px boundary shifts changed <1.6% of labels)

Results

  • Response prediction balanced accuracy: 85.69%/92.98% (Duke, 10%/20% thresholds) and 85.17%/95.25% (Isfahan external), best among all 15 methods; next best StegoGAN 80.22%/84.39% and 79.70%/91.51%
  • Image translation (Duke): MAE 51e-3, PSNR 23.98 dB, SSIM 83.41e-2, FID 1.655, KID 35e-4, best overall; external Isfahan: MAE 76e-3, PSNR 20.80, SSIM 79.09e-2, FID 1.991 (slightly behind Heng-SRC FID 1.949 and StegoGAN MAE 73e-3)
  • Ablation (Duke): removing two-stage raises MAE 51->63e-3 and drops F1 (10%) 82.29->77.49%; removing diffusion modules gives MAE 75e-3, FID 3.254, F1 75.17%
  • Two-stage vs single-stage: retinal-region MAE reduced 19.0% (63->51e-3, p<0.01); central subfield volume error reduced 12.4% (0.0217->0.0190 mm3, p<0.01)
  • vs direct classifier CADNet (10% threshold, Duke): accuracy 85.69% vs 80.31%, F1 82.29% vs 80.02%, AUC 0.879 vs 0.851, but sensitivity lower (75.97% vs 76.43%)

Limitations

  • Authors state: small single-center cohorts, need for larger balanced multi-center longitudinal data; endpoint limited to CST reduction due to incomplete visual acuity records; borderline cases (~+/-5% CST change) fail frequently; future work on 3D diffusion-GANs and Transformer backbones
  • External cohort is small (n=44) and translation fidelity on Isfahan-DME is not superior to several baselines (Heng-SRC, StegoGAN, PatchGCL), indicating domain shift
  • Response labels are thresholded CST changes (10%/20%) rather than clinician-defined outcomes; no prospective evaluation or comparison against clinician prediction
  • No calibration analysis, confidence intervals on external metrics, or stratification by anti-VEGF agent/injection count; inference relies on a separately trained UNet++ whose errors propagate

Availability

Source code to be released upon acceptance; Isfahan-DME dataset available upon academic request; Duke-DME from prior work [4].
