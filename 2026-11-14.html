Learning uncertainty fields for joint brain stroke diagnosis and intrinsic lesion localization in CT and MRI

Medical imaging MLscore 9 npj Digital Medicine · IF 15.1journal list2026-11-14Jin, Zehao, Xu, Shuzhen, Dong, Xiaojun et al.

Early stroke changes are hard to detect and localize on neuroimaging when lesion boundaries are indistinct. The study used a multicentre CT cohort of 6650 patients and 8873 images plus a separate MRI cohort of 67 patients and 615 images, with training and testing done separately per modality. The Dynamic Fuzzy Encoder Network (DFE-Net) uses learnable fuzzy membership fields to represent spatial uncertainty and jointly classify stroke and localize lesions. DFE-Net reached AUROC 0.974 and F1 0.897 on CT, and 0.978 and 0.879 on MRI, with CT localization Dice 0.70 against expert annotations; MRI localization used gradient-derived pseudo-targets because expert masks were unavailable, and calibration error was about 0.022 in both cohorts with 0.20 million parameters. The work shows that joint diagnosis and spatial localization can be achieved with a compact model, though prospective validation and expert-annotated MRI evaluation are still needed.

DOISummary
Deep dive (from abstract only)

This paper addresses the clinical problem of detecting and spatially localizing early ischemic stroke changes on neuroimaging, where lesion boundaries are often indistinct and expert annotation is scarce. The authors propose the Dynamic Fuzzy Encoder Network (DFE-Net), which learns fuzzy membership fields to explicitly represent spatial uncertainty and uses them to jointly perform stroke classification and lesion localization within a single compact model. Evaluation spans a large multicentre CT cohort (6650 patients, 8873 images) and a much smaller MRI cohort (67 patients, 615 images), with training and testing conducted separately per modality. Headline performance is strong: AUROC 0.974 and F1 0.897 on CT, and AUROC 0.978 and F1 0.879 on MRI, with calibration error around 0.022 in both cohorts and only 0.20 million parameters. CT localization against expert annotations reached a Dice of 0.70. The genuinely novel element is the use of learnable fuzzy membership fields as an explicit, lightweight uncertainty representation coupled to joint diagnosis and localization, rather than post hoc uncertainty estimation or separate segmentation heads. However, because only the abstract is available, architectural specifics, baseline comparisons, and statistical testing cannot be assessed; the MRI localization result rests on gradient-derived pseudo-targets rather than expert masks, and no prospective or external cross-site validation is reported, so the clinical generalizability claims remain provisional.

LifespanFix: Semi-supervised cortical correction across the human lifespan.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Medical Image Analysis · IF 11.8journal list2026-11-12T Wang, Y Sun, L Wang et al.

Cortical segmentation in brain MRI often contains topological and anatomical defects, due to the cortex's thin, convoluted geometry and imaging artifacts, which can bias downstream morphometric analyses across the lifespan. The framework was trained on 3,189 subjects spanning infancy to older adulthood from nine public datasets, and evaluated on an independent cohort of 1,058 subjects from five datasets, including label maps from different segmentation methods; the abstract does not state whether the data are single- or multi-centre. The method is a semi-supervised framework in which a generation network produces template-constrained pseudo-labels that train a correction network (LifespanFix); at inference only the correction network is used, avoiding the generation network and the time-intensive registration required during training. The abstract reports that LifespanFix consistently outperforms state-of-the-art correction approaches across ages, scanners, and protocols but gives no quantitative metrics. Its contribution is a lifespan-robust, end-to-end correction model trained and tested across many public datasets and segmentation methods, aimed at more reliable large-scale lifespan neuroimaging analyses.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via elsevier)

LifespanFix addresses a practical bottleneck in lifespan neuroimaging: automated white matter (WM) segmentations contain topological defects (holes, handles) and anatomical defects (spurious inclusions, missing WM) that bias cortical volume, thickness, and surface-area measurements. The authors assemble 3189 subjects from nine public datasets spanning infancy to old age, generate WM segmentations with iBEAT V2.0, and simulate defects by adding/removing random 3D primitives (spheres, cubes, pyramids, ellipsoids) to create paired defective/clean patches. Rather than supervising a correction network with a single ground-truth target (which admits many valid corrections), they train a generation network on 30 manually corrected, deformably registered WM templates to produce probabilistic pseudo-labels, which then supervise a densely connected U-Net correction network (LifespanFix) via cross-entropy plus Dice loss; identity-mapping cases (10% of subjects) discourage overcorrection. At inference only the correction network runs, iteratively (3 iterations, ~15.6 s/subject on a V100), with no registration. On 1058 test subjects across four age groups, LifespanFix outperformed BrainSuite, FreeSurfer, DU-Net, and AC-U-Net on successful rate, Dice, and average surface distance (P<0.001), and reduced detected topological defect components from 213.65 to 6.53 per subject (96.95%). The genuinely new element is the template-constrained soft pseudo-label supervision replacing single-target supervision, plus lifespan-wide training; the correction architecture itself is conventional.

Datasets

  • Training: IBIS, ABCD, HCPA, HCPD, BCP, ABIDE, ADNI, HCP, OASIS3; T1w MRI; 3189 subjects; public; train (iBEAT V2.0 WM segmentations, simulated defects)
  • Templates: 30 HCP subjects; T1w MRI; public; manually corrected WM templates for registration-based pseudo-labels
  • Validation: BCP, ABIDE, ADNI; T1w MRI; 284 subjects; public; SynthSeg segmentations, manually corrected; hyperparameter tuning
  • Test infant (0-2y): New England, dHCP; T1w MRI; 183 subjects; public; BIBSNet/BabySeg/SynthSeg inputs, manually corrected GT
  • Test child/adolescent (3-18y): New England, NKI; T1w MRI; 318 subjects; public; SynthSeg inputs, manually corrected GT
  • Test adult (19-59y): NKI, IXI; T1w MRI; 283 subjects; public; SynthSeg inputs, manually corrected GT
  • Test elderly (>=60y): NKI, OASIS1; T1w MRI; 274 subjects; public; FreeSurfer inputs, manually corrected GT
  • Zero-shot: cSeg challenge cerebellar WM segmentations; public; qualitative only
  • Preliminary: ImageCAS coronary artery segmentations; public; retrained model, supplementary

Method

  • Densely connected U-Net (DU-Net) backbone for both generation and correction networks; 48x48x48 3D patches
  • Generation network maps 30 SynthMorph-registered WM templates to probabilistic pseudo-labels (cross-entropy vs. original WM)
  • Correction network trained on simulated defective patches with soft pseudo-label targets (cross-entropy + Dice, lambda=0.5, alpha=0.0001)
  • 10% of training subjects kept defect-free with identity mapping to prevent overcorrection; ~3.2M patches
  • Inference: correction network only, 3 iterative passes, no registration; baselines BrainSuite, FreeSurfer, DU-Net, AC-U-Net

Validation

  • Internal held-out lifespan test set (1058 subjects, four age groups) with manually corrected ground truth; no prospective cohort
  • Metrics: Successful Rate (topological correctness + >=70% local agreement), Dice Ratio, Average Surface Distance within defect regions
  • Two-sided t-tests (P<0.001) and Cohen's d effect sizes; level-set defect counts before/after

Results

  • Topological defect components per subject reduced from 213.65+/-49.25 to 6.53+/-1.50 (96.95% reduction) across 1058 subjects
  • Best SR, DR, and ASD vs. BrainSuite, FreeSurfer, DU-Net, AC-U-Net in all four age groups (P<0.001)
  • Runtime 15.6 s/subject for 3 iterations vs. ~21 min for FreeSurfer and 14.9 s for BrainSuite
  • Optimal defect-free training proportion 10%; 3 iterations sufficient for most defects including large holes
  • Slightly lower performance in infant cohort than child/adult groups

Limitations

  • Trained only on neurologically normal subjects; lesion-induced holes (tumors, gliomas) may be wrongly 'corrected'
  • Lower accuracy in infants; cannot recover severely missing structures from degraded inputs
  • Patch-wise output does not guarantee genus-0 topology; downstream topology enforcement still needed
  • Anatomical correction results reported only for LifespanFix, without quantitative comparison to other methods; no prospective or pathology cohorts

Availability

Code and trained model at https://github.com/ittong/LifespanFix; raw data from public repositories (NDA, ADNI, OpenNeuro, etc.)

Measurement fidelity and external validation of automated breast DCE-MRI segmentation

Medical imaging MLscore 9 npj Digital Medicine · IF 15.1journal list2026-11-14Tan, Chunlei, Chen, Li, Xu, Jinling et al.

Automated breast DCE-MRI segmentation could support quantitative tumor measurement, but geometric overlap metrics alone do not show whether mask-derived measurements are faithful. The study used a retrospective multi-cohort design with 521 paired internal-test and DUKE cases, plus the BC-MRI-SEG and public BreastDCEDL_AMBL external-validation cohorts, evaluated with a frozen checkpoint and no external-label tuning. The method was an enhancement-aware three-dimensional MedNeXt framework with gated skip fusion and boundary supervision, with measurement fidelity as the primary endpoint. Median errors were 9.7% for tumor volume, 1.8 mm for 3D maximum surface diameter, and 10.2% for operational enhancement-defined volume, with ICCs of 0.97, 0.80, and 0.97; median Dice was 0.831 internally, 0.823 in the DUKE source-held-out stratum, 0.812 in BC-MRI-SEG, and 0.806 in BreastDCEDL_AMBL, and 78 of 80 masks needed no major correction in blinded two-reader review. In 276 paired response-analysis cases, AUROC was 0.732 with automatic geometry versus 0.757 with expert geometry (difference -0.025; 95% CI -0.095 to 0.045), so discrimination non-inferiority was not demonstrated, which distinguishes measurement fidelity from downstream predictive equivalence.

Beyond DXA: Microarchitectural correlates of HR-pQCT-based finite element bone stiffness in adults with low bone mass.

Bone and skeletalscore 9 Bone Reports · IF≈3.72026-10-31RN Dos Santos, U das Dores Pereira Neto, TD Stocco et al.

DXA captures only areal bone mineral density, so this study asks how much of HR-pQCT finite element (FE)-derived bone stiffness can be explained by DXA versus microarchitectural measures. Data came from 136 adults aged 20-79 (79 women, 57 men) in the Brazilian Normative HR-pQCT Project, a single-cohort cross-sectional sample split into normal (n=70) and low bone mass (n=66), imaged with second-generation HR-pQCT at the distal radius and distal tibia plus voxel-based FE models under simulated uniaxial compression. Stiffness was about 17-23% lower in the low-bone-mass group across sexes and sites, with women showing combined cortical and trabecular deficits and men showing prominent trabecular deficits plus lower cortical area. Adjusted R2 was 0.635-0.816 for DXA-based models, 0.788-0.917 for HR-pQCT-based models, and 0.791-0.919 for integrated models, so adding DXA to HR-pQCT gave only small explanatory gains. The authors note that because stiffness and HR-pQCT predictors derive from the same image data, the comparisons are explanatory rather than predictive, and the work positions HR-pQCT-based FE analysis as a complement to areal BMD for characterizing structural determinants of modeled stiffness.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via elsevier)

This cross-sectional study asks whether local HR-pQCT-derived cortical and trabecular structure explains variance in finite-element (FE)-derived bone stiffness beyond DXA areal BMD, age, and appendicular lean mass index (ALMI), and whether this differs by sex and skeletal site. In 136 adults (79 women, 57 men; 70 normal-BMD controls, 66 with low bone mass by lowest lumbar spine/femoral neck T-score) from a Brazilian normative HR-pQCT cohort, distal radius and distal tibia were scanned at 61 µm (XtremeCT II) and voxel-based linear-elastic FE models (E = 10 GPa, ν = 0.3, 1% uniaxial compression) yielded structural stiffness. Stiffness was 17–23% lower in the low-bone-mass group at both sites and in both sexes (all P ≤ 0.0003). Hierarchical regression showed adjusted R² of 0.635–0.816 for DXA-based models, 0.788–0.917 for HR-pQCT-based models (Ct.Ar, Tb.vBMD, age, ALMI), and 0.791–0.919 for integrated models; adding HR-pQCT predictors to DXA raised R² by 0.068–0.218, whereas adding DXA to HR-pQCT gained only 0.005–0.018 (all non-significant). Microarchitectural deficits were sex-specific: combined cortical and trabecular loss in women (including higher tibial cortical porosity), predominantly trabecular loss in men. The genuinely new element is modest: a sex- and site-stratified decomposition of explanatory variance in a Latin American cohort, with an explicit and honest acknowledgment that outcome and predictors share the same image data, making the analysis explanatory rather than predictive. No fracture outcomes or independent mechanical reference were available.

Datasets

  • Brazilian Normative HR-pQCT Project (UNIFESP, São Paulo), single-center cohort of 500 adults aged 20–79; this analysis n=136 (79 women, 57 men; 70 controls, 66 low bone mass); private; cross-sectional analysis, no train/val/external split
  • DXA (Hologic Discovery Wi): lumbar spine, femoral neck, ultradistal and 1/3 radius aBMD, whole-body ALMI; same cohort; private; predictor variables
  • HR-pQCT (XtremeCT II, 61 µm) distal radius and distal tibia; same cohort; private; morphometry and FE inputs; site-specific complete-case subsets n=52–71 for regression

Method

  • Voxel-based linear hexahedral FE models (61 µm elements) from HR-pQCT; homogeneous isotropic linear elastic tissue, E=10 GPa, ν=0.3; 1% uniaxial compression; stiffness = reaction force sum / displacement
  • Mesh quality criteria: element density ≥95% of target and stiffness error <5% vs reference mesh
  • Hierarchical multivariable linear regression per sex and site: Model 1 (age, ALMI, ultradistal radius, spine, femoral neck aBMD), Model 2 (age, ALMI, Ct.Ar, Tb.vBMD), Model 3 (integrated)
  • Model comparison via adjusted R², ΔR² by F-test, VIF; standardized coefficients in supplement; HC3-robust sensitivity models adjusting for age and BMI
  • Group comparisons by independent t-tests, unadjusted, uncorrected for multiplicity (exploratory)

Validation

  • Internal cross-sectional only; no external or prospective validation; complete-case analysis without imputation
  • Metrics: adjusted R², ΔR² with F-test P values, maximum VIF (3.81–7.67), standardized β with 95% CI
  • Sensitivity analysis with HC3 robust standard errors adjusting for age and BMI; no correction for multiple testing

Results

  • FE stiffness lower in low bone mass: ~21% radius and ~17% tibia in women; ~19% radius and ~23% tibia in men (all P ≤ 0.0003)
  • Adjusted R²: DXA models 0.635–0.816; HR-pQCT models 0.788–0.917; integrated 0.791–0.919
  • ΔR² adding HR-pQCT to DXA: 0.218 (men, tibia, P=6.69e-14), 0.151 (women, tibia, P=1.08e-8), 0.104 (women, radius, P=7.27e-13), 0.068 (men, radius, P=6.02e-6)
  • ΔR² adding DXA to HR-pQCT: 0.005–0.018, none significant (P=0.051–0.253)
  • Tibial cortical porosity higher in low-bone-mass women (3.1±2.0% vs 2.1±1.3%, P=0.010); men showed mainly trabecular deficits (lower Tb.vBMD, Tb.N; higher Tb.Sp)

Limitations

  • No fracture outcomes or independent mechanical reference; cannot support claims of prediction, diagnostic accuracy, or clinical utility
  • Circularity: FE stiffness and HR-pQCT predictors derive from the same scans; only DXA is an independent acquisition
  • Simplified FE constitutive model (homogeneous, isotropic, fixed modulus, uniaxial loading); no failure modeling; residual collinearity (VIF up to 7.67)
  • Single center, modest complete-case samples (n=52–71), unadjusted uncorrected group tests, T-score classification applied to younger adults, menopausal status not recorded, no reproducibility assessment of FE workflow

Availability

Data available on request; code availability not stated.

MRI Radiomics and Deep Learning for Differentiating Spinal Tuberculosis from Spinal Metastasis: A Multicenter Study.

Radiomicsscore 9 Neuroradiology · IF≈2.62026-11-14Z Zhang, J Chen, X Wang et al.

Differentiating spinal tuberculosis from spinal metastasis on MRI is clinically difficult, and the study compares radiomics, deep learning, and combined models for this task. The retrospective multicentre data comprised 186 STB and 219 SM patients from three hospitals, split into a training set (n=191), an internal test set (n=82), and an external test set (n=132). The authors built 2D, 2.5D, and 3D deep learning models using an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet50, a PyRadiomics-based radiomics model, and two fusion models (feature-based Rad_DL_FB and decision-based Rad_DL_DB). In the internal test set the Rad_DL_FB model achieved the highest AUC of 0.971 (95% CI 0.953-0.989) and accuracy of 0.927, while in the external test set the Rad_DL_DB model achieved the highest AUC of 0.961 (95% CI 0.940-0.982) and accuracy of 0.909. Fusion-model assistance improved junior spine surgeons' diagnostic accuracy and narrowed the gap with senior surgeons, indicating potential value as a decision-support tool, though the differing best-performing fusion model across test sets is notable.
