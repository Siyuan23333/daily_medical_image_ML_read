An interpretable graph reasoning framework for enhanced diagnosis in fundus fluorescein angiography

Medical imaging MLscore 9 npj Digital Medicine · IF 15.1journal list2026-10-27Liu, Xiaocong, Shao, An, Liu, Xindi et al.

Interpretable AI for fundus fluorescein angiography (FFA) that matches clinicians' reasoning and real-world diagnostic workflows is scarce. The study used a large, fine-grained, multi-center dataset of 25,312 FFA images from 2,651 patients. The proposed FFA-RGNet is a graph reasoning framework that explicitly models temporal patterns of fine-grained lesions across imaging phases and anatomical locations in sequential FFA images. On the internal test set it outperformed nine baseline models with an F1-score of 95.38% (95% CI 92.24%–97.88%), performed consistently on multi-center and prospective validation sets, surpassed ophthalmologists, residents and students, and improved their diagnostic F1-score by 7.48% to 14.11% as an assistive tool. Inference analysis showed correct recognition of fine-grained semantic entities and stable, disease-specific graph reasoning patterns aligned with clinical knowledge, supporting its use as interpretable clinical decision support.

Automated Opportunistic Bone Mineral Density Assessment at Low-Dose CT Lung Cancer Screening: Incident Vertebral Fractures and Mortality.

Bone and skeletalscore 9 Radiology · IF 17.6journal list2026-10-01MT Löffler, S Rühling, N Sollmann et al.

The prognostic value of opportunistic volumetric bone mineral density (vBMD) measured on low-dose CT lung cancer screening scans was unclear, particularly for predicting vertebral fractures and mortality in heavy smokers. The study was a secondary analysis of the CT arm of the National Lung Screening Trial, a multicenter randomized trial, including 21,856 participants aged 55-74 with at least 30 pack-years (mean age 61 years, 12,854 male). Automated SpineQ software measured thoracic and lumbar vBMD (normal, low bone mass, osteoporosis) and Genant-scale vertebral fractures, with Poisson and Cox models adjusted for demographics, comorbidities, and baseline fractures. Two-year vertebral fracture incidence was 0.9% with normal bone mass, 2.0% with low bone mass (adjusted RR 2.07, 95% CI 1.70-2.53), and 5.9% with osteoporosis (adjusted RR 3.40, 95% CI 2.69-4.30), while osteoporotic vBMD was associated with mortality in men (adjusted HR 1.24, 95% CI 1.02-1.50). The findings indicate that fully automated vBMD from screening LDCT predicts fracture risk, and that heavy smokers with osteoporotic vBMD exceed the 5% two-year fracture threshold used in current osteoporosis guidelines.

Radiographer fracture detection with and without AI assistance in prospective emergency department use

Bone and skeletalscore 9 npj Digital Medicine · IF 15.1journal list2026-10-27Tan, Jin Rong, Tran, Anh Nguyen Tuan, Sim, Wei Yow et al.

AI fracture-detection systems perform well in controlled settings but prospective real-world evidence is limited. The study used 2496 consecutive musculoskeletal examinations from a single emergency department over three months, read independently by thirteen radiographers in a radiographer-led screening programme, with paired reads against a radiologist reference standard. Radiographers interpreted each examination, reviewed output from a commercial deep-learning system, and recorded a final decision. Standalone AI sensitivity was 75.6% (46.2% for subtle fractures) versus 91.6% for radiographers, while radiographer accuracy rose modestly from 97.6% (specificity 99.0%) to 98.0% with AI (exact McNemar p = 0.0064), correcting 11 errors and introducing 1 (net gain of 10 cases); decisions changed in only 11 of 729 discordant cases and no or incomplete AI output occurred for 8.8% of examinations. The work is notable for quantifying prospective, real-world human-AI interaction in radiographer-led screening, showing that benefit came from complementary rather than superior algorithmic performance and that operational reliability mattered as much as diagnostic accuracy, though behavioural inferences rest on few events and downstream clinical outcomes were not measured.

Contrast-enhanced CT-based multimodal modeling for overall survival prediction after resection of retroperitoneal liposarcoma: a bicentric retrospective study.

Radiomicsscore 9 Abdominal Radiology · IF≈2.62026-10-27P Fan, J Zhang, W Luo et al.

Postoperative risk estimation for retroperitoneal liposarcoma is difficult because conventional clinicopathologic models capture only part of the tumor's biological heterogeneity. The bicentric retrospective study used contrast-enhanced CT and clinicopathologic data from 367 patients who underwent complete resection between 2009 and 2021, with a training cohort (n=128) and internal validation cohort (n=55) from one institution and an external test cohort (n=184) from a second. The method combined a 3D radiomics signature, a deep-learning signature from the largest axial tumor section using an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-101 trained with Cox partial-likelihood loss, and clinicopathologic variables selected by Cox regression into a combined model called RDCS. RDCS achieved C-indices of 0.775, 0.704, and 0.725 in the training, internal validation, and external test cohorts, higher than the individual radiomics, deep-learning, and clinical models, with calibration curves showing agreement and decision-curve analysis suggesting potential clinical utility. In an exploratory external primary-tumor subset (n=82), RDCS reached a C-index of 0.746 versus 0.590 for the Sarculator application, indicating that integrating preoperative CT signatures with postoperative clinicopathologic data may complement established prognostic tools, though prospective validation is needed.

Externally validated MRI radiomics machine learning models for differentiating between myxoid liposarcoma and myxoma.

Radiomicsscore 9 Skeletal Radiology · IF≈2.52026-10-27YH Chen, S Bazanjani, Z Wang et al.

The study addresses the difficulty of distinguishing myxoid liposarcoma from myxoma on MRI, a distinction that matters for treatment planning. It used T1-weighted and fluid-sensitive MRI sequences from 109 patients with histologically confirmed tumors to train SVM, random forest, and XGBoost classifiers, then tested them on an independent external cohort of 32 patients from a second institution with different scanners and protocols, making it a multi-centre design. The T1-weighted SVM performed best externally (accuracy 0.84, AUC 0.91), outperforming random forest and XGBoost (p = 0.005 and p < 0.001), and its performance was not significantly different from two fellowship-trained musculoskeletal radiologists (AUC 0.88-0.94; p > 0.05), though the study was not powered to establish equivalence. Fluid-sensitive SVM performance was weak (accuracy 0.56, AUC 0.60), and both models and radiologists showed limited accuracy for MLS grading. The external validation across different scanner hardware and protocols, plus the sequence-dependent drop in performance, indicates that T1-weighted radiomics may complement soft tissue tumor characterization while cautioning against extrapolating to fluid-sensitive sequences.

A Combined Clinical, Spectral CT, and Intratumoral Radiomics Model for Predicting Lymph Node Metastasis in Gastric Adenocarcinoma.

Radiomicsscore 9 Journal of Imaging Informatics in Medicine · IF≈3.92026-10-27D Wang, Y Lin, Z Lin et al.

Preoperative prediction of lymph node metastasis in gastric adenocarcinoma is difficult but important for treatment planning. The study retrospectively analyzed clinical, pathological, and dual-layer detector spectral CT imaging data from 275 patients (173 LNM-positive, 102 LNM-negative) at a single institution, using intra- and peritumoral iodine concentration, normalized iodine concentration, and effective atomic number metrics plus venous-phase radiomics features. Independent risk factors were identified by logistic regression, radiomics features were selected with LASSO, and a combined model of clinical data, spectral CT metrics, and radiomics scores was evaluated with ROC, calibration, and decision curve analysis.
