Confidence-Aware Mixup for Universal Semi-Supervised Medical Image Classification.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging · IF 10.0journal list2026-11-27L Ju, P Shi, Y Lin et al.

Universal semi-supervised learning for medical image classification must handle unlabeled images containing classes or imaging domains absent from the labeled set, and existing approaches add OOD detection modules such as prototype-based gates or open-set heads that bring extra hyperparameters and instability with few labeled images. The evaluation used dermatology, ophthalmology, and chest radiography benchmarks, including a multi-label CheXpert setting with 100 labeled patients; the abstract does not state cohort sizes, data sources, or whether the data are single- or multi-centre. The proposed Confidence-Aware Mixup (CAM) uses the classifier's own confidence without any auxiliary OOD detector: for each unlabeled image it retains the predicted-class probability, distributes the remaining probability evenly across other classes, and mixes this target with a labeled image via MixUp. Compared with 14 published SSL methods under fixed hyperparameters, CAM achieved the highest balanced accuracy in 10 of 12 single-label settings and the highest aggregate balanced accuracy on both single-label benchmarks, and on multi-label CheXpert it achieved the highest mean AUC and mAP with 100 labeled patients. The contribution is a universal SSL approach that avoids added detection modules and their hyperparameters while improving results across modalities, with code released publicly.

Deep dive (from abstract only)

This paper addresses universal semi-supervised learning (SSL) for medical image classification, where the unlabeled pool contains classes or imaging domains absent from the labeled set (open-set and out-of-distribution conditions simultaneously). Existing universal SSL methods add auxiliary modules such as prototype-based gates or open-set heads to detect OOD samples, which introduce extra hyperparameters and can be unstable when labeled data are scarce. The authors propose Confidence-Aware Mixup (CAM), which avoids any OOD detector: for each unlabeled image it takes the classifier's predicted-class probability, distributes the remaining probability mass uniformly over the other classes, and uses this soft target in a MixUp operation with a labeled image. The method is evaluated against 14 published SSL methods on dermatology, ophthalmology, and chest radiography benchmarks with fixed hyperparameters across methods. CAM reports the highest balanced accuracy in 10 of 12 single-label settings and the best aggregate balanced accuracy on both single-label benchmarks; on multi-label CheXpert with 100 labeled patients it achieves the highest mean AUC and mAP. The genuinely new element is the removal of explicit OOD detection in favor of confidence-derived soft targets, which simplifies the pipeline and reduces hyperparameter burden; however, only the abstract is available, so architectural details, exact numbers, and statistical testing cannot be verified.

A 2.5D Transformer-based multi-modal fusion framework for differentiating between brucellar spondylitis and spinal tuberculosis: a multicenter study.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 BMC Medical Imaging · IF≈3.82026-09-07A Alimujiang, H Zheng, J Lin et al.

Differentiating brucellar spondylitis from spinal tuberculosis is difficult because the two conditions overlap radiologically and clinically, yet the distinction guides antibiotic and surgical decisions. The retrospective multicenter study used clinical and MRI data from 238 patients (169 BS, 69 STB), with Center A (n=198) split into training (n=138) and internal validation (n=60) sets and Center B providing an external test set (n=40), addressing class imbalance during development. The method combined clinical features, MRI radiomics from 3D Slicer segmentations, and a 2.5D deep learning network, with a Transformer architecture fusing the multimodal features; ResNet18 gave the best slice-level prediction (AUC 0.863 training, 0.766 validation, 0.791 test), and the Transformer fusion model reached an AUC of 0.901 in validation versus 0.812 for multiple instance learning and 0.807 for ensemble fusion. The final combined model achieved AUCs of 0.957 (95% CI 0.928-0.986) in training, 0.916 (95% CI 0.834-0.998) in validation, and 0.896 (95% CI 0.797-0.994) in the external test set, with calibration and decision curve analyses supporting reliability. The work is notable for applying Transformer-based multimodal fusion to this differential diagnosis and validating it on external multicenter data, though the authors note prospective validation is still needed.

Preoperative CT-based topological intratumoral heterogeneity for predicting microvascular invasion and recurrence risk in hepatocellular carcinoma.

Radiomicsscore 9 Abdominal Radiology · IF≈2.62026-11-27J Zhao, Y Tan, Y Zhou et al.

Preoperative prediction of microvascular invasion (MVI) in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is clinically important, and the authors tested whether topological intratumoral heterogeneity (ITH) on portal venous phase CT adds predictive value beyond radiomics and clinicoradiological features. The multicenter retrospective study used 821 surgically confirmed HCC patients split into training (n=489), internal validation (n=211), and external validation (n=121) cohorts, with a separate exploratory TCGA-LIHC set of 42 cases for radiogenomic analysis. Manually segmented tumors were used to build clinical, ITH, radiomics, and stacking ensemble models, with SHAP interpretation and time-to-recurrence (TTR) analysis. The ensemble achieved AUCs of 0.805 (95% CI 0.760-0.847), 0.851 (95% CI 0.796-0.902), and 0.757 (95% CI 0.648-0.855) in training out-of-fold, internal, and external validation, respectively; the ITH model was numerically but not significantly better than radiomics on paired DeLong tests, and the 3D ITH score ranked as the top SHAP contributor and was associated with TTR. The work is notable for evaluating a topology-based heterogeneity descriptor in a large multicenter CT cohort with external validation and for linking it, hypothesis-generatingly, to transcriptomic patterns in a small TCGA subset.

Radiomics analysis based on DBT and US for differentiating benign from malignant breast lesions: a multicenter study.

Radiomicsscore 9 BMC Medical Imaging · IF≈3.82026-09-04DM Ye, SM Song, P Lu et al.

Differentiating benign from malignant breast lesions on conventional imaging is difficult because interpretation is subjective, and combining digital breast tomosynthesis (DBT) with ultrasound (US) has been underused. The retrospective two-centre study used DBT and US from centre 1 (n=183) and centre 2 (n=65), with lesions scored by BI-RADS to build a clinical model. Radiologists delineated ROIs, from which radiomics and habitat features were extracted and selected, while deep features came from a pretrained ResNet101 to form a DL model; AUCs were compared with the DeLong test. In training, clinical, habitat and DL models reached AUCs of 0.943, 0.965 and 0.926, all significantly above the radiomics model (0.826), with no significant differences among the top three; in testing, the clinical (0.943) and DL (0.874) models significantly outperformed radiomics. The external validation of the DL model on a second-centre cohort supports its potential as an adjunct for lesion characterization and reducing unnecessary biopsies.

Radiomics-based computed tomography urography image analysis for prediction of HER2 status in bladder cancer.

Radiomicsscore 9 BMC Medical Imaging · IF≈3.82026-09-05B Wu, T Zhou, C Hou et al.

The study addresses preoperative, non-invasive prediction of HER2 status in bladder cancer, which is currently determined pathologically. It uses a single-centre retrospective cohort of 190 patients with pathologically confirmed bladder cancer who underwent preoperative computed tomography urography between October 2019 and September 2024, split randomly into training (n=133) and test (n=57) sets, with HER2 positivity in 62.1% (118/190). Radiomics models were built with LASSO feature selection combined with five machine learning classifiers (logistic regression, SVM, KNN, XGBoost, random forest), and a clinical-radiomics model integrated radiomics features with clinical parameters, evaluated by AUC, calibration curves and decision curve analysis. The best radiomics model was random forest on corticomedullary-phase images, with AUCs of 0.798 (95% CI 0.718-0.877) in training and 0.734 (95% CI 0.601-0.866) in test, while the clinical-radiomics model reached 0.876 (95% CI 0.817-0.935) and 0.763 (95% CI 0.634-0.892) in training and test respectively. The work indicates that CTU-based clinical-radiomics analysis may offer a non-invasive route to HER2 status assessment in bladder cancer, though the test-set performance and single-centre design limit the strength of the evidence.

This single-center retrospective study asks whether preoperative CT urography (CTU) radiomics can non-invasively predict HER2 status in bladder cancer, motivated by the need to triage patients for HER2-directed antibody-drug conjugates (e.g., disitamab vedotin) without biopsy-based IHC/FISH. The authors retrospectively assembled 190 BCa patients (118 HER2-positive by IHC 2+/3+, 72 negative) scanned on four scanner models across three CTU phases (corticomedullary, nephrographic, excretory). The corticomedullary-phase RF model was best among radiomics-only models (test AUC 0.734); a clinical model using tumor number and basal margin reached test AUC 0.660; the fused clinical-radiomics model reached test AUC 0.763 (ACC 0.667, SEN 0.714, SPE 0.591). Genuinely new: first CTU-based radiomics for HER2 in BCa and a phase comparison showing corticomedullary superiority. However, the incremental gain over clinical variables is modest, test-set confidence intervals are wide, and several models showed test AUC exceeding training AUC, signaling instability rather than robust generalization.

Threshold optimization of artificial intelligence in digital breast tomosynthesis screening: a retrospective comparison study

Medical imaging MLscore 8 European Radiology · IF 4.7journal list2026-11-27Yang, Li-Ching, Cheng, Ting-Yin, Chen, Kang-I et al.

The study asks how a standalone deep learning AI system for digital breast tomosynthesis screening performs across different operating thresholds compared with radiologist-plus-CAD interpretation. It is a retrospective single-cohort analysis of 1780 asymptomatic women screened between July 2021 and June 2022, with 82.5% having dense breasts and 18 confirmed cancers (10.1 per 1000 examinations), using histopathology plus 24-month follow-up as reference standard; the abstract does not specify whether the data came from one or multiple centres. Three thresholds were compared (high-sensitivity ≥2%, Youden ≥27%, recall-matched ≥36%), with sensitivity, specificity and recall compared via McNemar tests. At the Youden threshold, AI had numerically higher sensitivity than radiologist-plus-CAD (77.8%, 14/18 vs 66.7%, 12/18; p=0.457) but significantly lower specificity (81.2% vs 95.2%; p<0.001) and higher recall (19.4% vs 5.4%; p<0.001), with high NPV for both (99.7% vs 99.6%), and AI detected two of six interval cancers missed by radiologists while four were missed by both. The work shows that standalone AI performance is strongly threshold-dependent and that the small cancer sample limits conclusions, so external validation is needed before clinical use.

Clinical LLMs are often scaled up in size, context, retrieval complexity, or inference-time compute under the assumption that higher accuracy implies safer behavior, which may not hold in medicine where a few confident or evidence-contradicting errors can outweigh average performance.
