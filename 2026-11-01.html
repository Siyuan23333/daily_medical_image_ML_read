Mind the Gap: Continuous magnification sampling for pathology foundation models.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Medical Image Analysis · IF 11.8journal list2026-10-01A Möllers, J Hense, F Schulz et al.

Pathology foundation models are poorly understood in how their performance varies across magnification levels and how magnification sampling during training affects them, with widely used discrete uniform sampling (0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0 mpp) causing degradation at intermediate magnifications. The study uses histopathology data, introducing two new benchmarks, TCGA-MS and BRACS-MS, with appropriate metrics; the abstract does not state cohort sizes or centre composition. The authors model magnification sampling as a multi-source domain adaptation problem, develop a theoretical framework showing trade-offs between sampling strategies, and propose continuous magnification sampling plus derived sampling distributions that optimize representation quality across scales. Continuous sampling improved downstream task performance at intermediate magnifications by up to 4 percentage points in balanced classification accuracy, and optimized distributions improved performance further. The work matters because it identifies magnification as a primary driver of performance variation across current histopathology foundation models and provides sampling strategies and benchmarks for building models that perform reliably across magnifications.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via unpaywall-pdf)

This paper asks how magnification sampling during self-supervised pretraining of pathology foundation models affects representation quality across the continuous magnification spectrum (0.25–2.0 mpp), motivated by the observation that pathologists use both low-magnification architecture and high-magnification morphology. The authors model magnification sampling as multi-source domain adaptation: each magnification is a source domain, and a similarity kernel (absolute-distance or field-of-view-overlap 'information-based') governs transfer between scales. This yields a training-signal proxy S(y) and shows that the prevalent discrete uniform sampling (0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0 mpp) leaves gaps at intermediate magnifications, while continuous uniform sampling degrades at boundaries. They propose continuous magnification sampling via dynamic crop-and-resize (no architectural or compute overhead, compatible with DINOv2), plus entropy-regularized max-average (CU-MAXAVG) and max-min (CU-MINMAX) optimized distributions. Evaluation combines RankMe embedding-rank profiling, two new multi-scale benchmarks (TCGA-MS, BRACS-MS), and analysis of public models. Continuous sampling removes the sawtooth RankMe profile and improves balanced accuracy at intermediate scales by up to ~4 pp without loss at standard scales; Virchow2 shows analogous dips. The genuinely new contribution is the principled, testable framing of magnification sampling as domain adaptation, with derived sampling distributions and multi-scale benchmarks.

Datasets

  • Pretraining: 200,000 WSIs from TCGA and Charité (private/institutional + public TCGA), H&E patches at 0.25/0.5/1.0/2.0 mpp, train only
  • TCGA-MS: 128 WSIs, 67,200 patches, 7 magnifications (0.25–2.0 mpp), 16 ICD-O-3 morphological subtypes from kidney/stomach/breast/soft tissue, public upon publication, 5-fold patient/class-stratified CV
  • BRACS-MS: derived from public BRACS, 269 WSIs, 10,773 patches, 7 magnifications, 6 atypical-lesion classes, public (source) + generation code, 5-fold CV x 3 seeds
  • RankMe profiling set: 10,000 held-out patches per magnification including intermediate 0.375/0.75/1.5 mpp, private/held-out

Method

  • ViT-S/16 trained with DINOv2 self-supervised framework, 60,000 iterations, batch size 320, 3 seeds
  • Continuous magnification sampling: dynamic crop-and-resize of source patches to arbitrary target mpp (crop size = target size × t/s), no architecture change or extra compute
  • Theoretical multi-source domain adaptation model with absolute-distance and information-based (FOV-overlap) kernels; transfer potential and accumulated training signal S(y)
  • Optimized distributions: CU-MAXAVG (entropy-regularized max average signal, λ∈{0.5,1.0}) and CU-MINMAX (max-min worst-case signal), derived with information-based kernel
  • Baselines: single-scale models (0.25/0.5/1.0/2.0 mpp), discrete uniform (DU), continuous uniform (CU); downstream linear and k-NN probes

Validation

  • Internal 5-fold patient/class-stratified cross-validation on TCGA-MS and BRACS-MS; no external prospective cohort
  • Metrics: balanced accuracy (linear and k-NN probes), RankMe effective-rank profiles across 7 magnifications; mean ± SEM over 3 training seeds
  • Theoretical predictions compared qualitatively/numerically to normalized RankMe (e.g., min/total signal vs. min/avg RankMe); no formal significance testing reported

Results

  • TCGA-MS: CU beats DU by 3.6 pp at 1.5 mpp and 3.5 pp at 0.75 mpp; CU-MAXAVG gains 4.4 pp over DU at 1.5 mpp
  • BRACS-MS k-NN: CU improves over DU by 3.9 pp at 1.5 mpp; linear probe differences at intermediate scales comparable
  • CU-MAXAVG over CU: +0.8 pp (TCGA, 1.5 mpp), +1.2 pp (TCGA, 2.0 mpp), +1.8 pp (BRACS linear, 1.5 mpp), +0.9 pp (BRACS linear, 2.0 mpp)
  • Single-scale models lose up to ~40% RankMe away from training scale; DU shows sawtooth dips at intermediate mpp; CU variants smooth
  • Virchow2 (multi-scale) beats single-scale models by up to 12 pp at 2.0 mpp on TCGA-MS but still dips at intermediate scales (-1.49 pp k-NN at 1.5 mpp TCGA; -1.42/-1.13 pp linear at 0.375/0.75 mpp BRACS)

Limitations

  • Authors: similarity kernels are approximations of cross-magnification feature relations; downstream tasks limited to cancer subtyping (mitotic counting/microorganism detection untested)
  • Authors: principled strategies not validated at SOTA data/model scale due to compute; magnification treated in isolation from tissue type, staining, scanner variation
  • CU-MINMAX improved worst-case RankMe but not boundary downstream accuracy, showing RankMe–downstream link is task-dependent
  • Omitted: no formal statistical significance testing; small ViT-S/16 models and modest pretraining (60k iterations); TCGA-MS not yet released at time of writing

Availability

Code, models, and benchmarks (TCGA-MS, BRACS-MS) at https://github.com/bifold-pathomics/continuous-magnification-sampling; TCGA-MS released upon publication, BRACS-MS generated from public BRACS.

DOISummary
Deep dive (from full text via unpaywall-pdf)

The paper addresses H&E-to-IHC virtual staining, where ground truth comes from adjacent tissue sections that are only weakly paired due to spatial misalignment and local deformation. The clinical motivation is to avoid costly, slow IHC staining (e.g., HER2, ER, PR, Ki67) by computationally generating IHC-like images from routine H&E. The authors propose TA-GAN, built on the CUT contrastive-translation backbone, adding two graph-based mechanisms: Topology-aware Consistency Matching (TACM), which builds patch-feature graphs via cosine-similarity thresholds, applies a shared graph convolution, and enforces mutual-information alignment between H&E and generated-IHC node features, including a topology-perturbation branch with random edge masking; and Topology-constrained Pathological Matching (TCPM), which uses PageRank node-importance scores to emphasize putative pathological-positive nodes and matches node-correlation matrices between virtual and real IHC. Evaluated on MIST (ER, PR, HER2, Ki67) and BCI (HER2, four classes), TA-GAN achieves the best FID/KID among compared methods, e.g., MIST ER FID 31.48/KID 1.6 vs USI-GAN 37.68/4.3, and BCI FID 40.55/KID 5.6 vs ASP 54.31/13.1. Genuinely new is the explicit graph-topology modeling of inter-patch structure and importance-weighted pathological matching for weakly-paired staining; the gains are real but incremental over contrastive/OT baselines, and pixel-level metrics are not superior.

Datasets

  • MIST: weakly-paired H&E–IHC breast patches, 1024×1024, four tasks (ER, PR, HER2, Ki67), ~4,000 train / 1,000 test pairs per task, public
  • BCI: HER2 H&E–IHC breast patches, 1024×1024, classes 0–3+, 3,896 train / 977 test pairs, public challenge data

Method

  • GAN translation with ResNet-6Blocks generator and 5-layer PatchGAN discriminator, CUT backbone
  • TACM: patch-feature graphs (cosine-similarity threshold adjacency), shared topology-adaptive GCN, InfoNCE mutual-information alignment plus topology-perturbation branch with mask ratio 0.15
  • TCPM: PageRank node-importance feature enhancement and correlation-matrix matching loss between virtual and real IHC
  • Training: 256×256 inputs, 100 epochs, Adam lr 2e-4, 4-hop GNN, thresholds [0.5,0.5,0.1,0.1,0.1], λ1=0.1, λ2=1, single RTX 3090
  • Baselines: Pix2Pix, CycleGAN, CUT, DDIB, PyramidP2P, ASP, TDKStain, PSPStain, USI-GAN

Validation

  • Internal train/test splits on two public datasets; no external or prospective cohort
  • Metrics: SSIM, PSNR, FID, KID (FID/KID prioritized for weak pairing); ICC of DAB positive-area ratios and regression slope/intercept vs y=x
  • Ablations on modules, GNN hops, thresholds, mask ratio, loss weights; results averaged over multiple runs; no formal significance testing reported

Results

  • MIST ER: FID 31.48, KID 1.6 vs best baseline USI-GAN 37.68/4.3
  • MIST Ki67: FID 25.77, KID 1.5 vs USI-GAN 29.97/3.9
  • BCI all: FID 40.55, KID 5.6 vs ASP 54.31/13.1; SSIM 0.3807 vs USI-GAN 0.3859
  • Ablation MIST ER: w/o all FID 44.62/KID 9.8; +TACM 39.08/5.9; +TCPM 33.27/3.3; full 31.48/1.6
  • ICC of positive-area ratios comparable to TDKStain/PSPStain/USI-GAN but higher than CUT

Limitations

  • Separate generator trained per IHC task; no multi-domain transfer
  • GNN sensitive to hyperparameters; manual tuning, no automatic search
  • No external/prospective validation; only two public breast datasets, HER2-centric
  • Pixel-level SSIM/PSNR not superior; no pathologist reader study or downstream diagnostic-task validation

Availability

Code to be released upon acceptance; datasets MIST and BCI are public.

Lobar analysis of classical and deep learning radiomic features in chronic airflow limitation.

Radiomicsscore 9 BMC Medical Imaging · IF≈3.82026-10-31E Bäcklin, B Janerot-Sjoberg, Ö Smedby et al.

The study asks whether lobe-based classical radiomic and frozen deep learning transfer features from inspiratory and expiratory chest CT improve discrimination of chronic airflow limitation (CAL) over handcrafted quantitative measures. It uses a population-based cohort of 2,579 participants with inspiratory and expiratory CT and post-bronchodilator spirometry, of whom 430 had CAL (FEV1/FVC < 0.70) and 2,149 served as controls; the abstract does not state whether the cohort is single- or multi-centre. Lobes were segmented with nnU-Net, classical features extracted with PyRadiomics, and deep learning features taken from frozen 3D U-Net and SegResNet encoders pretrained on the non-lung AbdomenAtlas dataset, with elastic-net logistic regression and 10-fold cross-validation evaluated by AUC. Expiratory features outperformed inspiratory-only models (AUC improvement 0.03-0.08), and the best result came from classical radiomics combining both phases (AUC 0.87, PPV 49%, NPV 95%), exceeding the handcrafted reference model (AUC 0.81, PPV 34%, NPV 94%), while frozen transfer features were slightly lower and combining feature types did not help. The work indicates that expiratory-phase imaging contributes substantially to lobe-level radiomic discrimination of spirometry-defined CAL, and that non-lung-pretrained frozen deep features did not add value over classical radiomics in this cohort.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via europepmc)

This study asks whether lobe-level radiomic features from paired inspiratory and expiratory chest CT improve discrimination of chronic airflow limitation (CAL, post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC < 0.70) beyond conventional handcrafted quantitative CT measures (lobar/total lung volume, volume change, and low-attenuation volume below -950 and -856 HU). In 2,579 SCAPIS-Stockholm participants (430 CAL, 2,149 sex-stratified non-CAL controls; median age 50-64 range cohort), five lobes were segmented with nnU-Net v2 (mean Dice 96%), and three feature families were compared under identical elastic-net logistic regression with 10-fold cross-validation: 13,160 PyRadiomics features (first-order, shape, texture with LoG and wavelet filters), frozen transfer features from SegResNet (608/lobe) and 3D U-Net (960/lobe) pretrained on abdominal CT (SuPreM/AbdomenAtlas), and 48 handcrafted features. The best model was combined inspiratory+expiratory classical radiomics (AUC-ROC 0.87, AUC-PR 0.63, Brier 0.092, PPV 49%), significantly better than frozen DL features (DeLong p<0.05); expiratory features consistently beat inspiratory (AUC +0.03 to +0.08), and feature fusion gave no AUC gain. Genuinely new: a head-to-head, lobe-resolved comparison of classical versus frozen non-lung-pretrained DL representations against a handcrafted reference in a large population cohort, with emphasis on expiratory-phase and lower-lobe texture signals. The negative DL result is largely a consequence of the deliberately weak transfer setup (abdominal pretraining, no fine-tuning), so it should not be read as evidence against DL radiomics.

Datasets

  • SCAPIS Stockholm cohort (Swedish CArdioPulmonary bioImage Study), paired inspiratory/expiratory chest CT + spirometry + questionnaires; n=2,579 analyzed (430 CAL, 2,149 non-CAL); private, license-restricted; single cohort used for 10-fold CV (no external test)
  • Lobe segmentation training set: 60 participants, manual 5-lobe annotation (MiaLab); 48 train / 11 eval / 1 excluded; private
  • AbdomenAtlas 1.1 (SuPreM): public abdominal CT used only for pretrained SegResNet and 3D U-Net encoder weights (frozen, no fine-tuning)

Method

  • Elastic-net logistic regression (GLMNET, alpha=0.5) on predefined feature classes; no outcome-informed preselection, correlation filtering, or PCA before CV; lambda via lambda_min
  • Classical radiomics: PyRadiomics 1.0.5, fixed bin width 25, 1 mm isotropic resampling, LoG sigma 1-5 mm and Coiflet wavelet sub-bands; 1,316 features/lobe, 13,160 total across 5 lobes x 2 phases
  • Frozen DL transfer features: SegResNet (608/lobe) and 3D U-Net (960/lobe) encoders from SuPreM, global average pooling of multi-level encoder maps, 1.5 mm resampling, HU clipping -1000 to 300
  • Handcrafted reference: 48 total/lobar features (inspiratory and expiratory volume, volume change, volume ratio, %LAV<-950 HU, %LAV<-856 HU)
  • Separate models for inspiratory-only, expiratory-only, and combined phases; UMAP/PCA used only for visualization

Validation

  • Internal only: 10-fold cross-validation with out-of-fold predictions; standardization and fitting within training folds; no external or prospective validation
  • Metrics: AUC-ROC with DeLong 95% CIs, AUC-PR, Brier score, calibration plots; DeLong test for correlated ROC curves for pairwise model comparisons
  • Threshold-dependent metrics (sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV) at Youden-optimal threshold selected and evaluated on the same out-of-fold predictions (acknowledged optimistic)

Results

  • Best model (classical radiomics, IN+EX): AUC-ROC 0.87, AUC-PR 0.63, Brier 0.092, PPV 49% vs 41% for expiratory-only (+8 points)
  • Expiratory-only outperformed inspiratory-only for classical radiomics, SegResNet, and 3D U-Net by AUC 0.03-0.08
  • Classical radiomics AUC significantly higher than frozen DL transfer models (DeLong p<0.05); SegResNet PPV rose from 35% to 41% with IN+EX; 3D U-Net similar for EX vs IN+EX
  • Feature fusion (radiomics+DL, radiomics+handcrafted, DL+handcrafted) gave no AUC improvement; PPV changed by at most +1 point (3D U-Net+handcrafted) and decreased for radiomics+SegResNet and radiomics+3D U-Net
  • Top-20 classical features were all texture-based (13 LoG, 6 wavelet, 1 original); 16/20 expiratory; lower lobes dominated (left 8, right 7); right middle lobe contributed 1 feature per model

Limitations

  • No external validation; performance is internally validated discrimination only, in a cohort with predominantly mild disease (148/179 COPD cases GOLD 1)
  • No radiomic feature robustness/reproducibility analysis across segmentation, resampling, discretization, or filtering; selected LoG/wavelet features treated as model components, not biomarkers
  • Youden threshold tuned and evaluated on the same out-of-fold predictions; PPV/NPV prevalence-dependent (CAL ~17%)
  • DL encoders pretrained on abdominal CT and frozen without lung-specific pretraining or fine-tuning, so the DL comparison is not representative of optimized deep radiomics; fixed FEV1/FVC<0.70 definition ignores age-related misclassification; no whole-lung vs lobar comparison or clinical-variable benchmark

Availability

Code/visualizations at https://github.com/emebac/Backlin_Radiomics_CAL_2026; SCAPIS data license-restricted and not publicly available; SuPreM weights public.
