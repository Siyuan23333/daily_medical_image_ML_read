A latent ODE approach to full-cycle biventricular cardiac motion modeling from cine MRI.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Medical Image Analysis · IF 11.8journal list2026-10-26D Brüggemann, E Krymova, F Ozdemir et al.

Conventional cardiac risk models rely on a few image-derived indices from selected cardiac phases, discarding the rich spatiotemporal information in cine CMR. The study used UK Biobank cine MRI from 72,386 participants without baseline cardiovascular disease (367 incident heart failure events), a single large multi-centre cohort. The method encodes bi-ventricular anatomy and full-cycle motion as a continuous latent trajectory using heart-rate-aware neural ODE dynamics and a graph-based mesh autoencoder, with a covariate-conditioned prior and a Cox model testing whether deviations from that prior predict incident heart failure. In a held-out subset, adding the latent score to refitted pooled cohort equations raised the stratified C-index from 0.704 to 0.785, versus 0.764 for seven established cardiac markers, and the model gave the best trade-off between reconstruction fidelity, generative realism, and prognostic performance compared with non-graph and non-ODE approaches. The work indicates that continuous full-cycle ventricular motion modeling yields prognostic phenotypes beyond conventional CMR summaries, though external validation in more representative cohorts is needed before clinical use.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via elsevier)

This paper addresses the clinical problem of early heart-failure (HF) risk stratification from cardiac MRI, arguing that guideline indices such as LVEF and strain discard most of the spatiotemporal information in cine CMR by collapsing anatomy into hand-crafted summaries and focusing on isolated phases (ED/ES). The authors propose a continuous-time generative model of full-cycle biventricular motion built on vertex-registered 3D+t meshes (5806 vertices, fixed topology). A hierarchical SpiralNet++ graph encoder aggregates frame tokens via a transformer and attentive pooling to infer a subject-specific end-diastolic latent state z(0); a latent neural ODE (3-layer MLP vector field, tanh) evolves z(t) through a learnable, heart-rate-conditioned monotone phase warp θ(t), and a graph decoder maps latent states back to meshes at arbitrary phases. The model is a conditional VAE whose Gaussian prior on z(0) is conditioned on age, sex and BSA, so that standardized posterior residuals define a normative-deviation embedding; a spline-expanded penalized Cox model on these residuals yields a scalar risk score r_lat. In 72,386 UK Biobank participants (367 incident HF), r_lat added to a refitted PCP-HF model raised the sex-stratified C-index from 0.704 to 0.785 versus 0.764 for conventional CMR indices (paired-bootstrap p=0.008). The genuinely new elements are the continuous latent-ODE formulation with HR-aware phase warping and the covariate-conditioned normative prior used to derive survival features; gains over the strongest published baseline (MeshHeart) in C-index were not significant, though reconstruction and distributional fidelity were.

Datasets

  • UK Biobank CMR (instance 2), cine SAX+LAX, 72,386 participants (367 incident HF), private/restricted access, random 1:1 development/evaluation split stratified by HF status; all fitting on development, all reported performance on held-out evaluation
  • Derived biventricular 3D+t meshes from the Dillon et al. (2025) pipeline (segmentation, breathhold correction, diffeomorphic fitting) plus added QC, Savitzky-Golay smoothing and watertight merging; 5806 vertices, Procrustes-aligned and standardized
  • Generative-realism evaluation set: 1000 synthetic samples conditioned on real evaluation-cohort covariates, compared against real evaluation meshes

Method

  • Conditional VAE over mesh sequences: hierarchical SpiralNet++ GNN encoder (64 filters) per frame + 2-layer transformer with attentive pooling; 3-layer SpiralNet++ GNN decoder; latent dimension D=64
  • Latent neural ODE (3-layer MLP, hidden 64, tanh) with circular phase embedding [sin θ, cos θ]; adaptive-step Dormand-Prince integrator; no explicit cycle-consistency term
  • Heart-rate-aware monotone phase warping θ(t) from a 2-layer MLP (hidden 16, softplus) with normalized integral, so θ(0)=0, θ(1)=2π; HR from DICOM
  • Covariate-conditioned Gaussian prior on z(0) via shared 1-hidden-layer MLP (width 64) with separate μ and σ heads over age, sex, BSA; β=5.0 KL weight; Adam, batch 8, lr 1e-3, weight decay 1e-6, early stopping
  • Downstream: standardized residual ẑ=(z̄(0)−μ(u))/σ(u), natural cubic splines (3 df per coordinate), penalized Cox (penalty 0.1 by 5-fold CV); baselines: linear vs graph decoders, ODE vs Fourier vs transformer temporal models, 4D CardioSynth, MeshHeart, refitted PCP-HF, conventional CMR-index Cox score

Validation

  • Internal only: single UK Biobank cohort split 1:1 into development and held-out evaluation; no external or prospective cohort
  • Reconstruction: mean vertex Euclidean distance, surface-normal angle error, vertex acceleration error; generative: Wasserstein distance over 7 CMR indices, MMD, temporal smoothness
  • Survival: sex-stratified Harrell C-index (pair-count pooled), 3-/5-year time-dependent AUROC, E/O calibration; paired bootstrap CIs and p-values; 5-seed representational similarity analysis (Spearman) for latent stability

Results

  • Incident HF C-index: PCP-HF 0.704 (refit) → 0.785 with r_lat vs 0.764 with conventional CMR score; r_lat vs r_conv difference 95% CI 0.0056–0.0371, p=0.008
  • Hazard ratio per SD of r_lat: 1.51 (95% CI 1.43–1.59, p=1.15e-51); 5-year E/O improved from 1.228 (PCP-HF) to 1.163 with r_lat, while 3-year E/O was closest to 1 for PCP-HF alone (0.977)
  • vs MeshHeart: C-index difference not significant (95% CI −0.0049 to 0.0227, p=0.210), but GNN+ODE significantly better in Euclidean reconstruction error (p=5.53e-5) and Wasserstein distance (p=4.00e-3)
  • Ablations: removing HR-aware phase warping lowers C-index by 0.009; restricting to ED+ES frames lowers it by 0.0140 (95% CI −0.0001 to 0.0273, p=0.054); removing covariate conditioning has little effect on C-index
  • Latent reproducibility across 5 seeds: mean pairwise Spearman r=0.977 with covariate-conditioned prior vs 0.922 without; prediction peaks at D=64, β=5.0

Limitations

  • Single-cohort internal validation only (UK Biobank, predominantly White European); no external or prospective test, and only 367 incident HF events, limiting power for baseline comparisons
  • C-index gain over MeshHeart is not statistically significant, and calibration is not uniformly improved (3-year E/O worsens with imaging scores)
  • No formal identifiability guarantee despite the iVAE-style conditioning; interpretability of individual latent coordinates is not established, and the Cox stage uses only the posterior mean
  • Authors do not share data; mesh pipeline depends on external segmentation/fitting tools and excludes cases with failed processing, which may bias the cohort

Availability

Code at https://github.com/brdav/cardiac_latent_ode; data not shareable (UK Biobank restricted access; authors state they lack permission to share data).

Joint segmentation of pancreatic tumors and peripancreatic vessels via a clinical risk-aware uncertainty alignment framework.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Medical Image Analysis · IF 11.8journal list2026-10-26W Fu, H Zhu, T Chen et al.

Preoperative assessment of pancreatic cancer resectability requires accurate joint segmentation of tumors and peripancreatic vessels from contrast-enhanced CT, but segmentation accuracy alone does not provide the task-specific characterization of peri-tumoral infiltration risk and topological discontinuity risk needed for decision support. The study uses contrast-enhanced CT from a multi-centre cohort of 1058 patients across five medical centres. The proposed clinical risk-aware uncertainty alignment framework builds on a dual-decoder architecture with evidential deep learning for joint segmentation and uncertainty estimation, adding an infiltration-aware boundary uncertainty alignment module that combines texture, spatial, and vascular proximity factors into an infiltration-inspired prior with ordinal structure to guide alignment, and a topology-aware discontinuity uncertainty alignment module that uses skeleton-mismatch regions as a connectivity proxy with a three-tier ordinal relation and region-weighted supervision. Multi-centre validation shows favourable and stable cross-institutional performance, and downstream experiments explore the utility of the aligned uncertainty for tumor-vessel invasion analysis; the abstract does not report specific quantitative metrics. What is new is the coupling of tumor and vessel delineation with clinically grounded risk characterization derived from aligned uncertainty, with potential to support tumor-vessel assessment in preoperative planning.

Deep dive (from abstract only)

This paper addresses preoperative resectability assessment in pancreatic cancer, where contrast-enhanced CT must delineate both the tumor and the peripancreatic vasculature to judge tumor-vessel invasion. The authors argue that segmentation accuracy alone is insufficient for decision support and that two task-specific risks must be characterized: peri-tumoral infiltration risk and topological discontinuity (vessel connectivity) risk. Their proposed clinical risk-aware uncertainty alignment (CRUA) framework uses a dual-decoder network trained with evidential deep learning to produce joint tumor/vessel segmentations together with voxel-wise uncertainty. Two alignment modules convert this uncertainty into ordinal risk indicators: an infiltration-aware boundary uncertainty alignment module builds an infiltration-inspired prior from texture, spatial, and vascular-proximity cues and uses its ordinal structure to supervise boundary uncertainty, from which infiltration-aware tumor boundaries are derived; a topology-aware discontinuity uncertainty alignment module uses skeleton-mismatch regions as a connectivity proxy and enforces a three-tier ordinal relation with region-weighted supervision to improve vessel continuity. The headline claim is multi-center validation on 1058 patients from five centers with stable cross-institutional performance, plus exploratory downstream experiments linking the aligned uncertainty to tumor-vessel invasion analysis. Genuinely new elements are the reframing of segmentation uncertainty as clinically ordinal risk (infiltration, discontinuity) and the skeleton-mismatch proxy for vessel connectivity; however, only the abstract is available, so quantitative accuracy, baseline comparisons, and the strength of the downstream invasion evidence cannot be verified.

AI-based diagnosis of frontotemporal dementia using neuroimaging: a systematic review and meta-analysis

Medical imaging MLscore 8review European Radiology · IF 4.7journal list2026-10-31Li, Yuqin, He, Xuebing, Li, Ni et al.

The problem is whether neuroimaging-based AI models can accurately distinguish frontotemporal dementia (FTD) and its clinical subtypes from cognitively normal controls in published diagnostic studies. The data come from 23 studies with 3670 subjects identified through a systematic search of PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library up to October 2025, covering sMRI, fMRI, PET, and multimodal imaging; the abstract does not specify whether the underlying cohorts were single- or multi-centre. The method was a systematic review and meta-analysis using a bivariate random-effects model to pool sensitivity, specificity, and AUC, with quality and certainty assessed via PROBAST+AI and GRADE. Pooled sensitivity was 0.91 (95% CI 0.87-0.93; I^2 = 73.2%), specificity 0.92 (95% CI 0.89-0.94; I^2 = 80.2%), and AUC 0.96 (95% CI 0.94-0.98), with the highest subtype performance for svPPA (AUC 0.98, 95% CI 0.97-0.99) and modality-specific AUCs of 0.94-0.98. The work matters because it aggregates the current evidence base and highlights that high reported accuracy is tempered by frequent high risk of bias, heterogeneity, and small subgroup samples, so the authors call for prospective multicentre validation before stand-alone clinical use.

This is a systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD420251270006) aggregating published diagnostic-accuracy studies of neuroimaging-based AI for frontotemporal dementia (FTD) and its clinical subtypes versus cognitively normal controls. The clinical question is whether AI models applied to structural MRI, fMRI, PET, or multimodal imaging can reliably separate FTD (and svPPA, nfvPPA, and behavioral-variant presentations) from CN, and how robust that performance is across modalities, subtypes, and time. Twenty-three studies with 3670 subjects were pooled using a bivariate random-effects model, yielding sensitivity 0.91 (95% CI 0.87-0.93), specificity 0.92 (0.89-0.94), and AUC 0.96 (0.94-0.98), with subtype-specific AUC peaking at 0.98 for svPPA and modality-specific AUCs of 0.94-0.98. Quality was appraised with PROBAST+AI and certainty with GRADE; heterogeneity was substantial (I^2 73.2% sensitivity, 80.2% specificity), and a temporal trend showed slightly declining reported AUCs from 2009 to 2025. What is genuinely new is limited: this is a synthesis of existing small, mostly single-center studies rather than a new model or dataset. Its value lies in quantifying pooled accuracy, stratifying by subtype and modality, and formally flagging high risk of bias and low certainty, which tempers enthusiasm for stand-alone deployment. For a radiomics/ML audience, the key takeaway is that reported performance is high but evidence quality is the bottleneck, not model capacity.

Multiparametric Quantitative MRI With 2.5D Deep Learning for Molecular Classification and Overall Survival Assessment in Adult-Type Diffuse Gliomas.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Journal of Magnetic Resonance Imaging · IF≈2.52026-10-31X Ge, Y Shen, Y Han et al.

Optimal analytic strategies for using quantitative MRI to noninvasively characterize gliomas and predict prognosis remain uncertain. The retrospective multicenter study used 3 T diffusion-weighted imaging, 3D pseudo-continuous arterial spin labeling, and synthetic MRI in 1408 adults with diffuse glioma, split into training (n=409), temporal validation (n=300), and three external test cohorts (n=119, 310, 270). Six map-specific models were stacked separately for VOI-mean, radiomics, 2D, 2.5D, and 3D deep learning, and a 2.5D-DL-derived IDH wild-type score was combined with age, MGMT promoter methylation, and extent of resection in a postoperative survival model. The 2.5D-DL stacking model achieved the highest external AUCs for IDH mutation (0.89-0.93) and 1p/19q codeletion (0.92-0.95), significantly outperforming the other four approaches, and the combined clinicopathologic model reached external C-indices of 0.750, 0.778, and 0.762 versus 0.704, 0.722, and 0.723 for the pathology-based model, with fixed-cutoff risk stratification significant across all external cohorts (hazard ratios 5.30-5.80). The work compares several quantitative-MRI analytic strategies head-to-head and shows that a 2.5D deep-learning IDH score can substitute for pathological IDH status in postoperative survival modeling.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from abstract only)

This retrospective multicenter study addresses whether quantitative MRI (qMRI) can noninvasively classify molecular subtypes of adult-type diffuse gliomas and add prognostic value beyond standard postoperative clinicopathologic models. The authors generated six parametric maps (ADC, CBF, T1, T2, proton density, contrast-enhanced T1) from DWI, 3D pseudo-continuous ASL, and synthetic MRI at 3T, delineated whole-tumor VOIs, and compared five analytic strategies—VOI-mean, radiomics, and 2D, 2.5D, and 3D deep learning—each stacked across the six maps. The 2.5D-DL stacking model gave the best external discrimination for IDH mutation (AUC 0.89–0.93) and 1p/19q codeletion (0.92–0.95), significantly outperforming the other four approaches. The derived 2.5D-IDHwt score was then embedded in a postoperative survival model (2.5D-qMR-Clin) with age, MGMT promoter methylation, and extent of resection, yielding external C-indices of 0.750–0.778 versus 0.704–0.723 for a model using pathological IDH status, with significant gains in two of three external cohorts and significant fixed-cutoff risk stratification (HR 5.30–5.80). Genuinely new elements are the head-to-head benchmarking of 2D/2.5D/3D DL on multiparametric qMRI and the demonstration that an imaging-derived IDHwt score can substitute for (and modestly improve on) pathological IDH status in survival modeling. The gains are incremental rather than transformative, and the abstract-only report limits assessment of methodology.

The osteoporosis-fracture paradox in older Taiwanese men: lower osteoporosis prevalence but comparable fragility fracture burden.

Bone and skeletalscore 8 Archives of Osteoporosis · IF≈3.02026-10-31YY Lee, YC Huang, SW Kao et al.

The study addresses whether the lower prevalence of osteoporosis in men than in age-matched women translates into a proportionally lower burden of fragility fractures, given that male fractures carry high morbidity and post-fracture mortality. It is a retrospective cohort using clinical DXA scans from 2021–2023 at a single centre, with 2031 eligible men compared 1:1 via nearest-neighbor matching to 2031 age-matched women; osteoporosis was defined as T-score ≤-2.5 at lumbar spine, femoral neck and/or total hip, and vertebral and hip fractures were identified from radiographs and electronic medical records, with multivariable logistic regression used to identify fracture predictors. Osteoporosis prevalence was about half in men (32.4% vs 51.4%, P<0.001), yet vertebral fracture prevalence was comparable (22.7% vs 22.8%, P=0.940) and hip fracture prevalence did not differ significantly (6.1% vs 5.0%, P=0.150); in sensitivity analyses men had more moderate-to-severe Genant grade ≥2 vertebral fractures (16.9% vs 12.3%, P<0.001), and male sex remained independently associated with vertebral and hip fractures after adjustment for age, BMI and osteoporosis. The findings indicate a dissociation between osteoporosis prevalence and fracture burden in older men, supporting fracture risk assessment that combines BMD with clinical risk factors rather than relying on T-score alone.

Deep dive (from full text via springer-oa-api)

This retrospective, single-center, age-matched cohort study addresses a clinically important question in male skeletal health: does the substantially lower DXA-defined osteoporosis prevalence in men translate into a proportionally lower fragility fracture burden? The authors analyzed 2,031 consecutive men aged 50–103 years who underwent routine clinical DXA (Lunar Prodigy; L1–L4, femoral neck, total hip) at a Taiwanese hospital (2021–2023), and matched them 1:1 by nearest-neighbor age to 2,031 women from the same institutional database. Prevalent vertebral fractures were graded on lateral thoracolumbar radiographs by two blinded orthopedic surgeons using Genant semiquantitative criteria (weighted kappa 0.80); hip fractures were ascertained from records and imaging. Osteoporosis (T-score <= -2.5 at any site) was far less common in men (32.4% vs 51.4%, P<0.001), yet vertebral (22.7% vs 22.8%, P=0.940) and hip (6.1% vs 5.0%, P=0.150) fracture prevalence were statistically indistinguishable. In multivariable models adjusted for age, BMI and osteoporosis, male sex remained independently associated with vertebral (OR 1.21) and hip (OR 1.58) fractures, and men had more Genant grade >=2 vertebral fractures (16.9% vs 12.3%). The genuinely new element is the clean same-scanner, same-system, age-matched demonstration of this dissociation, plus the finding that severe vertebral fractures are actually more frequent in men. However, the work is purely epidemiological: no imaging biomarkers, radiomics, or ML models are developed, and the cross-sectional design with indication/referral bias limits causal and predictive inference.

  • Institutional female DXA database (same hospital): DXA + radiography + EMR; 2,031 women selected by 1:1 nearest-neighbor age matching without replacement; private; comparator group only.
  • No external validation cohort; no public dataset used.

Method

  • Retrospective age-matched cross-sectional cohort; no machine learning or radiomics modeling.

  • Vertebral fractures graded by Genant semiquantitative method on lateral thoracolumbar radiographs by two blinded orthopedic surgeons with consensus; hip fractures defined as low-energy femoral neck/intertrochanteric fractures.

  • Sensitivity analysis restricted to Genant grade >=2 vertebral fractures; exploratory sex x osteoporosis interaction testing.

Results

  • Osteoporosis prevalence: 32.4% in men vs 51.4% in age-matched women (P<0.001); site-specific: spine 13.0% vs 31.6%, total hip 14.0% vs 23.3%, femoral neck 29.7% vs 43.2%.
  • Vertebral fracture prevalence: 22.7% (men) vs 22.8% (women), P=0.940; hip fracture 6.1% vs 5.0%, P=0.150.
  • Genant grade >=2 vertebral fractures: 16.9% in men vs 12.3% in women (P<0.001).
  • Adjusted ORs: male sex 1.21 (1.01–1.44) for vertebral fracture and 1.58 (1.19–2.11) for hip fracture; osteoporosis OR 2.51 (2.07–3.03) and 3.43 (2.39–4.92); age per year OR 1.12–1.13 (all P<0.001).
  • Age-stratified: osteoporosis in men 16.6% (50–59) rising to 65.2% (>=80) vs women 26.2% to 82.8%, while vertebral fracture prevalence was nearly identical at every stratum (e.g., 64.8% vs 65.2% at >=80).

Limitations
