Areal Bone Mineral Density Is Correlated with HR-pQCT Parameters 14 Years Later: Findings from the Osteoporotic Fractures in Men (MrOS) Study

Bone and skeletalscore 9late · published 2026-11-20 JBMR Plus · IF 3.0journal list2026-11-20Lauren Carlson, Eric Orwoll, Andy Burghardt et al.

The study asks whether baseline areal bone mineral density (aBMD) from DXA predicts later 3-D bone microstructure and estimated strength measured by HR-pQCT in older men. Data come from the Osteoporotic Fractures in Men (MrOS) cohort of 2424 community-dwelling men (mean baseline age 70.8 years), with hip aBMD measured in 2000-2002 and HR-pQCT at the distal radius and distal tibia roughly 14 years later in 2014-2016; central QCT vBMD measures were also available at baseline. Unadjusted Pearson correlations between total hip aBMD and later trabecular vBMD were 0.51 (distal radius) and 0.54 (distal tibia), with cortical vBMD correlations of 0.17 and 0.22, and failure load correlations of 0.52 and 0.60 (all p<0.01), and results were similar after adjustment for age, comorbidities, race, clinic site, quality of life, osteoporosis medications, and gait speed. Baseline central QCT cortical and trabecular BMD measures were likewise positively associated with HR-pQCT parameters 14 years later (p<0.01). The weak association with cortical vBMD is attributed to variable cortical bone loss at distal limb sites or trabecularization of cortical bone with age, indicating that DXA aBMD captures trabecular status better than later cortical microstructure.

Ordinal conformal partial-label prediction for medical image grading.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Medical Image Analysis · IF 11.8journal list2026-11-19L Ju, P Shi, Y Lin et al.

Medical image grading is often ambiguous between adjacent severity levels, and existing partial-label learning methods ignore grade order and return only a single prediction. The study evaluates 21 methods across nine medical grading datasets with three random seeds, including candidate labels from genuine expert disagreement and an eight-grade paediatric bone-age task; the abstract does not specify modality, cohort sizes, or whether the data are single- or multi-centre. The proposed OCP-PLL converts partial-label classifier outputs into contiguous ordinal prediction intervals using an ordinal-aware nonconformity score, with split-conformal finite-sample marginal coverage under exchangeability, and introduces Ord (a fixed Gaussian kernel encoding grade proximity) and UCOR (a learnable unimodal kernel adapting the ordinal prior during training). Across three PLL families, UCOR improved all five aggregated point-prediction metrics over corresponding baselines, with an overall macro-accuracy gain of 1.27 percentage points (p=0.0117), and OCP-PLL's aggregate empirical coverage was close to the nominal 90% target. The contribution is combining improved grading performance with contiguous severity intervals that carry coverage guarantees, rather than single-point predictions.

DOIPubMedSummary
Deep dive (from full text via elsevier)

The paper addresses two mismatches between clinical ordinal grading and standard deep-learning pipelines: (1) label ambiguity from inter-reader disagreement, which partial-label learning (PLL) handles via candidate sets but existing PLL methods ignore grade ordering; and (2) uncertainty communication, where point predictions hide uncertainty and standard conformal prediction sets can be ordinally disconnected (e.g., {0,4}). The authors propose OCP-PLL, combining ordinal-aware PLL training with split-conformal prediction. Two kernel extensions are introduced: Ord (fixed Gaussian proximity kernel over grades) and UCOR (learnable, symmetric, Toeplitz, row-unimodal kernel that generalizes the Gaussian), instantiated in POP, VALEN, and IDGP. At prediction time, an ordinal nonconformity score accumulates probability over nested neighbourhoods around the predicted grade, yielding contiguous intervals without post-hoc projection, with finite-sample marginal coverage under exchangeability (Theorem 1). Across nine public medical grading datasets (3-8 grades, including an eight-grade paediatric bone-age task and ACNE04-MR with genuine multi-expert candidate sets), 21 methods are compared. UCOR variants beat their baselines on all five point-prediction metrics, with a mean macro-accuracy gain of 1.27 pp (sign-flip test p=0.0117); fixed-kernel Ord gives the best mean macro-accuracy in the POP and IDGP families. Observed conformal coverage is close to the nominal 90%. Genuinely new: an ordinal nonconformity score guaranteeing contiguous intervals by construction, and a learnable unimodal ordinal kernel for PLL.

Datasets

  • Glaucoma fundus (ophthalmic, public, train/val/test)
  • PAPILA (fundus, glaucoma, public, train/val/test)
  • APTOS-2019 (fundus, diabetic retinopathy, public, train/val/test)
  • DRTiD (fundus, diabetic retinopathy, public, train/val/test)
  • MMAC (fundus, myopic maculopathy, public, train/val/test)
  • ACNE04-MR (clinical acne images, multi-expert candidate sets, public, train/val/test)
  • BreastIDC (histopathology, public, train/val/test)
  • COVIDGR (chest radiograph, public, train/val/test)
  • BoneAge-8 (paediatric hand radiograph, 8 ordinal groups from RSNA, public, train/val/test)

Method

  • Split-conformal wrapper (OCP-PLL) around any fixed PLL classifier; ordinal nonconformity score accumulating probability over nested ordinal neighbourhoods of the argmax grade
  • Ord: fixed Gaussian proximity kernel over grade distances; UCOR: learnable symmetric Toeplitz row-unimodal kernel parametrized by softplus increments, warm-started to match a Gaussian
  • Instantiated in POP, VALEN, and IDGP; POP-UCOR adds auxiliary kernel-consistency loss (lambda_aux=0.1) with separate kernel learning rate
  • Backbones: RetFound (fundus), PanDerm (acne), UNI (histopathology), RAD-DINO (chest/hand radiographs)
  • Baselines: 15 PLL/related methods plus six Ord/UCOR variants; model-based instance-dependent candidate sets for seven datasets

Validation

  • Internal train/validation/test splits across nine datasets, three random seeds per method-dataset pair; no external or prospective cohort reported
  • Metrics: macro-accuracy, ordinal and class-conditional errors, quadratic weighted kappa, conformal coverage and interval size
  • Two-sided exact sign-flip test on per-dataset macro-accuracy differences (p=0.0117)

Results

  • UCOR variants outperform corresponding baselines on all five point-prediction metrics; mean macro-accuracy gain 1.27 pp across the three PLL families
  • Fixed-kernel Ord achieves highest mean macro-accuracy within POP and IDGP families
  • OCP-PLL observed aggregate coverage close to nominal 90% target with contiguous intervals
  • Example: ordinal scores (1.00,0.98,0.90,0.55,0.90) with threshold 0.95 yield contiguous interval [2,4]

Limitations

  • Coverage guarantee is marginal and relies on exchangeability; class-conditional coverage not guaranteed
  • Calibration requires a separate fully labelled set, which may be scarce in grading settings
  • Candidate sets for seven datasets are synthetically generated from single labels rather than real multi-reader disagreement (only ACNE04-MR uses genuine disagreement)
  • No external or prospective validation; evaluation limited to public retrospective datasets

Availability

All datasets public; code at https://github.com/PyJulie/ocp-pll

Multicenter external validation of CT-based deep learning for proximal femoral fracture classification and quantitative fracture-line assessment.

Bone and skeletalscore 10late · published 2026-11-20 Skeletal Radiology · IF≈2.52026-11-20T Shimizu, K Kusunoki, Y Nishida et al.

The problem is accurate and objective CT-based classification of proximal femoral fractures and quantification of fracture-line angles, tasks relevant to preoperative assessment. Data came from coronal CT images across four institutions for training (259 patients, 19,844 annotated images) and internal validation (91 patients, 92 hips), plus external validation on 200 patients from independent institutions using scanners from two vendors, making it a multi-centre retrospective study. A ResNet-50-based convolutional neural network performed fracture region segmentation, fracture classification, pelvic fracture detection, and fracture-line angle estimation. Classification accuracy was 95.7% (κ = 0.944) internally and 91.5% (κ = 0.887) externally, with F1-score ranges of 0.927-0.982 and 0.667-0.952; fracture-line angle mean absolute errors were 4.54° and 6.26°, close to interobserver errors between orthopedic specialists (3.64° and 5.18°), with ICCs of 0.817 and 0.736. In an exploratory six-reader study, residents assisted by CAD showed higher sensitivity for non-displaced femoral neck fractures (0.78 vs. 0.47) with high specificity, indicating the system's potential to support diagnosis and preoperative assessment.

Prospective Validation of a Doppler-Enhanced Multimodal Ultrasound-Clinical Model for Predicting Clinical Pregnancy After IVF/ICSI Single Embryo Transfer.

Medical imaging MLscore 9 Journal of Imaging Informatics in Medicine · IF≈3.92026-11-20H Zhou, H Zhu, C Gao et al.

Predicting clinical pregnancy after transfer of a single morphologically high-quality embryo in IVF/ICSI is difficult, and the authors test whether Doppler-enhanced ultrasound combined with clinical data can improve individualized prediction. The single-centre study used 795 participants: a retrospective development cohort of 534 (427 training, 107 internal testing) and a prospectively collected temporal validation cohort of 261, with B-mode and Doppler videos acquired one day before transfer. Automated endometrial segmentation and feature extraction fed a four-branch gated-attention network that integrates imaging features, short-sequence descriptors, clinical variables, and embryo developmental stage. The locked model reached an AUC of 0.829 (95% CI 0.728-0.918) in internal testing and 0.771 (95% CI 0.661-0.874) in temporal validation, with accuracy 0.747, sensitivity 0.780, specificity 0.703, Brier score 0.187, and calibration slope 0.947. Ablation analyses showed numerically higher AUCs and accuracies for the complete model in both cohorts, with significant AUC differences in selected comparisons, and decision curve analysis suggested net benefit over part of the threshold range, supporting prospective temporal validation of a multimodal ultrasound-clinical approach.
